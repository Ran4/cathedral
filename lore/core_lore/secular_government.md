# The Common Bench of Ombreval

## Scope

Ombreval governs its streets, walls, markets and money through the **Common
Bench**. The Candor governs doctrine, clergy, consecrated property and offences
created by its own edicts. Neither body is the other one's department. Their
jurisdictions overlap at the Lanthorn, on feast days and whenever a case mixes
church and civic law, which produces negotiation rather than a simple chain of
command.

The eight planning wards are also the eight civic tax wards; Bell-and-Sluice is
one ward with two old names. Each ward holds an annual open **ward reckoning**
at which resident householders choose two **benchers** for one year. A resident
need not belong to a guild, and women and non-guild householders may be chosen.
The guilds exert influence through organised votes, credit and expertise, but
hold no guaranteed seats. Sixteen benchers make the Common Bench. Nine form a
quorum and nine agreeing votes make an ordinary rule.

Benchers are unpaid and may serve no more than three consecutive years. The
Bench chooses its paid working officers from any qualified citizen, not only
from its own members. No civic office is hereditary, purchased, or held for
life. Bribing a reckoning or selling an office is an offence even when the
payment is disguised as a guild gift.

## Rules, money and execution

The Common Bench makes market rules, street and building rules, secular
licences, tolls, civic penalties and emergency orders. Its presiding bencher is
the **First Seat**, chosen for one year. The First Seat arranges business and
speaks for decisions already made, but has no veto and is not a prince or
mayor.

The permanent **Bench House** staff execute those decisions:

- the **Common Clerk** keeps rules, contracts and minutes;
- the **Chest-keeper** receives revenue and pays warrants signed by two
  benchers from different wards;
- the **Line-keeper** surveys streets, walls, drains and disputed boundaries;
- eight part-time **ward hands**, one appointed after each reckoning, post
  notices, inspect nuisances and bring ward complaints to the Bench.

Toll and market collectors remain in the revenue service. Their receipts go to
the **Common Chest**. Expenditure is read aloud at every quarter reckoning and
proved against the Tallage weights at Gaudry's Audit. The Chest pays the watch,
wall repairs, wells, drains, gaol, fire equipment and hired militia service.

## Courts and custody

Three rotating benchers sit as the **Civic Measure Court** for debts, assault,
theft, licence breaches, toll fraud, property boundaries and breaches of Bench
rules. A paid **measure clerk** records the hearing. Serious violence, arson,
large fraud and appeals go before nine benchers as the **Full Measure**. A
bencher with a family, guild or creditor interest must stand aside.

The Candor's court hears clerical discipline, consecrated property and offences
under the Edict of F.244. A mixed matter is divided where possible. If it cannot
be divided, three civic benchers and three Chapter delegates sit together; a
decision needs four votes including at least one from each side. The
Praelucent does not command the Civic Measure Court, and the Common Bench does
not inspect the Grey Press.

The **Stone House** by the River Gate is the civic gaol. Its paid **Stone
keeper**, gaolers and guards answer to the Full Measure, not to a private
bailiff or the Candor. It holds accused people awaiting hearing, short-sentence
prisoners and debtors whom the court has specifically committed. Families may
bring food and bedding. Gaol fees are fixed publicly; inventing a fee is
extortion.

Paid **Bench sergeants** serve summonses, make arrests ordered by a court and
escort prisoners. Ordinary debt collectors have no arrest power. Gate and
night watchmen may stop an immediate breach of the peace but must bring a
prisoner and written cause to the Stone House by the next office bell.

## Walls and militia

Ombreval keeps a small paid watch and relies in danger on the **Wall Muster**.
Each tax ward maintains a roll of able residents, including craftspeople and
boat crews, who owe drills or may pay for an approved substitute. The Bench
appoints a paid **Wall captain** for a three-year term and may dismiss the
captain with twelve votes. Ward **file leaders** are temporary appointments for
one campaigning season. The Wall captain commands gates, armoury and mustered
companies during an alarm, but cannot levy tax, make law, imprison a civilian
after the alarm, or keep troops under arms once the Bench ends the muster.
Hired soldiers take the same oath and answer to the Wall captain while paid.

## Licensed intimate work, gaming and bathing

Selling sexual services is legal for adults in registered houses and as
independent licensed work. The Bench regulates location, coercion, debt terms,
noise and the employment of minors; it does not treat the livelihood itself as
a crime. Procurement by force, confinement, taking a child's earnings and
operating an unregistered house are offences. Licence debt and corrupt
inspection remain ordinary sources of exploitation.

Public gaming for small stakes is legal in licensed taverns and gaming rooms.
Marked pieces, rigged play, unlicensed large-stake rooms and lending against a
player's person are offences. Informal children's games and household wagers
need no licence.

Ombreval has wells, private tubs and a few rooms where an attendant heats water,
but no canonically established public bathhouse. Water carriers, well keepers,
boiler tenders and private bath attendants therefore exist without implying a
large civic or charitable bathing institution.
