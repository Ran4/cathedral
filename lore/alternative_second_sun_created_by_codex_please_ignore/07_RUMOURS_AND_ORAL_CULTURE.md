# Rumours, sayings, and oral culture of the divided light

This file is prompt fodder and performance material. Truth labels are for the
simulation or writer, never necessarily for the speaking NPC.

## Truth labels

- **S — Stone:** substantially observable fact.
- **G — Glass:** supported interpretation or incomplete fact.
- **F — Flame:** false, unverified, symbolic, or deliberate fabrication.
- **M — Mixed:** factual core altered in transmission.
- **L — Lie:** someone is knowingly spreading it for a current purpose.

## Citywide rumour pool

Each entry includes likely carriers and a mutation path.

1. **[S]** The lead in the rose's outer ring has been shaved away in several
   places. *Carriers:* glaziers, roofers, Custody guards. *Mutation:* missing lead
   becomes stolen glass, then the entire central medallion.
2. **[G]** Three independent calculators place conjunction in the coming season.
   *Carriers:* College students, printers, clergy. *Mutation:* same season becomes
   same feast, then an exact bell stroke.
3. **[M]** Wrong light passed through the bishop during last vespers and showed
   he was hollow. The light did cross the choir screen; a pillar shadow bisected
   his robe. *Carriers:* choir children, anti-clerical merchants.
4. **[F]** Every original pane contains the ashes of a person who died before
   sunrise. *Carriers:* relic sellers, pilgrims. *Mutation:* a person becomes a
   saint, then a murdered glazier.
5. **[S]** A new clear repair pane transmits no second light. *Carriers:* guild
   apprentices. *Mutation:* all new glass is blind, then all children born after
   the Silent Year are blind to the sun.
6. **[L]** Lucent Custody will arrest anyone found with blue glass after curfew.
   Spread by an amber-lens seller to move stock.
7. **[M]** A flour porter can walk streets that are not present. Avel knows absent
   routes but does not physically vanish. *Carriers:* porters, children, Keepers.
8. **[F]** Speak your own name backward beneath Saint Orra's Bridge and the water
   will show the day you die. *Carriers:* adolescent dare groups.
9. **[S]** Boatmen tap the arch rather than say “sun.” *Carriers:* everyone by
   the canal. *Mutation:* the tap is said to blind the thing below.
10. **[G]** The Office stores a reversed plan of the cathedral. *Carriers:*
    archivists, Unwallers. *Mutation:* reversed becomes upside down, then built
    around another sun.
11. **[L]** Master Tomas can sell an indulgence that guarantees two shadows.
    Spread to discredit him before a guild election.
12. **[M]** The painted window in Candlewick Court shone again. It produced an
    afterlight, though witnesses disagree about source. *Carriers:* plasterers,
    children.
13. **[F]** The foreign sky is the colour of human sight before birth. *Carriers:*
    midwives, poets, grieving parents.
14. **[S]** Counter-memory accounts share the obsolete name Bellfounders' Steps.
    *Carriers:* Keepers, physicians. *Mutation:* there is a bell foundry in the
    absent city making bells for conjunction.
15. **[G]** Sister Belis hears the lost second vespers in bell overtones.
    *Carriers:* choir, bellfounders. *Mutation:* she sings with the vanished dead.
16. **[F]** Swifts born in the western tower have violet yolks. *Carriers:*
    rooftop children, charm sellers.
17. **[M]** A person in prolonged wrong light tasted metal and remembered another
    spouse. Two separate witness accounts have fused into one scandal.
18. **[L]** The Concord poisoned a pilgrim lens. Spread by Odo's agents after a
    customer became ill from cheap pigment; a Concord member did smash the stall.
19. **[S]** Hessa Dault refuses all mirrors and coloured glass. *Carriers:* soap
    buyers, neighbours. *Mutation:* she casts no reflection.
20. **[F]** Gold blocks wrong light because the first sun bought it first.
    *Carriers:* goldsmiths, clergy, thieves who appreciate the excuse.
21. **[G]** Remote light-falls align with demolished west-quarter lanes.
    *Carriers:* surveyors, plasterers. *Mutation:* the absent city is rebuilding
    itself beam by beam.
22. **[F]** There is a market in the other city where one can sell a regret and
    buy the memory of having chosen differently. *Carriers:* tavern singers.
23. **[S]** Crowds gathered specifically for the phenomenon often see less than
    solitary workers. *Carriers:* cleaners, guides. *Mutation:* the Second Sun
    hates prayer, then loves thieves.
24. **[L]** A genuine rose splinter has entered the beggars' quarter. Spread by
    Custody to trace collectors, or by a collector to raise offers—versions vary.
25. **[M]** During plague, the light healed a ward. Some fevers broke; many died.
    *Carriers:* pilgrims, survivor families, preachers.
26. **[F]** A double-shadowed coin returns to its owner after being spent. A
    common line used when passing clipped coins.
27. **[G]** Open Sky radicals plan to remove lead during conjunction. *Carriers:*
    Fellowship, Custody. *Mutation:* lead becomes glass, glass becomes tower.
28. **[F]** Every locked door adds one spot to the hidden sun. *Carriers:* Iven's
    followers, burglars.
29. **[S]** A service stair is closed because voices echo at the wrong age.
    *Carriers:* cleaners, novices. *Mutation:* climbing it steals years.
30. **[G]** The window reacts to honest flaws more than perfect restoration.
    *Carriers:* glaziers. *Mutation:* broken things are holy, encouraging harmful
    anti-repair cults.
31. **[F]** The ordinary sun is the second one; the first was walled out.
    *Carriers:* philosophical Unwallers.
32. **[M]** An officer wears gloves because her hands have no shadows. Sabine
    actually uses gloves to prevent the rumour from being tested either way.
33. **[L]** Counter-memory invalidates a person's debts. Spread by debtors; courts
    have never accepted it.
34. **[S]** Records from the Great Revision removed obsolete street names.
    *Carriers:* archivists. *Mutation:* officials erased a complete second map.
35. **[F]** Cats lie across remote light-falls to keep doors between worlds shut.
    *Carriers:* householders; cats behave as cats do.
36. **[G]** A dry future-dated token came from the canal. *Carriers:* only Nera
    and Caldus at first; the form of the rumour can identify a leak.

## Profession-specific interpretations

When the same event spreads, let work shape metaphor.

- **Glazier:** “The colours stopped agreeing.”
- **Mason:** “The wall took a load from somewhere else.”
- **Carpenter:** “The world is racked out of square.”
- **Boatman:** “Another brightness is caught below the current.”
- **Clergyperson:** “A sign exceeded its safe interpretation.”
- **Merchant:** “Expectation outran supply.”
- **Physician:** “The memory coheres; the life around it does not.”
- **Child:** “The wrong shadow got there first.”
- **Custodian:** “Witness reports diverged after the third question.”

## Proverbs and everyday sayings

- **One wall for weather, two for secrets.** Privacy always creates concealment.
- **Keep one hand for this city.** Do not surrender entirely to wonder, grief,
  climbing, or ideology.
- **No colour alone.** Context determines character; borrowed from glaziers.
- **Admit the wound.** Honest flaws endure better than concealed ones.
- **The Guest chooses an empty chair.** Wonders come when not staged.
- **Two shadows, one pair of boots.** Contradictory beliefs do not relieve daily
  obligations.
- **Blue glass, blue tale, same buyer.** A story made to fit demand.
- **Eleven brass minutes.** Bureaucratic delay or time lost to futile precision.
- **Never sell the west before sunset.** Do not promise what you cannot control.
- **A smooth wall keeps a rough account.** Clean surfaces hide labour and history.
- **When the suns agree.** Never, or at the unlikely reconciliation.
- **Every bridge is a roof for someone.** Viewpoint determines whether a boundary
  shelters or blocks.
- **The first shadow is for law; the second is for conscience.** Public compliance
  can conceal private belief.
- **Wash it in running water.** Let go of an accusation or afterimage.
- **The glass remembers the furnace.** Origins remain present in finished work.

## Curses and insults

- **Half-shadow:** informer, coward, or Custodian.
- **Smooth wall:** hypocrite or person without visible commitments.
- **Token saint:** performatively devout pilgrim.
- **Window-eater:** person who consumes wonder without responsibility.
- **Blue-mouth:** compulsive spreader of Second Sun gossip.
- **Lead thief:** someone whose meddling endangers a structure they do not
  understand.
- “May your other self inherit your debts.”
- “Go queue west and wait for meaning.”
- “May every door become a doctrine.”
- “The Guest can have your second opinion.”

## Children's games and rhymes

### Two Shadows

One child is the Sun, one the Guest, and the others must freeze so their real
shadows overlap lines chalked in different directions. The Guest is allowed to
whisper new rules. Adults dislike the game because no version ends consistently.

Counting verse:

> First for bread and first for bed,  
> second walks where first has fled.  
> Red says stay and blue says through—  
> which of them remembers you?

### Three Wrong Turns

Children lead a blindfolded player through three turns, each accompanied by a
false street name. If the return echo sounds “one foot too many,” they claim an
absent alley has opened. The practical function is learning the dogleg streets
and escaping adults.

Route rhyme, also used by Caldus as a cipher key:

> Mortar, water, bell and bone;  
> mark the door that stands alone.  
> Ash above and blue below;  
> miss one name and west you go.

### Soot Window

On White Mortar Night children draw a window, name a place that should exist,
and dare one another to sleep with a foot inside its painted sill. Pip Ash claims
to have done this. Most children cheat.

## Prayers and liturgical fragments

### Prayer of sufficient light

> Giver of the day we have,  
> keep us from despising bread for wonder,  
> neighbour for distance,  
> and duty for the brightness of a sign.  
> Teach our eyes humility. Amen.

### Unwaller's threshold prayer

Spoken quietly before a passage meeting:

> We thank the wall that sheltered.  
> We name the wall that bound.  
> We ask no stone to vanish;  
> only that no wall call itself the sky.

### Glazier's furnace response

> **Master:** What does the glass owe the fire?  
> **Workers:** Its shape, not its obedience.  
> **Master:** What does the window owe the wall?  
> **Workers:** An edge honestly borne.  
> **Master:** What does light owe us?  
> **Workers:** Nothing.

### The two-vespers antiphon, official

> Evening entered twice, yet mercy was not divided.

The Fellowship replies in private:

> Evening entered once; the wall divided the witnesses.

## Tavern verses

### The Dousers' Measure

> They measured the warmth and measured the wall,  
> measured the pilgrim, purse and all;  
> when daylight doubled in spite of the rule,  
> they measured the length of the measuring fool.

### One Pair of Boots

> My other love waits in an absent lane,  
> with my other coat in the other rain;  
> but rent comes due where this roof stands,  
> so pour for these boots and these two hands.

## Rotating Fellowship passphrases

Passphrases should prove route knowledge, not operate as magic passwords. Pair
them with an observable detail that changes.

1. **Question:** “Where did evening lodge?”  
   **Response:** “Under the fourth bridge, where no west remains.”  
   **Proof:** identify the ceiling line where wrong light fell.

2. **Question:** “What did the plaster cover?”  
   **Response:** “A depth that never dried.”  
   **Proof:** bring blue lime dust from Candlewick Court, without damaging the
   painted window.

3. **Question:** “How many doors made the road?”  
   **Response:** “As many as opened for another.”  
   **Proof:** name the final host in the current hospitality chain.

4. **Question:** “Which sky bears the roof?”  
   **Response:** “The one beneath the roofer's hand.”  
   **Proof:** describe a specific lath mark visible only overhead.

5. **Question:** “Who owns the opening?”  
   **Response:** “Neither side alone.”  
   **Proof:** arrive with consent from both households sharing the passage.

## Rumour mutation examples

### A factual event across four hops

1. Witness: “A pale bar crossed the old bakery foundation after vespers.”
2. Porter: “The wrong light found Avel's absent bakery.”
3. Merchant: “The flour woman opened a shop in the other city.”
4. Pilgrim: “Buy bread from the dead under violet sun; it cures regret.”

The location and emotional theme survive while action and commercial claim grow.

### A planted lie that becomes partly true

1. Lens seller: “Custody will seize blue glass tonight.”
2. Customer hides a genuine old pane among cheap lenses.
3. Custody raids the stall for unrelated lead theft.
4. Market conclusion: the Office can sense true blue glass.

The emergent outcome validates the social behavior without proving magical
detection.

## Performance guidance

- Repeat compact sayings more than encyclopedic lore.
- Let a rhyme appear before its historical explanation; recognition is reward.
- Regional and occupational variants make culture feel lived rather than fixed.
- Use silence as an oral practice. Several groups consider refusal to name the
  sun meaningful, but for different reasons.
- TTS voices should whisper passphrases, shift to sales pitch for street cries,
  and leave generous pauses around counter-memory. The subject's acoustic form is
  part of its politics.
