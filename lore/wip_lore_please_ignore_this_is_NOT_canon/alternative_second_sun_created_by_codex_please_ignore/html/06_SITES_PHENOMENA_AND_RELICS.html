The Second Sun becomes playable lore when it is attached to places the player can revisit, routes they can learn, and objects people will fight over. The sites below are modular: exact placement may adapt to generated city geometry while their architectural relationships remain stable.

The great western rose

Appearance

The rose fills the west end above the gallery: concentric wheels of old glass, lead, and pale stone. Its iconography appears orderly at first and unsettles on inspection. Creation runs around the outer ring, but dawn and dusk occupy the wrong sides. Twelve human figures shade their faces from the blank central medallion. Several animals look beyond their own panels.

No glass matches the Second Sun's colour. The wrong hue emerges from the whole arrangement, most strongly where blue and nearly colourless panes overlap in the viewer's eye.

Spatial behavior

  • From the west door threshold, the second disc appears cut into four by tracery.
  • At the nave crossing, it looks whole but slightly too large.
  • From the north aisle, it leaves the glass and hangs one finger-width beside the window, an effect witnesses rarely admit because it contradicts “through.”
  • From the west gallery, the disc cannot be seen; only the foreign sky remains.
  • Reflected in a polished reliquary, it appears in the correct exterior position but casts no light.

Sensory signature

The wrong light has dry warmth like stone after sun, a smell of rain on cold ash, and an almost-heard interval a minor second above the nearest bell resonance. Dust rotates rather than rises. Spoken sibilants feel close to the left ear.

Human use

Pilgrims queue in marked cords. Custodians maintain witness stations. Pickpockets work the moment shadows split. Clergy offer a short prayer against “the pride of conclusions.” Glazier apprentices inspect lead from scaffolds and pretend not to look outward.

The west gallery: place where the image disappears

The gallery is the closest accessible point to the rose and paradoxically the worst for seeing the sun. From here, viewers see a pale alternate sky with slow cloudless gradients, sometimes crossed by dark shapes too large and slow for birds. Looking back into the nave, they see wrong light without its source.

Perrin Dault disappeared here in 83 AFG. His last chisel lies embedded in a joint that has been repointed many times without freeing it. The west-gallery parapet collects graffiti in layers; Custodians scrape words away but never the repeated symbol of two circles sharing an edge.

Gameplay qualities: vertical access, surveillance, dangerous proximity to the glass, a place for whispered confrontation while spectacle unfolds below.

The chamber of weights

Beneath the west tower, Lucent Custody's measuring room preserves instruments that failed instructively: bent plumb lines, smoked plates, Orra-style grids, glass thermometers, prisms, shadow pegs, sealed woad cloth, and the dismantled brass sighting frame.

Every instrument cabinet has two labels: an old description of what it measured and a later description of why that measurement was invalid. Scratches on the floor form a fan of recorded light angles. Extended backward, no rays converge on the rose. Extended forward, several point into the city.

Gameplay qualities: evidence comparison, stealth or sanctioned access, equipment borrowing, proof that institutional uncertainty has a material history.

Saint Orra's Bridge and the black-water oculus

A low canal bridge patched across many centuries. Beneath the central arch, a round foundation basin lies under dark water. At certain noons the Second Sun appears as a reflection even though the sky above shows one ordinary sun.

Boatmen follow three customs:

  1. tap the arch before naming any absent thing;
  2. let a passenger's sentence end before crossing from shade to light;
  3. never pole directly through a circular reflection.

The basin may be entered when the canal is briefly drained or by a dangerous dive. Its dry interior masonry bears outward-facing tool marks, as though built from inside solid ground. A drain too narrow for a person carries warm air west.

Gameplay qualities: moving viewpoint, sound boundary, underwater risk, future token mystery, conflict between canal labour and scholarly intrusion.

Candlewick Court and the last false window

A cramped court behind a covered passage, its walls layered with repairs. Under flaking plaster is one painted black window from the Night of White Mortar. It depicts no scene, only depth made from soot, bone white, and blue flecks.

When the real west is fully occluded by surrounding roofs, the painting may cast a narrow bar of wrong light. It illuminates objects but not faces. Children use the court for a door-game; Fellowship cells use it to validate route revisions; the plasterers' guild insists it is merely an embarrassing heritage surface.

If restored too perfectly, it stops working. If allowed to decay completely, the city loses a rare non-glass aperture.

Gameplay qualities: conservation dilemma, child lore, changing clue based on time and crowd, meeting threshold.

The fourth covered passage

The Fellowship's best-known secret meeting place is intentionally difficult to identify because “fourth” refers not to a fixed structure but to the fourth boundary crossed on a current walking chapter. In one revision it is a bridge house tunnel; in another a cloth market arcade; in another a gap between leaning upper floors roofed by a neighbour's washing.

A valid passage has three traits: no view of the west, ownership divided between at least two households, and a sound that arrives from above. Wrong light appears there as a line across the ceiling rather than floor.

Gameplay qualities: passphrase plus embodied route knowledge; procedural adaptability; meetings that cannot be unlocked by words alone.

The absent bridge in the forecourt

Seventeen witnesses at Two Vespers remembered a ceremonial bridge spanning the cathedral forecourt. Nothing stands there, but its route has accumulated signs:

  • rain dries in two parallel tracks above ground level;
  • birds occasionally land in midair from a distant viewpoint, actually perching on wires or ledges that align with the remembered parapet;
  • wrong-light shadows bend as if passing beneath an arch;
  • old processions divide around a central obstacle no longer named.

Several compatible plans survive. Walking the absent bridge requires navigating real stairs, balconies, market awnings, and overhead links around the forecourt. From the correct sequence of elevations, the city briefly composes the bridge.

Gameplay qualities: environmental route puzzle, parkour or flight temptation, shared counter-memory, an architecture made from viewpoint rather than collision.

The blue stair

A service stair behind the north aisle receives light through a narrow blue repair pane. During strong appearances, every other tread leads one step farther than its physical rise. Climbers arrive at the top with the bodily certainty of having climbed twice the height.

Names whispered on alternate treads return as echoes from different ages of the speaker. Custodians closed the stair after a novice heard herself answer as an old woman. Cleaners still use it because the sanctioned detour is inconvenient.

Gameplay qualities: acoustic clue, subtle spatial wrongness, NPC routine versus official closure, age or future-memory vignettes.

The kiln of admitted wounds

In the glaziers' lodge, the oldest furnace is relit only for rose repairs. A thin crack runs through its brick crown and must never be sealed. When molten glass is held beneath the crack during a manifestation, its surface reflects two bright points though the lodge has no view of the cathedral.

The ash beneath contains beads of unknown violet-grey material called debt glass. Ash Readers sift it secretly. Workers believe every bead recovered corresponds to a flaw appearing elsewhere in the city: a window crack, a broken promise, a forgotten street name.

Gameplay qualities: craft ritual, material sourcing, apprenticeship scene, choice between perfect product and honest flaw.

The Keepers' double map room

A rented upper room contains two maps. Black ink shows the chartered city. Blue thread, pins, scraps, recipes, rent notes, and witness tokens show absent streets. The blue map is not a clean alternate plan; it is a negotiated overlap of many memories. Threads thicken where testimony agrees and fray into personal worlds.

At certain angles the thread shadows create a third street plan no one remembers. The Keepers never mention this to newcomers. Residents of the inner circle call it “the city arriving.”

Gameplay qualities: evolving visual journal without conventional UI, testimony quests, ethical property disputes, prediction of remote light-falls.

The sealed pale-hands ward

A side chapel used as an exposure ward during plague, later sealed. Woad handprints cover the inner wall in pairs: one dark, one faded. Names were washed away, but some reappear in wrong light, including people who survived and people not found in any register.

Beds remain scratched with domestic counter-memories. One repeating sentence, in many hands, reads: “We were well on Bellfounders' Steps.” No present street has that name; the double map gives it three possible locations.

Gameplay qualities: plague grief, respectful investigation, names as collectible responsibilities rather than loot, convergence clue.

The one-shadow refuge

Hessa Dault's Concord maintains a plain hall with every coloured pane painted black, polished metal covered, and lamps placed so each body makes one strong shadow. No Second Sun discussion is allowed inside. The hall provides meals, sleeping pallets, burial assistance, and silence.

The setting should honour the relief of this place. Its safety makes later intolerance more complex. If wrong light finally enters, residents face not just a supernatural breach but loss of their only consent-based boundary.

Gameplay qualities: sanctuary with conversational rules, care work, political organizing, a no-investigation zone whose violation has lasting trust cost.

The five squares under wrong light

The city has five major squares; each relates differently to the phenomenon. Names can be adapted to existing nomenclature.

Glass Market

Licensed lenses, fake fragments, prints, double-wick candles, and viewing cords. During manifestations every seller claims their wares reacted first. Fun comes from distinguishing fraud, sincere error, and counterfeit objects that develop unexpected real effects.

Mortar Square

Plasterers display craft panels during First Glass. Soot windows appear here on White Mortar Night. The square's blank party wall acts as a civic canvas and a battleground over who may depict openings.

Scale Square

Merchants and civic magistrates insist that every wonder has measurable weights, fees, and liabilities. A permanent shadow grid from College experiments doubles as market-stall markings.

Pilgrims' Forecourt

Ceremonial approach to the cathedral and route of the absent bridge. Crowds make manifestations weaker but testimony more consequential. Public preaching, processions, protest, and spectacle collide here.

Quayside Exchange

Canal goods and boat traffic. Reflections can carry wrong light around corners. Boatmen undercut cathedral cosmology with depth metaphors and sell no lenses.

Taxonomy of manifestations

These categories help create variety without escalating immediately to cosmic spectacle.

Doublefall

Ordinary and wrong light create two shadows. Most common, socially readable, easy to fake badly. The separation angle and which shadow moves first matter to specialists.

Afterlight

A surface retains a pale imprint after illumination: woad, lime plaster, silvered parchment, bruised fruit, occasionally skin. Running water erases most afterlight.

Remote fall

A beam associated with the rose appears elsewhere, ignoring intervening walls. It often respects erased architecture rather than current geometry.

Counter-sky

Original glass reveals an exterior different from local weather or season. It may show stars, cloudless pallor, or slow dark shapes. No landscape has been seen.

Shadow refusal

An object blocks ordinary light but not wrong light, or vice versa. Gold, running water, new clear glass, and living leaves show inconsistent refusal patterns.

Recall

A witness gains counter-memory. Usually gradual and mundane; rarely a sudden complete alternate biography. Recall is an event in a person's life, not a clue container.

Concordance

Two or more unrelated systems align: bells resonate, absent maps match light angles, doors open in sequence, shadows vanish. Rare and politically explosive.

Invitation

The appearance seems to respond to the witness: light follows, an absent voice uses a name, a familiar silhouette stands in the foreign sky. Invitation should remain ambiguous and never become a routine quest marker.

Relics and contested objects

The seven strips of east-rose parchment

Silvered parchment developed in Sabine and Tomas's experiment, cut across case files. Each strip is innocuous alone. Together they depict reversed cathedral geometry and a star position not visible from the city's latitude.

Nera's future token

A dry brass viewing token recovered from canal water, bearing a plausible Office seal and a date forty years ahead. It was returned to the water. Counterfeiting, time anomaly, counter-memory made material, or Custody test are all viable.

Perrin Dault's fixed chisel

Embedded in west-gallery masonry. Tool records prove it belonged to the vanished mason. It grows warm before appearances and cannot be removed by force. Hessa's family claims it; the cathedral calls it part of the fabric.

The velvet of other stars

An ordinary-looking wrapping in Odo Vey's collection. In shadow, pale points change slowly. Spectrists can map them; glaziers say the cloth was dyed with ash from the original furnace.

The flawed Lamb

A replacement medallion that transmitted no wrong light until intentionally scratched. Its flaw is almost invisible. Continuists consider the damage sacred; Reframers use it to argue that imperfection is an active craft principle.

White Mortar soot

Powder scraped from a genuine false window. Mixed with lime, it sometimes makes a painted surface seem farther away. Most packets sold in markets are fireplace soot and crushed blue chalk.

The unstruck bell note

Not a portable object but a recorded interval Sister Belis hears beneath bells. Her transcription maps the rose. Completing it by ringing or voice may be a powerful social ritual; whether it objectively affects the phenomenon stays conditional on context.

Placement rule: evidence must have a life

Every anomaly should also support an ordinary function. The bridge carries boats, the kiln employs workers, the refuge feeds victims, the court belongs to children, the gallery requires cleaning. This prevents the city from becoming a sequence of supernatural museum rooms and ensures NPCs encounter mysteries while doing work.
