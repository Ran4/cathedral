This guide keeps generated lore and actor dialogue coherent without flattening every voice into the same vocabulary.

Preferred title

Capitalize the Second Sun when naming the phenomenon as a recognized concept. Use lowercase for a purely descriptive “second sun.” Factions often avoid the title for political reasons.

Faction names

  • Cathedral Chapter: the governing clergy, not synonymous with the whole Church or every priest.
  • Office of Lucent Custody: permanent cathedral bureaucracy; members are Custodians, Lectors, Weights, Veilers, and the Lucent Warden.
  • Guild of Saint Lume: glaziers and associated lead/furnace crafts.
  • Fellowship of the Unwalled Day: decentralized moderate Occlusionists; members can be called Unwallers, though some dislike the simplification.
  • Children of the Open Sky: radical splinter advocating physical opening; “Roofless” is both chosen title and insult.
  • College of Measures: loose empirical federation, not a modern university.
  • Keepers of the Absent Streets: mutual aid and counter-memory archive; Residents are a secret inner tendency.
  • Concord of One Shadow: movement for refusal and suppression; One-Shadows can be neutral or hostile depending speaker.

Dating and calendar

Use AFG (After First Glass) in scholarly or design-facing chronology. Citizens more often date by memorable events, feast days, guild tenure, floods, or family life.

Good dialogue:

  • “Two winters after the Silent Year.”
  • “The week before First Glass, when blue wool was dear.”
  • “Since old Perren kept the west furnace.”

Avoid having ordinary citizens say “in 348 AFG” unless they are archivists or giving formal testimony.

Material vocabulary

Favour physical craft words that fit the city:

  • ashlar, fieldstone, mortar, lime, plaster, lath, joist, sill, jamb, tracery, mullion, oculus, lead came, quarry, scaffold, putty;
  • culvert, lock, quay, towpath, pole stroke, basin, arch, current, silt;
  • woad, madder, soot, beech ash, cullet, cobalt, horn, silvered parchment;
  • nave, aisle, crossing, transept, quire, west gallery, undercroft, squint, reliquary, antiphon, vespers.

Use the precise term only where a speaker would know it. Pip says “stone flower,” Tomas says “outer wheel tracery,” Odo says “the famous rose.”

Tone

Aim for

  • numinous but material;
  • medieval civic complexity without parody ye-olde grammar;
  • ordinary humour beside metaphysical dread;
  • evidence expressed through labour and place;
  • institutions made of mixed motives;
  • grief handled with specificity;
  • compressed sayings actors can remember and mutate.

Avoid

  • modern multiverse, simulation, quantum, radiation, hallucination, or dimension language in ordinary dialogue;
  • generic “light versus darkness” morality;
  • constant purple, eldritch, madness, cult, and corruption vocabulary;
  • pseudo-biblical thee/thou as default speech;
  • treating all clergy as cynical, all heretics as liberated, or all merchants as unbelieving;
  • cosmic exposition without a concrete sensory anchor;
  • “chosen one” language for the player;
  • resolving ultimate cause through an ancient text that simply explains it.

Scholars can approach concepts analogous to optics or cognitive science using period-observable language: ordered colours, refraction, concordant testimony, attention, memory, bodily humour, interval, instrument error.

Naming conventions

Personal names should be concise and pronounceable, with variation across class:

  • Given: Avel, Belis, Caldus, Edrin, Enna, Gerren, Hessa, Iven, Jon, Leth, Mara, Nera, Odren, Orra, Perrin, Pip, Sabine, Tomas, Ysabet.
  • Family: Arquen, Ash, Dault, Fen, Noll, Pell, Quill, Rusk, Salvi, Vale, Venn, Vey, Voss.
  • Occupational/byname: of the Lath, Roofless, Blue-Hand, Underbridge, Ash-Eye, Half-Bell, Cord-Keeper.

Names may echo historical figures without implying direct descent. Avoid a dense fantasy apostrophe style.

Place names often derive from work, material, saint, lost structure, or sensory fact:

  • Candlewick Court, Dye Lane, Soapmakers' Row, Bellfounders' Steps;
  • Saint Orra's Bridge, the Blue Stair, chamber of weights;
  • Glass Market, Mortar Square, Quayside Exchange.

Absent names should be plausible, not surreal. Their uncanniness comes from specific nonexistence.

Sentence and dialogue texture

Workers

Use active verbs, load, failure, sequence, bodily knowledge.

“That wall is carrying weight it was never bonded for.”

Clergy

Use distinctions, obligation, parable, liturgical echo; let personality decide warmth or bureaucracy.

“A sign may enlarge duty. It does not replace it.”

Custodians

Separate perception from inference, ask source and audience, use risk-aware euphemism.

“Tell me where the brightness ended. Save what you think it wanted for later.”

Unwallers

Use threshold and ownership metaphors, but tether them to real construction.

“Somebody made that boundary, paid for it, and made someone else sleep beneath it. Start there.”

Keepers and counter-rememberers

Use domestic alternate detail, avoid dreamlike babble.

“Bellfounders' Steps smelled of wet rye. It was not beautiful. It was home.”

Merchants

Use invitation, conditions, price, plausible deniability.

“I sell the favourable angle. Heaven remains responsible for delivery.”

Children

Use rule inversion, mimicry, route knowledge, abrupt seriousness.

“Paid wrong is shiny. Real wrong makes the next footstep late.”

Recurring images

Use sparingly enough that recognition matters:

  • two shadows sharing one pair of boots;
  • a wall calling itself the sky;
  • a flaw admitted rather than hidden;
  • doors held open in a chain;
  • running water washing an afterimage;
  • one hand kept for the present city;
  • a second vespers heard but not sung;
  • a bridge remembered by people who never crossed it;
  • violet-white light that no pane contains;
  • the ordinary task continuing beneath wonder.

Protected ambiguity language

Use assertions for local facts:

  • “The lead curl matches the outer rose alloy.”
  • “Three witnesses independently named Bellfounders' Steps.”

Use attributed or conditional language for cosmic interpretation:

  • “Mara believes the route reveals a hidden sky.”
  • “If the disc is another sun, the tracery may be a seal rather than a cage.”

Avoid evasive overuse of “perhaps” at the sentence level. Be precise about what is known, then precise about why meaning remains disputed.

Micro-prompts for generated content

Rumour

Retell one event from this actor's occupation and emotional stake. Preserve one physical detail, mutate one social detail, name or conceal the source according to risk, and do not confirm protected cosmology.

Witness account

State place, light behavior, bodily sensation, who else was present, and one inference the witness knows is an inference. Include one ordinary concern that interrupted or followed the event.

Diegetic notice

Write a rule produced by a past harm. Let official wording hide the specific incident while graffiti or common speech remembers it.

Counter-memory

Describe an absent place through rent, food, weather, work, repair, and a relationship. The speaker knows it is not physically present and resents being treated as a prophet.

Quest seed

Interrupt ordinary work with one locally testable anomaly. Put a person at risk of becoming evidence. Give two custodians legitimate conflicting values, one architectural verb, one speech verb, a local answer, and a changed revisit.

Final consistency checks

  • Is the Second Sun visible as a disc only through original rose glass, unless the scene is clearly a disputed exception?
  • Does consequential wrong light remain possible outside that view?
  • Has a character been given knowledge beyond occupation, access, or source?
  • Does counter-memory remain coherent and humane?
  • Is a faction's internal disagreement visible?
  • Can the local mystery be solved without settling the cosmos?
  • Does the scene include something other than the mystery that a person needs?
  • Would the dialogue still sound like this actor if faction labels were removed?
