The first sun makes the world visible. The second makes visibility suspect. — scratched beneath the west-gallery parapet, author unknown

The image at the heart of it

At certain hours, someone standing in the cathedral nave and looking west through the great rose can see two suns.

The familiar sun is the colour of warm metal. It behaves as a sun should: it moves with the day, is softened by cloud, and exists when one steps outside. The other is smaller, lower, and the colour of a bruise seen through milk — not violet exactly, not silver, and not a colour glaziers can match. It hangs in a pale, depthless sky visible only inside the circles of the western rose. It does not illuminate the clouds around the city because, as far as anyone outside can tell, it is not there.

Yet its light enters.

It lies across pillars at angles the exterior sky cannot supply. It gives every lit body a second penumbra. Dust turns slowly in it even when the nave air is still. Red glass cools it; blue glass seems to make it brighter; clear repair panes transmit nothing but a faint trembling edge. On rare days the wrong light passes beyond the nave, crossing streets and walls to appear in a distant court or under a covered passage. People can stand in that light, warm one side of a hand, and disagree about the sensation five breaths later.

The city calls this phenomenon the Second Sun when it dares to call it anything at all.

Stone: facts the setting treats as stable

These are not necessarily known by every citizen. They are the foundation on which contradictory beliefs can safely stand.

1. It is mediated by the rose

The complete disc is visible only through the original coloured glass of the great western rose. It cannot be seen through an open door, ordinary window, telescope, mirror held outside, polished helmet, canal reflection, or a hole cut in the roof.

Detached fragments from the rose sometimes retain lesser effects. They may show a bright edge where no light source exists, cast doubled highlights, or reveal stars at noon. Counterfeit fragments do not, although belief in them can still cause very real violence.

2. It makes consequential light

The phenomenon is not only an image. Its illumination can be blocked, measured, photographed or traced, used to expose treated parchment, and felt as a slight dry warmth. A body standing in it casts a second shadow. On cloth dyed with woad, the shadow sometimes remains as a pale mark until washed in running water.

The light has never reliably kindled tinder. It has never nourished a plant in a controlled trial. Bread left in it stales slowly but develops a bitter taste. These failures matter: the Second Sun resembles daylight without fulfilling all of daylight's promises.

3. It follows attention as well as astronomy

The ordinary sun follows the hour. The Second Sun has a looser rhythm. Its most predictable appearances occur between late afternoon and vespers, especially in the forty days around the Feast of First Glass, but anticipation affects it. Crowds gathered solely to witness it often see less. A solitary cleaner, thief, or grieving pilgrim may see it blaze at an impossible hour.

Instruments record portions of the effect even when no witness reports seeing the disc. Witnesses sometimes report the disc when instruments record only ordinary light. Neither proves it imaginary; both make it difficult to own.

4. The contradiction is old, but not as old as the cathedral

Construction records from the first glazing do not mention a second sun. The earliest unambiguous account dates to the Year of Two Vespers, eighty-three years after the rose was completed, when the nave filled with cold lilac light during an eclipse. Earlier texts contain suggestive phrases — “the guest behind the day,” “our western witness,” “the light kept outside” — but may have been reinterpreted after the event.

5. Sustained exposure changes memory before it changes flesh

People who spend many consecutive appearances in the wrong light begin to remember spatial alternatives: a stair where a wall stands, a chapel with one more bay, the canal running on the other side of a square. These memories are internally coherent and sometimes shared by strangers. The affected are not generally confused about their present surroundings. They remember both cities and know one is absent.

The old term is counter-memory. The vulgar term is glass-sickness. Neither term should be treated as a simple medical diagnosis.

6. Authority has never succeeded in suppressing it

The Church has covered the rose, replaced panels, posted guards, forbidden measurements, commissioned measurements, declared a miracle, declared an optical impurity, and declared discussion spiritually unprofitable. Civic authorities have taxed pilgrims, expelled pilgrims, and sold licensed viewing tokens. The Second Sun has outlived every policy.

7. It is not morally aligned

The light does not reliably reward faith, punish blasphemy, expose lies, heal the innocent, or corrupt the wicked. People tell stories in which it does all of these things. The stable pattern is not moral judgment but revelation of contradiction: double shadows, incompatible recollections, hidden writing, unspoken loyalties, and institutions forced to explain what they cannot own.

Glass: the strongest interpretations

Each theory explains important evidence. Each demands that its followers ignore something inconvenient.

The doctrine of Reserved Creation

Official cathedral teaching, in its careful modern form, says that creation was not exhausted in the making of the visible world. The Second Sun is a reserved light: an image of divine possibility shown through a consecrated work, not a second heavenly body. It is real as grace is real, but asking where it is in the sky is a category error.

This accounts for why liturgy, attention, and consecrated glass matter. It does not comfortably explain measurable heat, remote light-falls, or counter-memory. Senior clergy respond that grace has always had material consequences.

The Occlusionist claim

The forbidden fraternity called the Unwalled Day, and its more radical children, argue that there truly are two suns. The cathedral does not create or symbolize the second; it is a wound through an enormous deception. The visible sky is a constructed vault, veil, or prison skin. Medieval humanity once saw both lights, and some power — divine, angelic, royal, or architectural — walled one out.

The rose is therefore not a window in the cathedral but a window through the world. Its tracery is a lock. Its glass is a remembered sky.

This explains the foreign background, the importance of original panes, and the heretics' obsession with removing architectural boundaries. It struggles to explain why breaking the rose has historically weakened rather than widened the phenomenon.

The glaziers' instrument theory

A minority within the Guild of Saint Lume holds that the original masters made an optical instrument without understanding it: multiple glass thicknesses, metal oxides, lead harmonics, curvature, and the geometry of the western towers combine to reveal a natural radiation or distant celestial body invisible to the naked eye.

This is the safest skeptical position because it admits the evidence without admitting theology. It explains why repairs matter, predicts measurable effects, and encourages experimentation. It does not explain counter-memories shared by people who never met, nor why faithful reconstructions fail.

The civic wound theory

Among archivists and canal engineers, one finds a quieter idea: the Second Sun is not above but beneath. The cathedral stands on a buried crossing where an older street plan, an older river, or another version of the city was sealed. The rose only gives that buried world a shape the mind recognizes. Remote light-falls follow forgotten foundations. Counter-memory is historical memory leaking upward.

This theory treats light as the city's way of remembering itself. It explains why impossible beams often land on old structures and why affected people recall consistent alleys. It cannot explain the clearly visible disc unless the disc is a symbol imposed by the witness.

The mercy hypothesis

Some anchoresses, bereaved parents, and plague survivors believe the light shows the world in which a refused event was allowed: the child lived, the bridge did not fall, the gate opened before the fire. The sun is “second” because it shines on second chances.

Counter-memories support this with terrible intimacy. So do accounts of hearing dead voices in the west gallery. But the alternate city is not consistently better. In some memories, familiar people were never born.

The predatory-light hypothesis

Inquisitors of the old school and frightened physicians say the phenomenon is an intelligence that requires observers. It offers meaningful contradictions so people will look longer, argue harder, preserve its glass, and carry fragments through the city. Counter-memory is not revelation but feeding damage.

This theory can interpret any curiosity as evidence, making it unfalsifiable. That has not stopped it from motivating purges.

Flame: claims that should remain volatile

  • The Second Sun has a dark spot shaped like the cathedral's floor plan.
  • A person conceived in its light casts only one shadow.
  • The bishop keeps a rose fragment beneath his tongue during major sermons.
  • There is a second cathedral around the Second Sun, and something there looks back through its own rose window at this city.
  • At the bottom of the canal, the wrong sun is visible without glass.
  • The sun moves closer whenever someone speaks the true name of a street erased in the Great Revision.
  • Birds cannot see it, except swifts born in the western tower.
  • The first master glazier was executed before he could explain what he built.
  • The light does not pass through gold because gold remembers the first sun.
  • On the day both suns touch, every oath sworn beneath the rose will become literally true.

These claims are story fuel, not facts to be globally resolved.

The seven protected mysteries

The following questions should accrue evidence but resist definitive answers.

  1. Where is it? A distant body, another sky, a buried city, divine reserve, mental artifact, or an intelligence without location?
  2. Why the rose? Is the window a lens, key, seal, icon, memory, or bait?
  3. Why did it begin? Was the Year of Two Vespers a first appearance, a first mass witness, or the moment an older censorship failed?
  4. Does it choose witnesses? Apparent preference may be consciousness, social selection, architectural chance, or retrospective myth.
  5. Are counter-memories true? They might be other histories, suggestions, ancestral memory, or patterns imposed on city geometry.
  6. Can it be freed or extinguished? Every faction has an answer; no faction can demonstrate consequences safely.
  7. What happens at conjunction? Calculations predict a day when the two discs overlap. The calculations disagree on both date and meaning.

What the Second Sun is about

The phenomenon is most powerful when it operates on several levels at once:

  • A visual impossibility: players understand the premise in one glance.
  • An epistemic problem: seeing is evidence, but evidence does not dictate an interpretation.
  • A social pressure: what a person is willing to say about it reveals their loyalties, griefs, class, profession, and appetite for danger.
  • An architectural mystery: glass, walls, angles, routes, and thresholds are the means of inquiry.
  • A temptation: every answer offers meaning or power at a cost.
  • A mirror of play: the player, like the city's citizens, sees an engineered rendering phenomenon and chooses whether to inhabit the fiction of it.

Tone guardrails

  • Keep the effect beautiful before making it frightening.
  • Let ordinary people have practical relationships to it: sweeping marked dust, selling smoked lenses, scheduling trysts, hiding contraband in doubled shadow.
  • Avoid reducing the Church to “knows truth, hides truth.” Different clergy sincerely hold incompatible positions, and official doctrine changes because institutions are made of frightened, thoughtful, ambitious people.
  • Avoid making heretics automatically correct. Their courage and persecution are real; so are their schisms, cruelties, frauds, and leaps of faith.
  • Prefer evidence with two readings over collectible exposition that closes a question.
  • Preserve human stakes. A widow's counter-memory of a living daughter matters more than another paragraph of cosmology.
