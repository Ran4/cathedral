These actors are seeds, not fixed quest dispensers. Each includes public role, voice, belief, secret, relationships, wants, and possible evolution. Names and minor details may be remixed, but preserving the relationship web makes the city feel socially continuous.

Relationship web at a glance

  • Lucent Warden Sabine Vale protects Master Glazier Tomas Rusk from an old scandal; Tomas believes Sabine destroyed the evidence.
  • Passage keeper Mara-of-the-Lath is Tomas's estranged daughter and Custody Veiler Jon Rusk's sister.
  • Physician Edrin Pell treats counter-rememberer Avel Noll, whose absent map threatens Provost Gerren Salvi's inheritance.
  • Boatwoman Nera Quill carried Custody archivist Brother Caldus during the canal manifestation; each believes the other kept the crucial object.
  • Pilgrim broker Odo Vey finances both preacher Canon Leth and radical Iven Roofless, making public order and public disorder profitable.
  • Child guide Pip Ash works for Odo, runs messages for Mara, and is the only person the Concord organiser Hessa Dault will not intimidate.

1. Sabine Vale, Lucent Warden

Public face: Severe head of the Office of Lucent Custody, fifty-eight, always wearing grey gloves so witnesses cannot inspect her shadows.

Voice: Quiet, exact, never hurries a sentence. Answers sensational claims by asking for angles, distances, and names. Calls the phenomenon “the western appearance” in public and “it” in private.

Belief: The Second Sun is materially real, conscious in no human sense, and dangerous mainly through institutions competing to possess it.

Secret: During a youthful experiment, Sabine and Tomas Rusk exposed a sheet of silvered parchment. It developed a map of a cathedral with its rose at the east end. Sabine did not destroy the image; she divided it into seven strips and hid each within an unrelated case file so no single thief could own it.

Wants now: Recover stolen lead from the outer ring before the chapter learns the theft exposes a decades-old inventory fraud.

Relationships: Protects Tomas out of guilt. Respects Mara Venn's original ethics but considers the modern Fellowship compromised. Secretly pays Edrin Pell to care for witnesses Custody has officially dismissed.

Disclosure gate: The player must report an observation accurately even when lying would win more favour.

Possible change: She may decentralize the archive, lock it forever, or become the very monopolist she believes herself to be preventing.

2. Tomas Rusk, master glazier of Saint Lume

Public face: Broad-handed master responsible for the rose's conservation. Charismatic at the furnace, evasive everywhere else.

Voice: Explains ideas through heat, viscosity, colour, and breakage. Says “show me the edge” when unconvinced. Laughs loudly before telling a lie.

Belief: The rose is an instrument assembled across generations, including repairs; originality is less important than a living chain of admitted wounds.

Secret: He replaced a supposedly original sliver twenty-six years ago after breaking it while drunk. The replacement now transmits wrong light more strongly than adjacent ancient glass. This could ruin him and the Continuist doctrine—or prove that craft, not relic age, sustains the window.

Wants now: Reframe one outer panel during the coming conjunction.

Relationships: Father of Mara and Jon; neither speaks to the other in his presence. Owes Sabine his office and resents the debt. Suspects Odo Vey owns the broken original sliver.

Disclosure gate: Assist in a mundane, difficult repair without asking about the Second Sun until the work is complete.

Possible change: Confession could renew his guild, destroy public trust, or be weaponized by people interested only in controlling access.

3. Mara “of the Lath” Rusk, keeper of the fourth route

Public face: Plasterer and repair contractor who knows every party wall west of the cathedral. Named for the wooden laths she carries over one shoulder.

Voice: Dry, impatient, speaks of theology as construction. “A boundary is a job someone was paid to do.” Gives directions by textures and overhead shapes, not street names.

Belief: The hidden daylight is real, but the Fellowship's task is to protect openings and witnesses, not force revelation.

Secret: Her route's newest opening is not a crack or window. It is a certain sequence of people opening doors for one another across the city. When performed before vespers, wrong light briefly connects all thresholds. She fears recording this will turn hospitality into ritual machinery.

Wants now: Identify which cell member gave a route phrase to the Open Sky radicals without triggering Custody raids.

Relationships: Estranged from Tomas over his secrecy; loves her brother Jon and avoids making him choose between family and office. Employs Pip Ash as a lookout but refuses to recruit the child doctrinally.

Disclosure gate: Player must name a wall that protected them and a wall that unjustly constrained someone else.

Possible change: Can become a public reformer, a hunted route-keeper, or an inflexible guardian who mistakes secrecy for safety.

4. Jon Rusk, Custody Veiler

Public face: A friendly crowd officer who checks tokens and jokes with pilgrims. Looks too ordinary for the fear attached to his uniform.

Voice: Easy humour, short questions, repeats a speaker's last phrase when testing inconsistency. Uses Fellowship slang accidentally when tired.

Belief: Both the Office and Fellowship are necessary, and each lies most dangerously when claiming sole responsibility.

Secret: Jon diverts low-risk witness reports to Mara before they reach the archive. One report he diverted described planned lead theft; people may die because he protected the source.

Wants now: Find the stolen lead himself and make it appear anonymously in the guild stores.

Relationships: Brother to Mara, son to Tomas, junior officer under Sabine. Dating Hessa Dault's nephew in secret, which complicates every Concord search.

Disclosure gate: Show concern for a person rather than evidence.

Possible change: His double loyalty may become an honest bridge or collapse into informing on everyone.

5. Avel Noll, keeper of an absent bakery

Public face: Elderly flour porter who carries a key to a bakery demolished before she was born. Calm, competent, not interested in being called visionary.

Voice: Rich in domestic specifics: oven heat, rent days, neighbour quarrels. Corrects people who romanticize her alternate memories. “It was no paradise; the roof leaked into the rye.”

Belief: She remembers another city because she lived there. She does not know whether that life was before, elsewhere, or might yet be.

Secret: In the absent bakery cellar, she remembers hiding a murder weapon with Provost Salvi's alternate mother. In the present city, a sealed cellar lies on that spot beneath property owned by the Salvi family.

Wants now: Open the cellar, not to prove herself, but to recover a child's wooden horse she remembers leaving beside the weapon.

Relationships: Patient and friend of Edrin Pell. Protected by the Keepers of Absent Streets. Gerren Salvi has offered her money to leave the city.

Disclosure gate: Spend time helping with flour deliveries and never call her memory “the better city.”

Possible change: Physical corroboration could vindicate, implicate, or further isolate her; lack of corroboration should not erase her human story.

6. Edrin Pell, physician of divided recollection

Public face: Middle-aged physician known for treating glass-sickness without bleeding, exorcism, or public spectacle.

Voice: Patient questions, precise distinctions. Refuses “mad” and “chosen” with equal firmness. Becomes cutting when a witness is treated as evidence.

Belief: Counter-memory is a real class of experience with social, physical, and perhaps optical causes. Its metaphysical status is not required for humane care.

Secret: Edrin has begun remembering patients he never treated, all with the same pale burn beneath the tongue. One absent patient gave a date for the conjunction. Edrin has told no one because self-diagnosis would destroy trust.

Wants now: Establish a refuge outside Church, Custody, and Keeper control.

Relationships: Treats Avel; paid secretly by Sabine; former spouse of Canon Leth, with genuine affection surviving ideological conflict.

Disclosure gate: Respect a patient's refusal to answer.

Possible change: Can model ethical uncertainty or sacrifice patients' trust to warn the city about the date.

7. Nera Quill, canal polewoman

Public face: Ferries goods and passengers beneath the low bridges. Sings only when alone, swears when watched.

Voice: Directional and rhythmic. Measures distance in pole strokes. Never says “sun” under an arch; taps the stone instead.

Belief: The pale disc is below the city, and the rose is how the cathedral's buried foundations breathe.

Secret: During a recent reflection she hooked a small object from the canal: a dry brass viewing token dated forty years in the future. Brother Caldus saw it but each pretends the other kept it. In truth Nera threw it back and remembers exactly where.

Wants now: Dive at night without attracting Custody or the canal guild.

Relationships: Distrustful friend of Caldus. Supplies Mara with routes by water but rejects her sky language. Pip Ash rides free after once warning her of a chain stretched across the canal.

Disclosure gate: Ride beneath three bridges without pressing her to name the phenomenon.

Possible change: Retrieve the token, choose to leave possible futures buried, or sell a lie about it that becomes more influential than the object.

8. Brother Caldus Fen, assistant archivist

Public face: Mild, ink-stained canon responsible for indexing Custody reports. Appears forgettable and works hard to remain so.

Voice: Dates everything, then apologizes. Uses quotations to avoid stating belief. Notices wording variations immediately.

Belief: No institution is capable of holding the whole record without shaping it; copies in adversarial hands are the closest thing to truth.

Secret: Caldus has copied sealed archive entries into the blank spaces of merchant ledgers distributed across all five squares. The indexing cipher is a children's skipping rhyme. He has also inserted one invented report to detect who is reading the copies.

Wants now: Learn which leaked fragment caused the lead theft.

Relationships: Shared the canal incident with Nera. Feeds harmless archival crumbs to Odo Vey in exchange for circulation networks. Sabine suspects but has not stopped him.

Disclosure gate: Identify one contradiction between two public records and admit what remains unknown.

Possible change: His distributed archive may democratize evidence, spark panic, or collapse because nobody can distinguish his trap from truth.

9. Canon Leth Arquen, preacher of enough

Public face: Popular Sufficientist whose sermons redirect fascination toward food, burial, and mutual obligation.

Voice: Warm, memorable, uses one concrete object per argument. Excellent at making a listener feel that restraint was their own idea.

Belief: The light may be holy or natural; obsession is definitely a moral failure when neighbours suffer.

Secret: At thirteen, Leth saw an absent version of Edrin die beneath the rose and entered the Church to prevent it. Every act of skepticism is partly an attempt not to make that remembered world real.

Wants now: Cancel public conjunction festivities and redirect their funds to a witness refuge—without explaining the personal terror beneath the campaign.

Relationships: Former spouse of Edrin. Funded publicly by Odo Vey, whose motives Leth understands but tolerates. Hessa Dault attends the sermons and hears permission for harsher action than Leth intends.

Disclosure gate: Help someone at material cost when a spectacular clue is available elsewhere.

Possible change: Can reconcile action and wonder, or weaponize charity as a way to silence legitimate inquiry.

10. Odo Vey, broker of permitted wonder

Public face: Elegant organiser of viewing tokens, pilgrim lodging, licensed lenses, printed guides, and discreet unofficial experiences.

Voice: Makes every idea sound like an invitation. Never promises an appearance, only “the most favourable conditions.” Calls bribes hospitality.

Belief: The Second Sun is real because unreal things do not sustain three generations of profitable human behaviour. Ultimate cause is commercially irrelevant.

Secret: Odo's family possesses a fragment believed to be the one Tomas Rusk broke. It is ordinary old glass. The truly anomalous object in his collection is the velvet wrapping, which shows changing constellations when placed in shadow.

Wants now: Turn conjunction into a citywide festival while funding both orderly and disorderly spectacles so no outcome ruins him.

Relationships: Patron of Leth; financier of Iven Roofless through cutouts; employer of Pip Ash; distant descendant of Ysabet Vey, a fact he advertises far beyond its actual significance.

Disclosure gate: Odo reveals facts readily but motives only when the player successfully costs him money.

Possible change: Become a civic impresario, scapegoat, genuine convert, or man who tries to put a price on the one event that ends prices.

11. Iven Roofless, Child of the Open Sky

Public face: Young roofer dismissed from three crews for removing more tiles than assigned. Magnetic speaker at illicit gatherings.

Voice: Fierce, funny, speaks in inversions: roofs are buried skies, doors are walls on hinges, secrecy is obedience delayed. Hates cautious nouns.

Belief: The visible sky is an enclosure maintained by habits of property and fear. Removing the rose's lead at conjunction will allow both daylight and social truth to pass freely.

Secret: Iven cannot see the Second Sun at all. Doubled shadows, warm stone, and other people convinced him. The Unleading is partly an attempt to force the experience everyone assumes he has.

Wants now: Acquire guild diagrams and recruit someone able to reach the outer rose without flight or scaffold access.

Relationships: Funded unknowingly by Odo. Once saved Mara from a Custody raid and believes that debt entitles him to her routes. Hessa Dault's movement killed his closest friend in a fragment-burning.

Disclosure gate: Admit to having believed another person without seeing their proof.

Possible change: Courageous liberator, catastrophic iconoclast, or person who finds a more difficult freedom than breaking glass.

12. Hessa Dault, organiser of One Shadow

Public face: Soap boiler and head of a neighbourhood burial fund. Her house provided refuge after a pilgrim crush killed six locals.

Voice: Blunt, protective, refuses metaphor. Lists names of harmed people when others discuss ideas. Never raises her voice.

Belief: Attention feeds the phenomenon socially and perhaps literally. The humane response is collective refusal.

Secret: Hessa sees the Second Sun every time she closes her eyes, outside or inside, because a glass fragment injured her during the crush. She organizes denial not from ignorance but because there is no other darkness available to her.

Wants now: Brick the west-gallery stair before conjunction and destroy the object she believes is lodged behind her eye.

Relationships: Descendant of Perrin Dault, the vanished mason. Loves her nephew and suspects his relationship with Jon Rusk. Cannot intimidate Pip Ash because Pip reminds her of a child killed in the crush.

Disclosure gate: Spend a full conversation on the names of victims without turning back to cosmology.

Possible change: Advocate for consent and limits, leader of a purge, or unwilling witness whose testimony reshapes the movement she built.

13. Pip Ash, child guide and passage runner

Public face: Eleven-year-old seller of chalk maps that are almost correct. Offers pilgrims a “guaranteed second shadow” using mirrors and an accomplice.

Voice: Quick bargains, devastating mimicry of official phrases, changes names for adults weekly. Goes quiet around genuine manifestations.

Belief: Adults make rules about the light because children find routes first. Pip distinguishes “real wrong” from “paid wrong” but cannot articulate how.

Secret: Pip can locate transient absent alleys by sound: footsteps return one extra echo. The ability began after sleeping inside a painted soot window. Pip has used it to hide runaway apprentices.

Wants now: Find an absent route into Odo's locked collection and steal back a friend's confiscated charm.

Relationships: Works for Odo and Mara; rides free with Nera; disarms Hessa; is classified in Custody records as three different children due to the names.

Disclosure gate: Follow Pip on an apparently pointless route without taking control or demanding an explanation.

Possible change: Become a route-keeper, informant, celebrated witness, or child allowed to remain more than a mechanism for adult mysteries.

14. Provost Gerren Salvi, civic rationalist

Public face: Magistrate responsible for property, market regulation, and conjunction crowd planning. Publicly dismisses sectarian extremes.

Voice: Legal clarity, polished impatience. Reframes metaphysical disputes as jurisdiction, liability, and access. Says “regardless of interpretation” often.

Belief: Whatever the light is, the city must remain governable on the morning after it appears.

Secret: Gerren's title depends on an inheritance whose boundary was created by the Great Revision. Avel's absent map identifies the older deed and could transfer half the family estate to a charitable foundation—or expose an old murder unrelated to inheritance but worse for the family name.

Wants now: Contain Avel's claim without publicly discrediting all counter-memory, which the city relies on for emerging light surveys.

Relationships: Institutional ally and personal rival of Sabine. Buys Odo's crowd data. His alternate mother appears in Avel's most dangerous memory.

Disclosure gate: Demonstrate an ability to compromise without pretending the compromise is truth.

Possible change: Build fair civic rules for incompatible realities or use administrative neutrality to protect inherited injustice.

15. Sister Belis, singer of the unheard vespers

Public face: Elderly choir nun with perfect pitch and a reputation for stopping rehearsals when the building “breathes between notes.”

Voice: Musical interval and bodily sensation. Hums before remembering. Treats silence as an answer, not absence.

Belief: The Second Sun is heard before it is seen. The duplicate vespers of 83 AFG still continues at a pitch most people cannot perceive.

Secret: Belis has transcribed the unheard service. Its melodic intervals map onto the lead geometry of the rose; a missing cadence corresponds to the stolen outer pieces. Singing the completed cadence might activate, repair, or end something.

Wants now: Hear each city bell alone and record its wrong-light overtone before conjunction.

Relationships: Caldus supplies old music fragments. Tomas dismisses her theory in public and adjusts repairs to her humming in private. She taught Pip the skipping rhyme used in Caldus's cipher without knowing its archival role.

Disclosure gate: Listen through an entire silence without interrupting.

Possible change: Complete the second vespers, choose to leave its final note unsung, or discover the city itself supplies the missing cadence.

Generating additional actors

For each new Second Sun actor, specify:

  1. an ordinary livelihood and present inconvenience;
  2. one phrase for the phenomenon that marks social position;
  3. one evidence source and one limit on knowledge;
  4. one belief, one doubt, and one thing they refuse to risk;
  5. a relationship across faction lines;
  6. a secret with human consequences, not only cosmic information;
  7. a disclosure gate expressed through play;
  8. at least two possible evolutions.

Avoid making every secret a hidden fragment or every want a request to investigate the sun. The baker whose oven goes cold in wrong light, the debtor using a fake counter-memory, and the guard embarrassed by never seeing anything may be more alive than another occult expert.
