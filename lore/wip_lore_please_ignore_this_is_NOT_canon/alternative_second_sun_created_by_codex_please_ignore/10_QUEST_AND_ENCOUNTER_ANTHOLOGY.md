# Quest and encounter anthology

These situations are structured around observation, movement, speech, custody,
and return. They can be authored arcs, generated incidents, or prompt contexts.
Each preserves ultimate ambiguity while offering local facts the player can
actually determine.

## 1. Two Shadows at Closing

**Scale:** introductory encounter, 10–20 minutes  
**People:** Enna Voss, Jon Rusk, a frightened pilgrim  
**Places:** nave north row, west doors

### Inciting moment

As the cathedral closes, a lone pilgrim casts a second shadow that remains after
wrong light fades. The pilgrim sees no duplicate and panics when strangers point.
Jon asks the player to clear space; Enna insists the pilgrim be paid before any
questioning delays their journey.

### Player approaches

- observe without touching or accusing;
- place neutral objects beside the shadow to compare behavior;
- calm the pilgrim by voice or ask consent for a woad trace;
- fetch a physician, Custodian, glazier, or family member;
- turn the crowd away, losing some evidence but protecting the person;
- publicly claim miracle, fraud, or uncertainty.

### Local answer

The persistent mark is on fine woad dust transferred from a market viewing card,
but the second shadow created the pale pattern. The material explanation and
wrong-light cause are both necessary.

### Return states

A careful response makes the pilgrim later write with trust. Spectacle creates a
profitable “shadow cure” based on woad. Custody-first response yields access to
their process but makes Enna distrust the player. Dismissing it as fraud harms
the pilgrim and pleases the Concord superficially, until they learn a person was
humiliated.

## 2. The Counterfeit That Worked

**Scale:** market investigation  
**People:** Odo Vey, lens seller, sick customer, guild apprentice

### Inciting moment

A cheap blue lens sold as ancient rose glass produces a visible bright edge and
makes its buyer ill. The guild proves the glass is modern bottle waste. Custody
wants the stall closed; Odo wants the “miracle” packaged; Concord threatens to
smash every lens.

### Investigation

Trace each component: glass, pigment, frame, velvet pouch, seller's demonstration
site. The anomalous element is not the lens but soot from Candlewick Court ground
into its lining. Illness comes from toxic pigment, a separate mundane danger.

### Choice

Publicly separate anomaly from poisoning, suppress everything, give the soot to
the plasterers, publish the recipe, or let Custody confiscate it. A precise answer
may still fuel a market in dangerous fake soot.

### Fun

The quest teaches that “counterfeit” and “inactive” are not synonyms and that
evidence follows objects through trade.

## 3. The Fourth Passage

**Scale:** social-navigation initiation  
**People:** Mara-of-the-Lath, Pip Ash, Jon Rusk  
**Places:** changing dogleg route, market door, covered passage

### Inciting moment

Mara offers a meeting if the player can walk the current chapter and whisper the
response. Pip sells an easier map. Jon is posted near one route segment.

### Requirements

The player must notice the obsolete street name after rain, accept a cooper's lie
without exposing it, hold a door for a burdened stranger, and find the ceiling
line in the actual fourth boundary space. Flying to the final location fails to
establish the social route but reveals a roof clue useful later.

### Complication

An Open Sky follower shouts an old passphrase to force the cell to relocate. The
player chooses whether to pursue, protect the meeting, or help uninvolved passage
residents whose homes are suddenly searched.

### Outcome

Access is not binary. The player can earn Mara's trust, learn only the next route,
or be accepted by radicals instead. A compromised route remains physically
present but socially dead.

## 4. Avel's Door

**Scale:** ethical investigation  
**People:** Avel Noll, Edrin Pell, the Cobb family, Provost Salvi

### Inciting moment

Avel recognizes a filled door in the Cobbs' cellar and knows the hinge position.
She wants a wooden horse remembered on the other side. The Cobbs want crowds and
claimants removed. Salvi offers the player a legal access order if all findings
go first to him.

### Approaches

- negotiate limited access and repair bond with the Cobbs;
- survey from an adjacent property;
- compare Avel's memory before revealing existing plans;
- use remote light alignment to locate a void;
- break through without consent;
- refuse the investigation and instead commission a replica horse for Avel.

### Local answer

There is a sealed space containing a child's wooden horse and evidence of a past
crime. Dates do not match Avel's biography. Whether she remembers history,
another life, or information gained through wrong light remains open.

### Stakes

Discovery can vindicate Avel while violating the Cobbs. Restraint can protect a
home while leaving an injustice sealed. The best negotiated result costs time,
money, and some evidentiary purity.

## 5. Seven Strips, Seven Custodians

**Scale:** multi-stage archive conspiracy  
**People:** Sabine, Tomas, Caldus, Mara, other strip holders

### Inciting moment

A portion of silvered parchment appears in a mundane case file. It shows reversed
architecture. Sabine asks for its quiet return; Caldus asks the player to find
why seven index entries share a deliberate error.

### Structure

Each strip is held in a different social mode: legal archive, guild pattern,
Fellowship route, merchant ledger, family memorial, confiscation inventory, and
one player's prior screenshot alignment. Gathering them does not mean stealing
them; the player may persuade holders to bring their pieces while retaining
custody.

### Key choice

Assemble physically, hold one hand apart as Sabine requests, reproduce by drawing,
publish locations, or leave distributed. Each method creates a different kind of
knowledge and vulnerability.

### Local answer

The image depicts a coherent but architecturally different cathedral and predicts
one remote light-fall. It does not prove whether the building exists.

## 6. The Missing Eleven Minutes

**Scale:** bell-and-route mystery  
**People:** Sister Belis, clockmakers, night workers

### Inciting moment

City clocks lose eleven minutes again. During the gap, several NPC routines have
already occurred: bread delivered, gate unlocked, a confession heard. The actors
remember doing them but no clock registered time.

### Play

Reconstruct the interval through bells, walking distances, cooling bread, canal
current, and testimony. Players who rely on UI time alone are misled; the city is
the clock.

### Local answer

The events occurred in a consistent eleven-minute interval associated with a
duplicate bell return, not time travel in any provable sense. Someone exploited
the gap to steal lead while guards followed clocks.

### Consequence

Publishing a reliable method helps workers account for missing time and teaches
thieves how to use it. Concealing it aids Custody and leaves accused workers
unable to prove alibis.

## 7. The Sun Under Water

**Scale:** canal expedition  
**People:** Nera Quill, Caldus, canal guild, College substructuralist

### Inciting moment

Nera needs help reaching the future-dated token she returned to the canal. The
College seeks permission to drain the reach; this would halt deliveries and
expose the Fellowship's water route.

### Approaches

- night dive guided by pole taps and reflection;
- negotiate a brief drain during low trade;
- use a basket, hook, or trained swimmer;
- falsify an obstruction order;
- decide the token should stay buried.

### Complication

At the basin, the wrong sun appears below. Looking up loses sight of it. Speaking
“sun” causes Nera to abandon the attempt, not because of magic but because the
player violated a stated boundary at the most vulnerable moment.

### Local answer

The token is physically real, dry inside a sealed seam, and stamped with a die
not currently used. Its temporal origin is unresolved.

## 8. A Roof Is Weather Chosen for Another

**Scale:** faction crisis  
**People:** Iven Roofless, Mara, residents beneath targeted roofs, Hessa

### Inciting moment

Open Sky radicals plan to uncover a chain of roofs to create a citywide sightline
at conjunction. Their map crosses crowded tenements, a secret detention room,
the one-shadow refuge, and an empty guild warehouse.

### Player role

Walk the proposed line from both above and below. Interview residents, expose the
detention room, distinguish consensual openings from imposed ones, offer
alternative structures, or support/stop the entire plan.

### Design point

The player cannot solve politics by choosing “open” or “closed.” Every roof is
both barrier and shelter. A negotiated route may be less optically perfect but
more legitimate.

### Return

Rain makes the consequence immediate. People remember who left them under sky,
who hid prisoners behind doctrine, and who did practical repair afterward.

## 9. The Unheard Vespers

**Scale:** acoustic collection and performance  
**People:** Sister Belis, bell ringers, Tomas, choir

### Inciting moment

Belis asks the player to record or remember the overtone of each major bell in
wrong light. Bells are navigational anchors across the city; reaching the right
listening positions requires roof, canal, square, and nave routes.

### Interaction

The player can request rings by voice, persuade campanologists, wait for canonical
hours, or illegally ring a bell. Their own sung or spoken pitch may help identify
intervals without requiring perfect musical ability; accessible visual vibration
supports the task.

### Decision

Belis's completed cadence appears capable of joining two lead sections. Perform
it at conjunction, omit the last note, give it to Custody, or teach it publicly so
no faction controls the song.

### Ambiguity

Any resulting change could be acoustic resonance, collective ritual, or response
from the phenomenon. The social meaning depends on who was allowed to sing.

## 10. The Witness Who Saw Nothing

**Scale:** character-centered conversational quest  
**People:** Iven Roofless, a skeptic College member

### Inciting moment

Iven privately admits he has never seen the Second Sun. A skeptical scholar wants
to test whether belief predicts reported vision and considers Iven a perfect
subject. Iven wants access to the gallery during a strong appearance and fears
public humiliation.

### Options

- arrange private access;
- refuse to convert him into a trial;
- help test nonvisual phenomena he does perceive;
- expose his secret to weaken the Unleading;
- lie that he saw it;
- let him testify publicly that belief does not require personal vision.

### Local answer

Iven reliably detects a glass resonance without seeing the disc. His absence of
vision remains real. The quest reframes witness hierarchy rather than “curing”
him.

## 11. One Room Without Colour

**Scale:** sanctuary breach  
**People:** Hessa, refuge residents, Edrin, Custody

### Inciting moment

A narrow afterlight appears inside the one-shadow refuge despite blacked panes.
Some residents want investigation; others insist the room's rule must hold. A
frightened child is accused of smuggling glass.

### Investigation with consent

The player can inspect exterior walls, interview only volunteers, trace recent
repairs, or ask everyone to leave temporarily. The source is a remote fall
following an erased door line, not contraband.

### Choice

Seal the old boundary, redirect the fall, preserve a small opt-in viewing space,
or publicize the breach. Even a technically elegant solution can destroy the
refuge if imposed.

### Return

The hall's future—safer, divided, closed, radicalized—depends on process as much
as result.

## 12. The Lead in the Bread

**Scale:** compact investigation feeding the main arc  
**People:** Jon, a baker, Tomas, Sabine, Pip

### Inciting moment

A stolen curl of rose lead is anonymously baked into a loaf delivered to the
guild. The baker is arrested despite having handled a sealed order.

### Clues

Oven ash, delivery route, dough scoring, bread token, and when the metal entered
relative to baking. Pip knows a shortcut that makes the official timeline false.

### Local answer

Jon arranged the return but did not bake it; he used a Fellowship hospitality
chain. The baker is innocent. Revealing Jon may expose the chain and Mara's cell.

### Resolutions

Prove only the baker's innocence, expose Jon, manufacture a plausible market
accident, or convince Sabine to accept an off-record surrender. Precision and
discretion conflict rather than align neatly.

## 13. Window-Eater

**Scale:** emergent city-name episode  
**People:** any actors who have heard player rumours

### Inciting moment

After repeatedly chasing manifestations and neglecting people, NPCs begin calling
the player Window-Eater. A merchant likes the name and prints it; a bereaved
witness spits it; a child asks whether windows taste blue.

### Player response

The player can embrace spectacle, object publicly, change behavior, perform care,
or attempt to seed a different epithet. Directly demanding a rename has little
effect; giving the city a new compressible story does.

### Purpose

The encounter turns play style into language and makes reputation audible. If
the player changes, the old name may persist among groups harmed earlier.

## 14. Procession on the Absent Bridge

**Scale:** major public set piece  
**People:** chapter, Fellowship, pilgrims, guards, counter-rememberers

### Inciting moment

At Two Vespers, several counter-rememberers independently propose carrying the
processional lamp along the absent forecourt bridge. Officials see a crowd risk;
Unwallers see public recognition; merchants see spectacle.

### Route design

The bridge exists as a sequence of stairs, balconies, awnings, and overhead
links. The player can survey it, repair supports, negotiate passage, guide by
voice, or use flight to demonstrate the visual composition. Public flight may
redirect all theology toward the player.

### Outcomes

- successful consensual route makes absent knowledge civic without proving its
  ontology;
- forced route damages homes and radicalizes opposition;
- official cancellation prompts a spontaneous street-level shadow procession;
- a staged merchant version looks spectacular but severs Keeper trust.

## 15. Conjunction: No Wall Calls Itself the Sky

**Scale:** culminating systemic event  
**People:** entire relationship web  
**Places:** rose, five squares, passages, canal, roofs, refuge

### Preparation ledger

The event reads prior conduct rather than asking one final dialogue choice:

- rose condition and stolen lead recovered or not;
- archive centralized, distributed, or destroyed;
- Fellowship routes safe or compromised;
- roofs opened consensually or forcibly;
- refuge respected or breached;
- cadence known and by whom;
- counter-rememberers invited, exploited, or excluded;
- crowd shaped by rumours and market spectacle;
- player's city epithet and trusted relationships.

### During the event

Both discs approach overlap. Remote falls connect chosen locations. Actors act
from their actual beliefs and memories. The player moves, speaks, carries one
critical object or message, and cannot be everywhere. Who can act without them is
the result of earlier empowerment.

Potential urgent choices:

- stabilize a lead line or allow two colours to meet;
- complete or interrupt Belis's cadence;
- open west doors to a crowd or protect those inside;
- follow an invitation into an absent route or remain with a present person;
- tell the city exactly what the player sees, repeat someone else's account, or
  admit divergence.

### The non-answer

At overlap, players receive an intense but perspective-bound experience: perhaps
the alternate cathedral, two cities superimposed, a sun beneath water, or total
ordinary daylight. Different witnesses report different coherent views. Physical
consequences—glass, light paths, missing or present objects—remain shared.

### The answer that matters

The next morning reveals who owns access, whose testimony counts, who has shelter,
which routes remain, whether craft continues, and how the city names the player.

## Repeatable systemic encounters

### A rumour asks for correction

An actor repeats a mutated version of something the player said. Correcting it
may expose the original source. Letting it stand may protect someone but cause a
new harm.

### Two shadows in a market deal

A seller claims an item's second shadow proves authenticity. Buyer and seller ask
the player to witness. Physical observation can detect a trick; social history
determines whether calling it out is safe.

### An absent address

An NPC asks directions to a street remembered but not present. The player can use
the double map, ask locals, follow remote light, dismiss them, or guide them to
the closest present equivalent.

### Custody cord closure

A manifestation closes part of the cathedral during another obligation: funeral,
delivery, choir practice, repair. The player chooses whether wonder overrides
ordinary access.

### Soot window at dawn

A new painted opening appears. Preserve, clean, investigate, sell, report, or let
children decide. Its physical effect may be absent; its political effect is not.

### A request not to look

An actor asks the player to turn away while they cross wrong light, destroy a
letter, or confess a counter-memory. Respecting this sacrifices information and
builds a particular kind of trust.

## Quest-writing template

For additional content, fill in:

1. **Ordinary activity interrupted:** work, travel, worship, trade, care.
2. **Observable anomaly:** concrete and locally testable.
3. **Person at risk of becoming evidence:** name their non-mystery need.
4. **Competing custodians:** at least two groups with different legitimate values.
5. **Architectural verb:** viewpoint, route, threshold, reflection, elevation.
6. **Speech verb:** whisper, testify, refuse, relay, read aloud, warn.
7. **Local answer:** something the player can determine fairly.
8. **Protected mystery:** what remains open.
9. **Circulation consequence:** how the story spreads or is suppressed.
10. **Return state:** visible, audible, or relational change on revisit.

If a quest only ends with a lore revelation, add a person who must live with the
revelation and a place the player can revisit afterward.
