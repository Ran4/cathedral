# Game design: seeing the unsayable

## Design promise

The Second Sun makes a rendering contradiction into a social world. The player
does not merely discover that a wrong-coloured sun exists. They learn where it
can be seen, whom it is safe to tell, how evidence changes when circulated, and
what kind of city grows around an undeniable phenomenon no institution can
explain without harming someone.

The feature is fun when a spectacular sight leads directly to curiosity,
navigation, conversation, performance, and consequence:

> **See something impossible → test it in architecture → decide whom to involve
> → watch the account mutate socially → revisit a changed place or relationship.**

It should not collapse into “collect ten lore notes” or a faction dialogue tree.

## Player fantasy

The player becomes an **unlicensed witness**: unusually mobile, socially
unattached, able to cross class and district boundaries, and not yet committed to
the city's inherited vocabulary. Their advantage is not a chosen-one ability.
It is freedom to compare perspectives that institutions keep separate.

Over time the player may be treated as:

- a naive pilgrim;
- a useful courier of observations;
- a safe listener or dangerous gossip;
- a surveyor of wrong light;
- a route-keeper, Custody auxiliary, professional skeptic, spectacle broker, or
  unwelcome independent;
- a person whose own shadow and memories have become public evidence.

The city should coin an epithet from conduct—“Two-Step,” “Window-Eater,” “the
Quiet Witness,” “Lead-Bringer,” “One-Hand-Here”—rather than show a Second Sun
reputation meter.

## Experience pillars

### 1. Beauty before explanation

The first encounter should be visually legible without prior text: two suns
through one rose, contradictory rays, doubled shadows. Let the player inhabit
the image before anyone explains it.

### 2. Architecture is the investigative instrument

The player changes position, angle, enclosure, elevation, glass colour, water
reflection, time, and route. The city itself replaces a scanning gadget.

### 3. Speech is action

What the player says, how loudly, where, and before whom changes the social state
of evidence. Whispered passphrases, spoken testimony, public accusations, careful
omission, and reading documents aloud are verbs with consequences.

### 4. Every answer has a constituency

Interpretations are tied to jobs, griefs, money, power, and care practices. Giving
evidence to a faction advances people, not an abstract ideology.

### 5. Mystery without arbitrariness

Ultimate cause remains open, but local questions have reliable answers. The
player can determine which pane transmits light, who stole lead, which report was
forged, where a beam will land, or who leaked a passphrase. Ambiguous cosmology
must not excuse incoherent detective design.

### 6. Consequence through retelling

The player experiences impact by overhearing changed stories, seeing new notices,
finding a route sealed or crowded, and being addressed differently—not by a
modal “faction reputation +10.”

## Core verbs

### Look

Stand in a precise place; align the rose with architecture; compare direct and
reflected views; observe a person's two shadows without violating them; notice
colours, dust, warmth, and sound.

The design should reward patient looking but never require medically unsafe
staring or exact colour discrimination.

### Move

Follow beams through dogleg streets, climb to boundary spaces, pass under bridges,
walk counter-memory routes, re-create an absent structure through viewpoints.
Flight can expose otherwise impossible sightlines but carries social consequences
if witnessed and risks bypassing the relational knowledge needed to interpret a
site.

### Listen

Hear how a rumour differs by district, compare a bell note with the rose's lead
geometry, follow an extra footstep echo, notice when an actor changes from public
to private vocabulary.

### Speak

Ask open questions, report precisely, lie, protect a source, whisper a rotating
passphrase, read a document aloud, give public testimony, shout a warning, or
refuse to answer.

### Record

Make a charcoal shadow trace, mark a map, preserve afterlight on woad, collect a
testimony with consent, or create a screenshot/plate as a survey artifact. A
record includes provenance; detached “evidence points” are less valuable.

### Carry

Transport documents, lens frames, lead curls, woad cloth, spoken messages, and
secrets between people. The object or account can be inspected, altered, stolen,
sold, or deliberately withheld.

### Alter

Open or close shutters, move a mirror, clean or preserve plaster, scratch a
repair flaw, relight a kiln, expose a sealed site, remove a roof tile, or return
lead. Alteration should be consequential and rarely framed as morally neutral.

### Wait

Attend a particular bell, allow a silence, revisit a site after weather or public
reaction changes, or choose ordinary duty over chasing an appearance. Waiting is
meaningful when the world continues around it.

## The witness loop

### Phase 1: manifestation

A visual, acoustic, or mnemonic anomaly occurs. It can be scheduled within a
window but should retain some unplanned appearances tied to routine workers and
low-spectacle moments.

### Phase 2: situated observation

The player can change one or two conditions: viewpoint, pane colour, obstruction,
reflection, company, voice, instrument. The observation produces a small local
question with testable options.

Example: Does the remote fall stop at the present wall, pass through it, or stop
at the line of a demolished wall?

### Phase 3: custody choice

Evidence is fragile or socially owned. The player decides whether to bring in a
glazier, Custodian, Keeper, witness's family, merchant, or nobody. The choice
determines method, access, risk, and interpretation.

### Phase 4: circulation

The account enters conversations. Each retelling preserves source and emotional
core while potentially mutating detail. Publicity may draw crowds, seal a site,
raise prices, validate a victim, expose a cell, or create a fraudulent market.

### Phase 5: return

The player revisits the physical site or affected person. The world reflects
circulation: chalk marks, guards, offerings, vandalism, absence, changed dialogue,
or a new use. The return closes the small question while opening a larger one.

## Observation design

### The condition matrix

Each manifestation can be authored from a compact set of conditions:

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| Viewpoint | nave, aisle, gallery, reflection, roof alignment |
| Boundary | indoors, outdoors, threshold, shared wall, bridge arch |
| Material | original glass, repair glass, woad, gold, water, lime |
| Time | ordinary hour, bell interval, feast, weather, crowd state |
| Social | alone, with believer, with skeptic, being watched, speaking |
| History | current wall, erased street, plague ward, old foundation |

A strong puzzle asks the player to vary only a few dimensions and lets the result
remain readable in-world.

### Evidence quality

Evidence is not a universal score. Actors judge it differently:

- Custody values chain of handling, independent accounts, and controlled wording.
- Glaziers value panel provenance, material response, and craft repeatability.
- Keepers value mundane counter-memory detail and overlap without prior contact.
- Fellowship route-keepers value embodied knowledge of a changing location.
- College members value measurements and published failure.
- Ordinary kin value whether the investigation helps or harms the witness.

One artifact can therefore open one door and close another.

### Fair ambiguity

For any major ambiguous clue:

1. establish the physical observation clearly;
2. provide at least two interpretations with named advocates;
3. show what each interpretation predicts next;
4. allow a local prediction to succeed or fail;
5. preserve an alternative explanation at the ultimate level.

The player should say “I know what happened; I don't know what it ultimately
means,” not “the game makes up whatever it wants.”

## Voice and smart-actor design

### Whisper as social and spatial key

Fellowship meetings require an actual whisper because acoustic behavior is part
of secrecy. Volume alone is insufficient: a current route response and observed
proof are also needed. A shouted correct phrase compromises the route instead of
unlocking it.

### Testimony as structured free speech

After a manifestation, a Lector or scholar asks concrete questions:

- Where did you stand?
- Which surface brightened first?
- Did your second shadow move with you?
- Who else was present?
- What did you infer, distinct from what you saw?

The player answers naturally. The actor should summarize back, allowing correction.
The stored memory separates perception and interpretation. This makes careful
speech mechanically useful without demanding magic exact phrasing.

### Public and private vocabulary

Actors track audience. A glazier who says “the Occluded” in a private loft may
say “western appearance” when a guard enters. The player learns affiliations by
code-switching. Repeating private wording publicly can betray trust even without
naming a source.

### Silence and refusal

“I won't tell you” is a supported action. The social system can remember refused
testimony, protected names, or an actor's request for silence. Some relationships
deepen when the player does not exhaust every dialogue possibility.

### Rumour drift with anchors

Free retelling should keep structured anchors: event identity, original source,
place, confidence, and harm risk. The language can mutate, but game-critical facts
remain recoverable through triangulation. NPCs may explicitly say whom they heard
it from, allowing the player to trace drift.

## Social consequence model

Track concrete remembered deeds and a few latent dimensions per community:

- **discretion:** protects sources and boundaries;
- **precision:** distinguishes observation from conclusion;
- **care:** treats witnesses as people;
- **access politics:** broadens or restricts who may see;
- **spectacle:** amplifies events publicly;
- **intervention:** alters sites and artifacts.

These dimensions do not need UI. They inform dialogue, requests, access, and the
epithet the city develops. A player can be precise but cruel, caring but
inaccurate, discreet but complicit. Avoid a single moral axis.

## Long-form arc: the approaching conjunction

The conjunction provides urgency without supplying the cosmological answer.

### Act I — The extra shadow

The player encounters the full visual premise and resolves local incidents:
counterfeit lens, unexpected remote fall, witness in distress. They learn that
talking changes access.

### Act II — The city behind its explanations

Faction invitations overlap. The player walks an Unwaller chapter, enters the
measuring room, helps at the kiln, maps an absent street, or protects the refuge.
Evidence suggests the appearance is intensifying. Lead theft begins.

### Act III — The disputed preparations

Three conjunction calculations align. The chapter plans controlled worship,
merchants a festival, Custody a closure, Fellowship a public witnessing, Open Sky
an Unleading, Concord a bricking-up, College an experiment. Each preparation has
reasonable small tasks whose combined consequences become visible.

### Act IV — Two days at once

The conjunction is not one fixed cutscene. Its social and spatial form reflects
the city's preparation:

- Are crowds in the forecourt, dispersed across openings, or locked out?
- Is the rose structurally whole, reframed, missing lead, or veiled?
- Do bells complete Belis's cadence?
- Are counter-rememberers present by choice or confined “for safety”?
- Which documents have been made public?
- What name does the city call the player?

The sky remains ambiguous. The civic outcome is not.

### Act V — The morning that remains

The feature's emotional payoff is the next ordinary morning. Shops open or do
not. Someone is missing, returned, vindicated, scapegoated, freed, imprisoned,
or simply exhausted. The cathedral light may have changed subtly. People argue
about what happened. The player sees what their way of handling truth built.

## Conjunction outcome families

These are systemic configurations, not good/bad endings.

### Custodied wonder

The rose survives, panic is low, evidence is centralized, and access narrows.
Witnesses receive orderly care but dissident routes close. The player gains
archives and loses street trust.

### Unwalled commons

Many boundary sites are open and public testimony flourishes. The Office loses
monopoly; fraud, crowd danger, and coercive visibility grow. The Fellowship must
learn governance it previously criticized.

### One-shadow peace

The city collectively turns away or covers the rose. Everyday life stabilizes;
some affected people feel profound relief. Inquiry and pilgrimage collapse, and
wrong light begins appearing in less controllable domestic places.

### Reframed instrument

The guild alters part of the rose. New phenomena reveal patterns but erase old
ones. Craft becomes recognized as active authorship rather than passive custody,
splitting the guild and chapter.

### Spectacle city

The festival succeeds economically and evidence spreads widely, mixed with
fraud. The phenomenon becomes impossible to suppress and difficult to discuss
sincerely. The player may be celebrated while private witnesses withdraw.

### The unfinished cadence

No faction controls the event because the player preserves incompatible needs
and leaves a key action incomplete. The conjunction passes quietly or diffusely.
This is not the “neutral best” outcome: unresolved dangers and relationships
remain, but no single theory colonizes the future.

## Failure philosophy

Avoid hard quest failure where possible. Convert mistakes into changed situations.

- Shouting a passphrase compromises a route; the cell moves and distrust grows.
- Losing a document means another faction finds or sells it.
- Missing an appearance preserves relationships or duties elsewhere.
- Publishing bad evidence creates a rumour the player can trace and correct, but
  never perfectly erase.
- Breaking glass does not reload the mystery; it changes transmitted phenomena
  and creates responsibility.
- A witness refusing the player is a valid relationship state, not incomplete
  content.

Irreversible choices need clear material warning: a craftsperson says what cannot
be restored, a resident states whose consent is missing, an object visibly bears
history.

## Integrating flight

Flight creates a productive theological and social collision.

- It offers exceptional alignments: absent bridge composition, exterior view of
  remote falls, rose inspection without official scaffold.
- Witnesses interpret it through their beliefs, feeding the Heresy of Flight and
  contaminating Second Sun testimony.
- Using flight during a secret route may invalidate embodied proof: the point was
  learning doors and social permission, not reaching coordinates.
- A flying player can discover evidence earlier but may be treated as part of the
  phenomenon, reducing credibility with skeptics and increasing dangerous
  attention from apocalyptic groups.

Do not universally punish flight. Make it a method whose social meaning travels.

## Integrating screenshots as camera-obscura plates

The screenshot system naturally supports survey play if framed diegetically:

- a captured image can carry position, facing, time, and visible manifestation;
- actors judge composition differently from evidentiary value;
- the Second Sun might appear in the image when not seen, or fail to appear when
  witnessed, creating a specific contradiction rather than random unreliability;
- side-by-side images from absent-route viewpoints can compose a larger symbol;
- public display of a plate spreads a rumour more quickly than private testimony.

Preserve actual player images as personal history. Do not overwrite them with
canonical art.

## Sound design intent

The Second Sun has no grand constant theme. Use a restrained acoustic grammar:

- a near-unison or minor-second beating tied to wrong light intensity;
- lead and glass resonance at specific viewpoints;
- room tone subtly arriving from the wrong direction;
- duplicate bell returns without duplicate strikes;
- breath and whispers unusually intimate under remote falls;
- silence at conjunction as absence of expected reverberation, not muted master
  audio.

Players should sometimes locate a manifestation by hearing architecture disagree
before seeing it.

## Visual design intent

- Colour: pale violet-white with a bruised edge, explicitly difficult to name.
- The ordinary sun remains warmer and more physically complete.
- Godrays should contradict direction but still respect local occlusion enough
  to be read as light.
- Double shadows differ subtly in softness and response latency.
- Wrong light reveals texture rather than covering the world in a colour grade.
- Rare escalation removes shadows or shows counter-sky; constant excess makes the
  impossible ordinary too quickly.

## Accessibility

The mystery must not depend on colour vision, hearing, microphone use, or precise
timing alone.

- Pair hue with shadow direction, warmth, dust motion, vibration, and actor
  reactions.
- Provide subtitles/descriptive text for wrong-direction sound and bell intervals.
- Passphrases may be spoken, selected, typed, or signed through an equivalent
  interaction; secrecy intent matters more than vocal ability.
- Do not require sustained bright-light exposure or rapid flicker.
- Allow generous observation windows or repeatable low-stakes manifestations for
  core information.
- Give nonvisual routes: echo count, bell direction, warmed surfaces, guided
  testimony.
- Let actors accommodate a player who communicates without speech in-world,
  preserving social depth rather than treating it as failure.

## Content pacing

Use a ratio near:

- 60% ordinary life touched subtly by the phenomenon;
- 25% social conflict and interpretation;
- 10% investigative anomaly;
- 5% full impossible spectacle.

The exact numbers are illustrative. The point is contrast. A loaf tasting bitter,
a sweeper losing half a day to testimony, or a child selling a fake map keeps
the Second Sun present without constantly firing godrays.

## Prototype ladder (design only)

This collection does not change the game. If implementation is considered later,
the feature can be validated in increasing scope.

### Stage 1: the iconic room

One rose-only second disc, contradictory ray, doubled player/NPC shadows, three
vantage reactions, and a short conversation with public/private vocabulary.
Question: does the image create immediate curiosity?

### Stage 2: one evidence loop

Add a remote fall, woad trace, two actors who value evidence differently, and a
return-state change. Question: does circulation feel like play rather than lore
delivery?

### Stage 3: one passage cell

Add route-based whisper access, compromise behavior, and a meeting with internal
disagreement. Question: can natural voice and architecture jointly gate trust?

### Stage 4: citywide rumour

Propagate one witnessed event through structured mutation and let the player
triangulate it. Question: is drift legible and emotionally believable?

### Stage 5: conjunction slice

Combine three preparations and show a changed next morning. Question: do choices
express values through concrete city state?

## Evaluation questions

Playtests should ask:

- Did players notice the second source before being told?
- Could they describe what was physically stable and what remained uncertain?
- Did they care who received evidence, or only seek the nominal quest owner?
- Could they identify an NPC's position from vocabulary and behavior?
- Did withholding speech ever feel active and respected?
- Did a rumour return in a recognizable but surprising form?
- Did architecture—not UI—guide at least one successful inquiry?
- Did the next-morning consequence feel connected to player conduct?
- Did any faction seem secretly designated as correct?
- Was the phenomenon still beautiful after prolonged play?

## What not to build

- A morality meter labelled Church versus Heretics.
- A single conspiracy in which one bishop knows the complete scientific answer.
- Random hallucinations that invalidate careful investigation.
- Passphrases as static keyword checks detached from place and trust.
- Exposition NPCs who recite the entire canon.
- Endless collectible shards whose only purpose is completion percentage.
- A “true ending” that explains the sun and makes other interpretations foolish.
- Constant purple post-processing.
- Punishment for players who use accessibility alternatives to voice or colour.
- Apocalyptic stakes so total that bread, rent, family, craft, and ordinary harm
  stop mattering.

The Second Sun should deepen the city, not replace it.
