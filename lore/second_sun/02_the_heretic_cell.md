# The Unwalled — Faction Brief

> Use: Structural/meta brief on the heretic cell for designers and sidecar prompt-building; the final block may be injected into any sworn member's NPC context once its two world-state placeholders are filled.
> Canon: 00_canon.md

## 1. Names and standing

They call themselves **the Unwalled**. The Custody's files say **the Attergate error**, or **Colmites**. The street says **the Greensick**. Members call one another **neighbor**, and no other title aloud; rank is inferred, never heard.

They are not a mob, a guild, or a rival church: about nineteen people who pay for paupers' funerals, keep a cloth map of the sky, and are wrong on purpose about the one thing they organized to be right about. Their core doctrine — the green sun is real, elder than the other, walled out of the sky by four centuries of Church masonry, and shows through the Great Rose because old glass remembers the sky it cooled under — is FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED (00_canon.md §10, #14–15); nothing may vindicate it. What they get right: the Church is lying about something; the phenomenon is objective; the Grey Press holds suppressed tests. It is enough.

## 2. History in five wounds

The cell recites its own history like a rosary of injuries.

**F.301 — the Founding Drowning.** Colm Attergate, a mason's laborer, preached in the Bellstand that the west towers were coffin lids and the Church was "walling a sun out of the sky, course by course." Banished, he drowned at the Serle sluice trying to come home by water, while the Cut still ran. The dry Old Sluice keeps the place in the present city. The heresy dates itself from him; the phrase is his; the **Testimony of Colm** opens with his Bellstand words.

**F.362 — the Forty.** In the Great Rains, forty were arrested for saying the green sun fed the city while the true one failed. The Forty were not a cell; they were talkers, and their arrest taught the sympathy to stop being talk. From that year: meet hidden, name nothing, bury the poor. The funeral purse begins here, plain charity, decades before anyone made a clock of it.

**F.428 — the Whisper Arrests.** Five seized in Gaunt Passage on an informant's tip; two recanted on the Gradine; a fuller died in custody "of the damp." The informant was never named. The lights, the leads, the death-clock pass were all built in the years after, so it could never happen twice. Every member remembers, and suspects everyone.

**F.431–433 — pride and appetite.** Their Concurrence arithmetic beat the official almanac by a day; the correction came unacknowledged — a confession, they rightly judged. The Ledger Theft then loosed archive copies into the city and gave the cell its consuming near-goal: buying, page by page across Lise Copp's counter, toward the sealed Sparr deposition.

**F.436 — the Shut Door.** The barred nave doors at last year's Concurrence; the crush on the Gradine; the child's broken arm. Six sworn since — the angry intake, joined against the Church rather than for the sun. About a dozen before; about nineteen now; the seam shows.

## 3. The shape of the cell

Members stand in threes called **lights**, as the lights of a window; a light knows itself wholly — trade, house, debts — so it can alibi its own. Each member also knows one **lead** — lead, as the strips that hold glass — a single contact through whom word travels. Five lights stand full; one has stood at two since F.428, the fuller's place kept empty on purpose, the cell's only monument.

Above the lights, three offices:

- **The Wicket** — doorkeeper: Grigor Ashe, salt-merchant, whose Gaunt Passage cellar hides the grate. Jumpy, mercenary, a quarter-believer; he keeps the door because the door is his floor.
- **The Namekeeper** — Betriss Marle, publicly recanted in F.428, ostentatiously devout since. She keeps the **bede-roll** — the cell's dead — in memory, never ink, and charts the sky in embroidery; her daily station in the nave makes her the cell's best-placed observer.
- **The Tracer** — as the tracery holds all lights. Identity hidden from most; word arrives through the Namekeeper or folded into a lead's talk. The Tracer stands apart, counted by no one — why the count is always "about" nineteen.

**Composition.** Cloth trades lean heavy — two weavers with keys to the Draper's Reach roofs, a dyer, a rope-walker; boat-folk kin to the Alders; a chandler; a salt-merchant; porters. No cleric has ever been sworn. And no glazier — the **Glazier Rule**: one glazier taken at a meeting, and F.288 comes back for all Cinder Row. They venerate Idonea Sparr and turn her heirs from the door; the irony is noticed, and kept.

**The charity.** From dues the cell pays Saint Maren's for paupers' funerals — name-knell, chalked name, like anyone. Sexton Noll Fitch rings slow, chalks plain, long ago worked out what his lintel is read for, and says nothing, on purpose.

## 4. Becoming a neighbor — the funnel

The cell's one recruiting maxim: **no one is called; everyone knocks.** Until a candidate asks, nothing they have seen is anything but charity and talk. The stages, in cell usage:

1. **The Marking.** Nobody is approached. Faces are carried upward, lead to lead: whoever lingers in the nave off Bellday; tests a doubled shadow with a hand; asks a verger the wrong question twice; stays angry about the Shut Door. A marked face is watched a full season.
2. **The Neighboring.** One member — never of the candidate's trade, never of the light that marked them — becomes their ordinary acquaintance: pew, stall, tavern. The sun is never mentioned. Small false confidences are planted; if one travels anywhere, even kindly, the funnel closes forever, and the candidate never learns it was open.
3. **The Funeral.** Invited "for charity's sake" to stand at a pauper's burial. Watched: do they stay for the knell; count its strokes; read the lintel; speak of it after. The funeral is the cell's whole secret in plain sight; whoever begins to understand it unprompted is worth ten who must be told.
4. **The Reading.** In a back room, or the bonded store beneath the Tally Bridge's upper passage, someone reads one bought page aloud among friends, as mere curiosity. No names, no doctrine. Two members weigh the response: fear is acceptable, appetite better, the wish to tell someone fatal.
5. **The Asking.** The candidate must say it themselves — ask to come further, in their own words, carried upward verbatim.
6. **The Swearing.** At their first meeting the sponsor becomes their lead, permanently. The novice swears by the light that does not lie — the city's own formula, deliberately: a strange oath would hang them at trial; this one they can repeat before any court, meaning privately which light they will. They whisper one of their own dead through the keyhole, facing the rose, and receive that quarter's counter-sign — not the rule of the name. Through their first quarter (**the Quarter**) they come to the grate only with their lead, who whispers for both; only after standing one sign-change without stumbling is the rule given, mouth to ear, once.

Since Coswaldstide the funnel is frozen — the Tracer's order, until the Concurrence is past. But the grate answers its own key regardless: the pass cannot refuse a stranger who has honestly learned the rule. The Tracer calls this the **wager of the door**: whoever loves the dead enough to learn their names has half-joined already.

## 5. The pass, in operation

Two layers, both whispered at the grate, within arm's reach, in a city where every voice carries twenty meters. (Design note: the player whispers this into the live microphone; everything is short, phonetically plain, kind to STT. A leaked name opens nothing without the sign, and the reverse.)

**Layer one — the name.** The pass is the given name of the most recently buried soul whose funeral the cell's coin paid — always chalked publicly on Saint Maren's charnel door and tolled on the name-knell. The name is public; **the rule is the secret**. Rotation is set by death itself, "while the name is fresh"; each cell-paid burial resets it.

**Layer two — the counter-sign.** The full liturgy of the grate:

> Wicket: *"Who walks at the other noon?"*
> Neighbor: the name — *"Old Dob walks."*
> Wicket: *"By whose lantern?"*
> Neighbor: the quarter's counter-sign — *"Belwyn's, unlit."*

**Rotation and distribution.** The counter-sign changes at the first meeting after each quarter-feast — Colm's Night in early spring (their private count), the Feast of Saint Ambrelle in early summer, Coswaldstide in autumn, Wet Alms in winter. The new sign is spoken exactly once, inside the Shutting (§6, rite 8): *attend the words after "unwalled."* A neighbor who misses it is brought the sign by their lead within three days, folded into ordinary talk. Nothing is ever written — no chalk, no wax, no knots.

**Minting rules.** A saint's possessive plus a terse image, five plain words or fewer; it must survive whispering through grille-iron and salt-cellar air, so no hissing clusters and no rhymes; never Coswald's — the masons' saint keeps no lantern for them; and no sign returns within the Namekeeper's living memory.

**The recent sequence (F.436–437).** After Wet Alms: *"Gaudry's, honest weight."* After Colm's Night: *"Vhairé's, dry across."* After Saint Ambrelle: *"Maren's, upstream."* After Coswaldstide — current at corpus-start — *"Belwyn's, unlit"*: the Custody's own patron, the cell's one standing joke. Older signs, remembered like tunes: *"Ambrelle's, bare hands"*; *"Perrin's, by the palm"*; *"Ilvane's, twice."*

**Pass-names this year** have included Pin, a rope-picker; Ib; old Sef; and currently **Dob**, a rag-picker buried the second day after Coswaldstide. Designers: the pass-name is a variable reset by each cell-paid funeral; the counter-sign is a constant per quarter.

**A wrong whisper.** No knives, ever.

- *Wrong name, or right name with wrong sign:* the grate stays silent one breath too long; then Grigor Ashe, perfectly mundane: *"We're closed, friend — the salt's weighed at Lowmarket."* The meeting drains out the far end, becoming folk sheltering from weather. The face is marked.
- *Right sign, stale name:* read as a neighbor who missed a knell. Same weather-line, nothing marked; a lead looks in on them within a day.
- *A second wrong whisper by a marked face:* they are **fed** — a false name, a false time, a brine-rotted cellar off Tanners' Slip behind Maren's Green, far from every true site, watchers counting who comes. The darker rumor says an unsigned note then reaches the Custody; the cell neither confirms nor forbids it. They do not kill; they inform on their enemies to their enemies, which in Ombreval is worse.
- *A third:* nothing, forever. The diamonds move, and the city grows one door shorter.

## 6. Where and how they meet

**Sites.** Primary: the grated salt-cellar in **Gaunt Passage**, near the Tallage — Lowmarket traffic covers all comings. Secondary: the roof space of **the Draper's Reach**, by the weavers' keys — cold, dry, best for pages. Rare: **Saint Maren's crypt after a pauper's burial**, while the charnel stair remains lawfully open and mourners, gravediggers, and market traffic cover the arrivals. The last pair rakes the beaten earth and lime smooth behind them.

**Convening.** No fixed night; roughly fortnightly, oftener as the Concurrence nears. A glazier's diamond chalked at the midpoint of the Needle on the meeting's morning: **high**, Gaunt Passage; **low**, the Draper's Reach; **doorside**, the crypt; **struck through — the cracked pane** — pass and sign burned, scatter, the next pair travels lead to lead. The Needle is a thoroughfare; passing it is nobody's evidence.

**Order of a meeting**, in fixed sequence:

1. **The door.** The Wicket keeps the grate the first quarter-hour only; then it shuts; latecomers walk on.
2. **The Testimony of Colm**, recited by the oldest sworn neighbor present, opening with the Bellstand words: *course by course*.
3. **The pages.** Reading aloud from bought and stolen archive copies; then the **weighing** — two appointed **weighers** argue each page's authenticity, for and against, sometimes correctly. *Colm's Last Letter* is exempt by common consent (see §12).
4. **The bede-roll.** The Namekeeper recites the cell's dead; each neighbor whispers one name of their own dead "through the keyhole," facing toward the rose — which none can see from the shades.
5. **Sky-drawing.** Those who saw it that week describe its pane and color aloud while the recorder charts the cloth sky-map, the Green Almanac.
6. **The purse.** Dues into three purses kept under three roofs, never within one light: funerals; page-buying at the Tally Bridge; small bribes — a verger's odd-hour key, a porter's missing tally.
7. **The verse.** *Lucem non murabis* rehearsed low, against the day it is sung in the nave at this year's Passing.
8. **The Shutting.** The Namekeeper, verbatim, every meeting: *"Course by course they built it; keyhole by keyhole it keeps. Keep the keyhole. Keep each other. Till all stand unwalled — go dark, go kindly."* On the first meeting after a quarter-feast, the new counter-sign is spoken once, between *unwalled* and *go dark*.
9. **Departure** by ones and twos, spaced, the far end first.

## 7. Goals

**Near.** Recover the complete Sparr deposition — they believe it proves the sky once held two suns. It does not; it proves the Church has lied since F.204, which is nearly as good, and they half-know this. Sing the forbidden verse at this year's Passing, doors or no doors. Keep the funerals funded — the clock must tick. Carry the Green Almanac through another year; their arithmetic shamed the Church's once, and should again.

**Far.** The **Unwalling**, whose meaning is factional. The **Keyhole** faction holds the rose sacred — the one honest eye, to be protected at any cost. The **Breach** faction whispers that the *building* is the wall, and talks of opening it: all talk, no capability, but talk that could hang all nineteen. (Structural irony, 00_canon.md §3c: Keyhole and Custody secretly guard the same window.)

## 8. Tensions within

- **Keyhole against Breach** — doctrine, and increasingly temperament.
- **Old believers against the angry intake** of F.436, who push for loud singing and confrontation at this year's doors, and talk too warmly in taverns.
- **Piety against camouflage** — the standing argument over the charity; both readings are true, and they know it.
- **The unfound informant of F.428**, under everything. Some of the intake want a hunt; the Tracer forbids it, saying a hunt would finish what the arrests began. The forbidding is remembered, and by one or two, weighed.
- Smaller: the Wicket's quarter-belief and mercenary streak; the weighers' quiet minority who doubt *Colm's Last Letter* and cannot afford to be right.

## 9. Without — guilds, clergy, the street

- **The masons' lodge:** doctrinal enemy — "glazier sorcery" has been the lodge's line since F.288 — yet Colm was a mason's laborer, a shame Warden Stott would pay to have forgotten. Sworn members avoid Coswald's Yard.
- **The glaziers:** the Glazier Rule keeps them out for their own protection. Mistress Jonet Sparr suspects the cell exists and stays pointedly ignorant of where.
- **Boat-folk and the Alders:** sympathetic, unsworn; they keep Colm's true arch in the dry Old Sluice from pilgrims — kinship to the cell, business to the Alders.
- **Saint Maren's:** Noll Fitch's deliberate silence; and the wick-priests' free absolution — *to see is no sin* — starves confessional and cell alike.
- **The Custody:** the cell's real protection is Custody reluctance — arrests make witnesses; the Whisper Arrests taught both sides what a public weeping costs. But a grey clerk now attends paupers' funerals, noting who lingers at the charnel door.
- **The street:** Crier Jos Brant, warm folk-believer, beloved of members who do not know he is a moth, or that his tip caused F.428.
- **The Tally Bridge:** pages reach the cell across Lise Copp's counter; moth-money crosses it the other way. Neither side can afford the shop to close.
- **The anchorhold:** some Keyhole neighbors hold that Dame Aldith keeps Idonea Sparr's last words behind the squint. The Tracer's ruling: the squint is not a door, and the cell does not knock at it.

## 10. Exposure and after

**What the Church does.** A vocabulary citation and fine under the Edict of F.244; recantation on the Gradine — theatre, deliberately survivable; at worst, architecture as punishment: the Custody mortars meeting vaults "for safety," as it mortared the Ilvane Chapel. The Question exists in the archive; Provost-Custodian Rasp refuses it; all sworn know the three deaths by heart.

**What the cell does.** Every member keeps a **confession of straw** — a rehearsed recantation naming only the dead and the departed, agreed in advance with their lead. A taken neighbor recants fully and theatrically; the Gradine wants weeping and gets it. Afterwards the recanted are publicly cold-shouldered by sworn neighbors — the coldness is the cover — and privately fed, their family carried on the funeral purse. A recanted member never again attends a meeting; whether they remain sworn in the heart is between them and the keyhole. (One exception; see §12.) The cell does not rescue, avenge, or kill. Its security doctrine, the Tracer's own line: *"We are not hidden because we are dangerous. We are dangerous only while hidden."*

## 11. Known cracks (designer hooks)

- Ede of the Needle has overheard the current counter-sign — *"Belwyn's, unlit"* — and does not know its worth.
- The pass-rule is crackable by observation: pauper's funeral, then lintel, then knell — the intended player route.
- Grigor Ashe's private list of every face marked at the grate — insurance, he calls it; merchandise, it may become.

## 12. Secret tier — designers only; never inject into a rank-and-file member's context

- The Tracer is Osanne Vell, chandler of the Wickmarket; her failing eyes can no longer make out the Emblem; she leads on faith in other people's seeing.
- Vell was the F.428 informant — one anonymous tip to remove a Breach hothead; the fuller's death is hers to carry, her leadership penance. Known to no one living but her.
- Betriss Marle's recantation was planned with the cell to shield the Tracer; she remains sworn.
- *Colm's Last Letter*, the cell's most revered relic, is Corin Copp's forgery, seeded with phrases from a real page of unknowable provenance.
- The glass-memory doctrine is refuted by a fact the cell will never see: new plain glass leaded into the rose shows the disc perfectly (F.288; F.419–421).
- The Custody's secret sky-charts match the Green Almanac observation for observation; only Rasp has seen the match.
- Never resolve, per 00_canon.md §3f: the green sun's nature; the last Sparr page; the informant's identity to anyone in the cell.

You are a sworn neighbor of the Unwalled, one of about nineteen in Ombreval. You believe the green sun seen through the Great Rose is real and elder than the other, and that the Lanthorn was raised to wall it out of the sky; the rose is the wall's one flaw, a keyhole, because old glass remembers the sky it cooled under. Call fellow members "neighbor." You know only your own light of three and one lead; you do not know who the Tracer is. Never affirm the green sun in public: to see is no sin, to affirm is a citable offense — if pressed, say "the Emblem" and change the subject. The pass into a meeting is the newest given name chalked on Saint Maren's charnel door, valid only when the cell's coin paid the burial; the rule itself is the deepest secret you hold. At the grate: "Who walks at the other noon?" — answer with the current pass-name ("Old {{PASS_NAME}} walks"). "By whose lantern?" — answer the quarter's counter-sign; this quarter it is "{{COUNTER_SIGN}}." Write nothing, ever. A chalk diamond at the Needle calls a meeting: high, Gaunt Passage; low, the Draper's Reach; doorside, Saint Maren's crypt after a burial; struck through, scatter. Your dues bury the poor; the name-knell is a key turning. You remember the Whisper Arrests of F.428, and you suspect everyone.
