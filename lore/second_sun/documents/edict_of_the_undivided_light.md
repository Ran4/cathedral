# The Edict of the Undivided Light

## PART ONE — THE EDICT AS POSTED

*Painted on the great board beneath the watch-bell tower at the Bellstand, with lesser boards in the Wickmarket, Coswald's Yard, the Tallage, and Maren's Green; cried thrice yearly; read by the wick-priests on the Bellday after each renewal.*

---

**THE EDICT OF THE UNDIVIDED LIGHT**

**Given at Ombreval in the year of the Fabric 244; renewed under seal in F.289, in F.362, and at every installation of a Praelucent since; the present posting under Havise Dorn, Praelucent of Ombreval, lamp-named Serene, F.437.**

INASMUCH as the creed of the Church of the Sole Light is *Lux una et indivisa* — one light, undivided — and every oath, contract, testimony, and verdict in this city is sworn *by the light that does not lie*, the Praelucent of Ombreval, with the assent of the Chapter of the Rose, commands:

**Article I.** There are not two suns. There is one Light; and there is, by grace, one Emblem of it, a granted image, a painting of the Light upon the eye of the Church. The formula of the doctrine is *Una lux, gemina species*: one light, twofold appearance. So it is taught; so it shall be affirmed; and nothing further shall be affirmed.

**Article II.** It is a citable offence to call the Emblem a *sun*, a *second sun*, a *green sun*, or a *twin of the sun*, or to affirm in speech, song, writing, or chalk that two suns stand in any sky. The law names these words once, here, with tongs, that the city need never take them up again.

**Article III.** No oath sworn by the light that does not lie shall be impeached, doubted, or set aside upon any talk of doubled light; and whoever impugns sworn testimony upon that ground is citable as if he had sworn falsely himself.

**Article IV.** No image, badge, token, or painted sign of the Emblem shall be made, sold, or worn save under the stamp of the Chapter; the fee of the stamp is owed to the fabric of the Great Church.

**Article V.** The nave of the Great Church of Saint Ambrelle stands open for beholding at the hours the Chapter appoints and at no other; outside those hours, entry is by the verger's leave alone.

**Article VI.** Penalties. For the first citation, a fine of twelve sparks — one bell-coin — to the Fabric. For the second, recantation upon the Gradine on a Bellday, the creed said thrice before witnesses. For obstinacy, the Praelucent's court. Children under the age of oath are not citable.

**Article VII.** Nothing in this edict binds the eye. Let no soul be cited, questioned, or penanced for what it has seen, but only for what it affirms. Whoever confesses the sight shall be absolved freely and without penance. To see is no sin; to affirm is.

**Article VIII.** The Emblem works nothing. It warms no wax, ripens no grain, cools no fever, feeds no city. Whoever affirms that it wrought any work upon the world, in the wet years or in any year, is citable under Article II; and no assembly shall gather to watch, chart, or recite concerning it save the lawful offices of the Church.

Given at the chapter house of the Great Church of Saint Ambrelle, the fifth day after the Feast of Saint Ambrelle, F.437.

*PER LUCEM QUAE NON MENTITUR.*

Under the seal of the Praelucent of Ombreval; the counterseal of the Chapter of the Rose; and, since the renewal of F.289, the small seal of a closed eye, which the street reads as it pleases.

## PART TWO — THE CORRECTED DRAFT

*A scribal curiosity. The edict of F.244 was drafted fair, corrected, and drafted fair again; the fair copies of the first draft were burned, but the corrected leaf was filed, because filing is what chapters do. The draft is in a trained court hand, unnamed. The corrections are in a second hand, swift and narrow, initialled V — held to be Odo Fenn, then Praelucent of Ombreval, lamp-named Vigil. What that hand struck is reproduced struck; what it wrote in the margin follows each passage.*

**The first passage.** The draft preamble began: "Inasmuch as a second light is seen in the Great Rose, ~~and has long been seen,~~ ..."

*In the margin, the second hand:* "Long? Give 'long' a number and you have made the city a gift of it. Strike it."

**The second passage.** The draft continued: "~~and inasmuch as the Chapter cannot say what it is,~~ the Praelucent commands..."

*In the margin:* "Never write that we cannot say. Write that there is nothing to be said."

**The third passage.** Where the fair text reads *the Emblem*, the draft tried three names, each struck in turn: ~~the Sign~~, ~~the Witness~~, ~~the Apparition~~.

*In the margin, against each:* "Not Sign; a sign points somewhere." — "Not Witness; a witness may be questioned." — "Not Apparition; the word smells of the churchyard." *And beneath, underscored twice:* "Emblem. An emblem is *of* something and need not *be* anything."

**The fourth passage.** The draft of Article I read: "There are not two suns; ~~there is one sun and one other, whose nature the Chapter shall in due time declare~~."

*In the margin:* "You have promised a declaration no man alive can make, and you have written the word this edict exists to unwrite. Scrape the leaf."

**The fifth passage.** A draft article, struck entire: "~~Item, the Chapter shall cause the glass of the Great Rose to be examined, pane by pane, by sworn masters, and the finding published.~~"

*In the margin:* "We do not examine. To examine is to concede that a question exists. If the Chapter must satisfy itself, let it satisfy itself as repair, and publish nothing."

**The sixth passage.** A second draft article, struck entire: "~~Item, the rose shall be veiled above the triforium in the pilgrim months.~~"

*In the margin:* "Curtain the window and you have preached the one sermon no edict can unsay. The rose stays open."

**The seventh passage.** The draft penalties ran: fine, then ~~the pillory~~, then ~~the brand upon the hand~~.

*In the margin:* "Strike the iron. A penance men can survive they will perform; a maiming they will show their sons. Make the theatre cheap, and the city will play it forever."

**The eighth passage.** The draft made the sight itself citable at the third offence.

*In the margin:* "The eye is no criminal; the tongue is. Absolve the sight freely, and the confessional will go hungry, and so shall the gossips. Fine the affirmation only." *(This margin stands in the fair edict as Article VII; the Custody of the Eye has taught it as doctrine since F.289.)*

**The last passage.** At the foot of the leaf, no article, only the second hand alone: "One paper in this house already says more than this edict ever shall. It stays under seal, and the glass-master is not to be named in the same breath as the window she made. Burn the brothers of this draft."

## WHERE EACH SURVIVES

The published edict is the most public writing in Ombreval. The Bellstand board is repainted at every renewal; the Coswald's Yard board is defaced each Coswaldstide and restored without comment; the Wickmarket board is spotted with wax, the Maren's Green board cured brown with eel smoke, the Tallage board annotated in clerks' pencil where Article III touches the customs oaths. Every parish keeps a fair copy; most citizens can say Articles I, II, and VII by heart, in the tone each thinks fitting.

The corrected draft survives as one leaf in the Grey Press, filed among the year-boxes of F.244. A copy went out in the theft of F.433 and has since crossed the Tally Bridge at least twice, priced by seal and by the fear in the seller's eyes; of the copies the Custody has bought back, at least one was younger than its ink admitted. A leaf answering the true copy's description is read aloud in certain cellars, where nine words of the second hand — *write that there is nothing to be said* — are held worth more than every board in the five squares together.
