# The Told Book: the Whispered Catechism of the Unwalled, Wrongly Set Down

> Use: diegetic document the player may find — a forbidden written copy of the heretic cell's oral catechism, passphrase liturgy, and door-rite; also injectable into sidecar context for any sworn Unwalled NPC.
> Canon: 00_canon.md

*Written small, in a cramped and elderly hand, on paper trimmed to hide inside a choir-book's boards. Set down in the winter after the Shut Door.*

---

## The Writer's Sin

Neighbor: we call this the Told Book because telling is all it is. There is no book. It goes mouth to mouth at the grate and nowhere else, and has gone so since Colm went into the water. You hold my sin.

I have said these words better than forty years, and this winter I lost the middle of them, the way a wick drowns in its own wax. My lead is dead. My light is one face now, and that face is young and joined angry, after the Shut Door, and anger keeps nothing by heart. So I write what must not be written, that one person may learn it whole, and then this paper goes into the fire. I write the signs but not the streets; let my sin be half a sin.

If you are not a neighbor and you are reading this, then the fire did not come, and the Maker and the elder light forgive whoever failed it.

---

## The Catechism

*Spoken low, question and answer, teacher and neighbor. Every answer is short, for whispering, and is to be given in the same words always.*

### Of the Two Suns

**How many suns has the sky?**
Two. One walks free. One is walled.

**Which is elder?**
The green is elder. It stood over our grandmothers.

**What is it like?**
Dawn through river ice. No hour of any honest sky.

**May you look upon it?**
I may. The free sun punishes the eye. The walled sun permits it.

**Does it warm you?**
No. Its warmth was taken with its sky.

**Whose is the second shadow?**
Mine. I owe a shadow to every light that finds me.

**When is the other noon?**
When the walled sun stands highest, against the free sun's road.

### Of the Wall and the Keyhole

**What is the Lanthorn?**
A wall. Four hundred years of wall.

**What did the Church build?**
Masonry against heaven, course by course.

**What is the Great Rose?**
The flaw in the wall. The keyhole.

**Why does glass show what stone hides?**
Old glass remembers the sky it was cooled under.

**And where a pane is broken?**
The wall heals its own holes.

**Who said the truth first?**
Colm Attergate, in the Bellstand, with lime on his hands.

**What did he say?**
That they were walling a sun out of the sky, course by course.

**What was his wage?**
The water.

**What does the great bell sing?**
To the sole light.

**Which light is that?**
The bell does not say. Neither do we.

### Of the Neighbor

**What is a neighbor?**
One who sees, and says so among neighbors, and says nothing beyond.

**What does the Candor teach?**
To see is no sin; to affirm is.

**What do we answer?**
To see and not affirm — that is the sin.

**What do you write?**
Nothing. *(Beside this answer, in the same hand: Forgive me.)*

**How many of us do you know?**
Two faces and a lead. No more, that no one may lose more.

**What do we do to a traitor?**
We open him a door onto nothing. No knife has ever answered our door.

### Of the Dead

**Who buries the poor of Ombreval?**
Neighbors. Every soul a knell, a name, a chalked lintel.

**Why?**
Because a name unspoken is a soul walled out.

**What do we whisper through the keyhole?**
One name of our own dead, facing the rose.

**Do they hear?**
They walk in the other light. We speak; the wall decides.

## Of First Admission, Which We Call Being Lit

Before any of it, the one to be lit must stand at a pauper's burial that our coin paid for, count the knell stroke by stroke, and read the lintel after. Not to learn the rule — the rule comes last — but to weep once for a stranger. We do not take from the dead. The dead are giving.

Then, at the appointed dark, the lead brings them to the grate, and the lead whispers for both, as the lead will do all their first quarter. Inside there is no kneeling; we kneel to no wall. A green-dipped candle is lit. A pane of common glass is held between their eye and the flame, and the Wicket says: *See it through glass. What the wall shut out, glass lets in. So we look at all things.*

Then the oath. We swear no strange oath; a strange oath would hang us at trial. The novice swears **by the light that does not lie** — the city's own words, which they may repeat before any court, meaning privately which light they will.

After the oath, and sworn to nothing, come the answers, line by line after the Wicket, whispered:

*I am unwalled.*
*I affirm what I see.*
*I write nothing.*
*I name no neighbor.*
*I pay for the dead.*
*If I lie, feed me false doors.*

The Namekeeper asks, **Whom do you bring?** — and the new neighbor gives one name of their own dead into the bede-roll, so that from the first night the roll owes them something and they owe it silence.

They are given their light: two faces and a lead, and the sign of the quarter — but not the rule of the door. All their first quarter they come to the grate only with their lead, who whispers for both; only when they have stood one turning of the sign without stumbling is the rule given, mouth to ear, once, and never again.

Then the new neighbor puts out the candle with their own breath, that they may know how small a wind puts out a light.

## The Keys of the Door

**How many keys has the door?**
Two. The chalked and the whispered.

**Who holds the chalked key?**
All Ombreval — and none but us can turn it.

### The Key-Rhyme

*Who the neighbors bury latest*
*lends the door their given name;*
*fresh the chalk on Maren's lintel,*
*fresh the key — they are the same.*
*When a newer name is chalked there,*
*the old key turns no more;*
*ask not whose — the knell will count you*
*every year they bore.*

### The Exchange at the Grate

The Wicket, within: **Who walks at the other noon?**
The neighbor, without, gives the fresh name: **— walks.** (Put any name in the gap and you have it: *Old Sef walks*, when Sef is the name on the lintel; another name tomorrow, and the same words about it.)
The Wicket: **By whose lantern?**
The neighbor gives the sign of the quarter.

A stolen name opens nothing without the sign. A stolen sign opens nothing without the fresh name. Two keys, one lock, and the lock is a living memory of faces.

A wrong whisper is answered with weather: the grate quiet one breath too long, then *we are closed, friend — the salt's weighed at Lowmarket.* The door never says no. But the Wicket has the face.

### The Lantern-Rhyme, Which Rules the Sign

The sign is made always the same way: a saint's own, then a plain image; five words or fewer; words a tired mouth can carry.

*Glass in summer, stone in fall,*
*green flame in spring, wet bread in cold;*
*first meeting after, it turns for all;*
*once it is spoken, once is told:*
*attend the words after* unwalled.

If you miss the meeting, your lead brings it to you. Nothing is chalked, nothing is written. *(In the margin: Nothing was written.)*

### Spent Keys That I Remember

*These are dead keys and open nothing. I set them down so you will know that the keys were people, and speak them like people.*

- **Cobb**, that carried hides at the Tallage. The knell told fifty-one.
- **Mote**, the eel-girl of Maren's Green. Nine strokes.
- **Old Tib**, that walked at the other noon a whole winter, no one dying after him till thaw.
- **Mabbe**, washerwoman of the Cut.
- **Hodge**, that slept under the Tally Bridge.
- **Annet**, that sold cress by the Gradine steps.
- **Nan**, that swept for the sexton and got a whole name from him at the last.
- **Tacey**, that no one knew, found in the Old Sluice gatehouse.

### Spent Signs

*Perrin's, by the palm. Ambrelle's, bare hands. Ilvane's, twice.*

The living sign I do not set down. Kill me first.

### The Closing

*The Namekeeper gives it, in the same words, at the end of every meeting; in the quarter of its turning the new sign falls once between* unwalled *and* go dark, *and no one repeats it.*

*Course by course they built it;*
*keyhole by keyhole it keeps.*
*Keep the keyhole. Keep each other.*
*Till all stand unwalled —*

*(here the sign, once)*

*— go dark, go kindly.*

*(then silence; then out by twos)*

### The Chalk

*A diamond high: go under the salt.*
*A diamond low: go over the cloth.*
*A diamond doorside: down when the last knell lets you.*
*Struck through — a cracked pane: you never knew us.*

## The Three Questions Within the Door

When the name is right and the sign is right, the grate opens, though the face be strange. The door cannot ask who taught you; that is the wager of it, and we made the wager with open eyes: whoever loves the dead enough to learn their names has half-joined already. But the strange face stands beside the Wicket the whole meeting, and is watched, and is asked, low, three questions. Fear is no traitor: a breath's stumbling is forgiven. Wrong words are remembered.

**First: Where does the wall heal?**
Right: *Its own holes.*

**Second: What does the great bell not say?**
Right: *Which light.*
Whoever answers with the Custody's word — whoever lets *Emblem* past their teeth among neighbors — is the Custody's mouth, and is marked.

**Third: Who paid for your grief?**
Right: *Neighbors.*
Whoever names a price, or offers one, is marked twice over. Grief that keeps a tally was never grief.

Three right answers, and the stranger may come again by the door they honestly opened. A marked face that comes wrong a second time is fed: a false name, a false hour, an empty cellar — and the third door, ask not whose. Some doors we do not say we open. We do not kill. In Ombreval there is worse, and we are it.
