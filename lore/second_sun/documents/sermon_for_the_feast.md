# A Sermon for the Feast of the Sole Light

> Use: Diegetic document the player may find (a stolen Grey Press leaf); also prompt fodder for clergy, Custody, or Unwalled NPCs, who may quote either the pulpit voice or the margins.
> Canon: 00_canon.md

*Cover-slip of the Grey Press, in grey ink, pinned to the leaf at filing:* Preached in the Great Church of Saint Ambrelle, called the Lanthorn, at the Feast of the Sole Light in the four hundred and thirty-sixth year of the Fabric, by Havise Dorn, Praelucent of Ombreval, lamp-named Serene. Taken in church by ear and set fair for the chapter book by Ansel Tarn, deacon. The margins are in the fair hand, smaller and later; this office gives them in their places, marked thus: [margin]. — *S.R.*

---

Children of the Light: before there was a wall in the world, there was light.

Before the Serle found its bed, before stone learned to sit on stone, before any tongue could swear or forswear, the Light was — and of all things made, it alone never disobeyed. Rivers flood; stone cracks; bells go out of true; the tongue we shall come to. But light has kept its first commandment from the first morning to this one: to be one, and to be seen. *Lux una et indivisa.* Hold it like bread, for it is bread, and this feast is the eating of it.

> *[margin]* Two hundred years in the window and never once different. Constancy is her whole proof. It is also its.

Every honest thing in this city leans on those words. The tollman at the Tallage swears the weight by the light that does not lie; so swears the witness, the bride, the boatman striking hands at Maren's Green. Take that oath away and Ombreval is not a city but a crowd with walls round it. A city is not held up by its walls, whatever the masons tell you. It is held up by its oaths.

And the Light is generous with its image: each parish a wick of the one flame, and the flame not divided by the wicks that bear it. No one whispers about candles in a covered lane.

So — I know why the nave was full at dawn. Not for my preaching: for the west window, for the appearance that has stood in the Great Rose since your mothers carried you. See how carefully I speak of it. Learn to hear as carefully.

> *[margin]* They come because it is the one faithful thing in Ombreval. Not even bread is so constant.

Now hear the Church's mercy plain: to see is no sin. The eye is a door the Light itself made, and what comes in unbidden at that door accuses nobody. Your children have seen it. Your dead saw it. I — your Praelucent does not preach to the blind. There is absolution for the eye, freely, without penance. To see is no sin; to affirm is. What you have seen, bring to the Light's confessors — not to the lane, not to the grate.

> *[margin]* She stopped herself. I stood six feet below; she folded the word away like a letter into a sleeve. She has seen it. She is not blind either.

> *[margin]* To affirm is sin. Then what is it to write?

What shows, then, in the eye of the Rose? *Una lux, gemina species*: one light, twofold appearance. What stands in the glass is the Emblem — a granted image, a painting of the Light upon the eye of the Church. You see a moon in the evening pool and do not cry to the watch that heaven keeps two moons. A portrait is not a second person; a seal pressed in wax is not a second king. The image proves no rival but a favour: of all cities under the one sun, it pleased the Light to leave its likeness here, upon our glass. Ombreval, named for shadow, keeps the portrait of the Light.

> *[margin]* A seal takes the colour of its wax. This takes none — red pane, blue pane, the same green-white, and every other light obeys the glass. Her seal argues against her.

> *[margin]* A pool doubles the moon too. Nothing doubles moon, star, torch, or face. Only that, only there — and it walks the lights against the sun's own course. What portrait moves against its original?

And bless the hands that made the window. Saint Ambrelle carried burning glass in her bare hands, and under her name your fathers raised this church. The glass does nothing but receive; it is a servant, as wax is, as parchment is. There is no art of man in what you see, and no guilt in Cinder Row. Leave the makers in peace.

> *[margin]* Idonea Sparr wept at her finished rose, said she had made two of something there was one of, and never fired glass again. The maker did not think the glass does nothing.

> *[margin]* In the year 421 I stood the Re-leading scaffold as a Taper. Where a pane was lifted out I set new glass with my own hands, plain glass a week from the kiln. The disc stood in it before the putty had skinned. I signed their paper that I had seen nothing. Now I have written this. One of the two is perjury; let the light that does not lie say which.

Now the warning. Light cannot be double; words can. Man made words in his own image, and a word can face two ways at once; a name can be a little wall with a lie living behind it. So mind your names. The thing in the window has one name, the Church's name: the Emblem. The name of sun — hear how I must say the word to forbid it — belongs to one lamp only. He who tells you in a lane that there are two has added nothing to heaven; he has subtracted an oath from Ombreval — his own first, then yours — until nothing in this city weighs true. If a man would persuade you there are two lights, ask him: which one heard your oath? Which one will keep it?

> *[margin]* "I must say the word to forbid it." Every feast, the Edict borrows the tongue it cut out.

> *[margin]* Which one heard your oath. Both, perhaps. There is the true sermon, and no pulpit will hold it.

And there are some — few, pitied, prayed for whether they believe it or not — who say this house is a wall, raised through four hundred years to shut something out of the sky. Look up. Does a jailer cut his wall a window forty feet across, glaze it, and set it where the whole city may look through, any hour the doors stand open? You do not build a dungeon and call it the Lanthorn. This church is raised as a hand is raised when it holds a lamp.

> *[margin]* Any hour the doors stand open. Remember she said this.

> *[margin]* Attergate drowned at the Serle sluice, coming back. Banished men do not swim toward a city they hate. Say that from a pulpit once.

And you who are no heretics — mothers, ringers, boatmen — mind your customs too. I know what is done in this nave when the vergers are patient: names spoken into the light; a fevered child carried into a certain beam; a bride stepping wide of a certain shadow; games whose rules no one living taught the children. I say only: the Light is not a well to be shouted down. Your dead need no coloured doorway. They are with the Light — with it, not behind some pane of it. Pray as the Church prays; it has always been enough.

> *[margin]* I spoke my mother's name into it the winter she died. I will not unsay it. Set down the fine with the rest.

> *[margin]* Count both your shadows wherever you go. The children keep a catechism of their own; it asks no one's leave.

You will say — and are not fined for saying it — that wax will not soften in that beam; that it is a cold light. Good. A figure should not compete with what it figures. The fire is one, and elsewhere, and coming; the window holds the promise, not the flame. Do not warm yourselves at a portrait.

> *[margin]* Cold as a key; I have carried tapers through it since boyhood. She turns even the cold to doctrine. I no longer know whether I watch faith or masonry.

This afternoon comes the crown of the feast. The appearance will stand in the eye of the Rose, the true sun will come round into the west to stand with it, and for a handful of minutes every doubled shadow on this floor will gather itself and be made one. The Church does not fear your eyes today of all days: look. It is the whole creed acted out on paving stones — whatever seems divided is gathered; whatever seems second is taken up into the first. Pray with me for a clear sky. And if the press at the doors be great — it will be great — be patient with the vergers.

> *[margin]* There is no office for a clouded Concurrence; I have searched the books of the Rite. If cloud comes, the disc stands alone in the eye with nothing to be made one with — and that is the other sermon.

> *[margin]* "Be patient with the vergers." She knew what the Chapter had in mind, or feared it. Mark this line.

Go, then. Swear this year as your grandmothers swore, by the light that does not lie, and let no lane, no grate, no clever grief unswear you. Whatever stands in the window, the Light stands where it has always stood. One. Undivided. Yours. *Lux una et indivisa.*

*Here ends the sermon.*

> *[margin]* Nineteen times she said "one". I counted on my knuckles, like a Taper at his offices. A strange number of ones.

*Beneath the last line the small hand fills the foot of the leaf:*

> *[margin]* Within the hour the Chapter barred the nave doors, with the Gradine full and the Passing coming on — the first time in living memory. Over the singing I heard the crying at the west doors, and a child's cry in it. Within, the shadows were made one, exactly as she promised; and the doors were shut exactly as the heretics say the sky was. She preached one light; the Chapter preached with a doorbar. *[In a third hand, grey ink, at the foot:]* The leaf is trimmed by a binder's knife; the small hand's last line runs off the cut. Razored from the chapter book of the Lanthorn some weeks after the feast, none confessing; bought back across the Tally Bridge in the spring of F.437 for a sum in dry money, the seller not named to this office. Fair hand and margin hand are one: Ansel Tarn, deacon, gone from the rolls since that spring — to Ostrelle under escort, or over the wall by night, the rolls do not say. Verdict: genuine. Value: citable, every margin. Filed in the Grey Press under the Edict of F.244. To be kept, not shown. — S.R.
