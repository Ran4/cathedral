# The Sparr Deposition, as the Leaves Are Sold

> Use: diegetic document the player may find — an illegal circulating copy of the sealed F.204 deposition; its answers are street-famous and quotable, and excerpts may be injected into heretic, Custody, antiquary, or glazier NPC context.
> Canon: 00_canon.md

## The copyist to the reader

What you hold is a Bridge copy — the first whole bundle was sold across the Tally Bridge — of the examination of Mistress Idonea Sparr, master glazier of Cinder Row, taken before the Chapter in the two hundred and fourth year of the Fabric and sealed the same day: the oldest paper in the Grey Press. No one outside the Custody read a word of it for two hundred and twenty-nine years. Then, in the year 433, a Custody scribe died, or fled, and a satchel of the Custody's own fair copies went into the city; every leaf since sold, bought back, or copied by candle descends from it. The original has never left the Grey Press.

Understand three things. A Sparr page is the costliest contraband in Ombreval; this bundle is nine of them, and one more I will speak of — burn it whole or not at all, for a single leaf sold back sells the seller's face with it. The clerk wrote *the second sun* with a clean conscience, the Edict being forty years unborn; it will not cover yours. And there is a hand in this city — the trade says **the Second Hand**, knowing no better name — that makes young paper speak old words. I have copied what I trust and marked what I do not. Some of these answers you know already; they walk the taverns without their paper.

## The deposition

*[The first leaf]*

In the two hundred and fourth year of the Fabric, the fourth day after the Feast of Saint Ambrelle, in the chapter house, before the Praelucent Osric Harn, lamp-named Vigil, and Warin Grell, Provost of the Rite, and Bertram Chace, Provost of the Fabric, was examined Idonea Sparr of Cinder Row, master glazier, sworn by the light that does not lie; her answers set down by me, Aymer Pett, clerk.

Item, asked her name and trade. She saith: Idonea Sparr, glazier, as my mother was.

Item, asked what went into the glass of the great west window, and whether the ash of the fire of one-and-seventy were in the melt. She saith: Serle sand from the reed-shoals below the walls; beech ash, fern ash for the greens; the metals the craft appoints — nothing that has not gone into glass since glass began. And that fire's ash is in everything on Cinder Row — the glass, the gardens, the bread. It did not change the bread.

*[The second leaf]*

Item, asked of the work. She saith: the Chapter appointed me in the year 198; the work was five years. I drew every figure full on whited tables, and the Provost of the Fabric passed each before the fire — the obedience of the Light, and in the round we of the craft call the eye, the Sole Light in glory.

Item, asked whether she wrought in any pane a second sun, or any figure that might be taken for one. She saith: none. There is one sun in my cartoons, and I know it, for I drew it. Let the tables be fetched and counted. [The clerk notes: they were, and one sun found.]

*[The third leaf]*

Item, asked of the day the work was finished, and what she saw. She saith: on the eve of Saint Ambrelle, last year, we set the last pane at first light, the morning being clear. I came down and walked to the crossing, to see my window whole, as a master does once. The window was true; every lead lay where I had drawn it — five years of leads. And low in the west lights, great, the bigness of a cartwheel, there stood a sun where I had put no sun; and the sun of heaven stood at my back, over the quire, where at that hour it ought.

Item, asked how she knew it for no reflection, nor trick of new glass. She saith: it stood behind a red pane, and the pane did not redden it. Fathers, all my life light has done what glass tells it; red glass makes a red sun. This keeps its own colour through every pane it stands behind — green-white, like dawn through river ice — and no pane tells it anything.

Item, asked whether she looked long on it. She saith: the length of a prayer. It does not punish the eye. The sun of heaven will not be looked at; this one suffers it. Asked whether that comforted her: no. That was when I wept.

*[The fourth leaf]*

Item, it being put to her, as the Fabric's chronicle has it, that she said that day she had made two of something there was one of, asked whether the words were hers, and what she meant by them. She saith: they were, and are; and I meant what a weaver would mean, that found in her finished cloth a figure she had not woven. One sun drawn, one sun fired, one sun leaded; the window gives back two. What a craftsman makes past his count he has made without meaning it, and cannot unmake, for he does not know which of his motions made it. That is what I meant, and why I wept.

Item, asked whether she meant that she had made the second sun. She saith: I mean that I never saw it before my window was whole, and that it is nowhere my window is not. Whether that is making, you fathers must judge. I have no other word I dare use.

[The clerk has written: the examination was stayed at the third hour, the fathers going apart into the nave, and resumed at the ninth. The Praelucent did not sit again; the Provost of the Rite presided.]

*[The fifth leaf]*

Item, asked what she had observed in the year past. She saith: it rises when the day rises and goes when the light goes, and it goes against the sun — widdershins to heaven; once a day they meet in the west and pass, and neither gives way.

Item, asked in what manner it differs from the sun of heaven, besides colour and course. She saith: it stands still for every eye. The sun of heaven walks pane to pane beside you across the nave; this keeps one pane — the same for me at the font and the sexton at the altar — as a painted figure does. But painted glass borrows its shining and casts no beam. This casts its own, cold — wax set in it a summer day keeps its corners — and aslant the true; where the two lie together they lie apart, like oil and water in one cup, and a body standing in both has two shadows, the second faint, cold, and aimed wrong.

*[The sixth leaf]*

Item, asked whether she held her oath good, sworn by the light that does not lie. She saith: I swore it and I hold it. Asked whether, having seen what she deposes, she doubted the form of it: I have never seen light lie. I have seen it be two. Admonished that this was perilous speech, and asked whether she would amend it: I will amend it when the window does.

Item, asked who beside herself had seen it. She saith: whoever has stood in the nave with open eyes on a bright day. Asked their names: Cinder Row is a name; take it.

Item, admonished on her obedience to speak of the matter to no one. She saith: you may seal my mouth, and this paper with it. The window is forty feet wide.

*[The seventh leaf]*

Item, asked whether she would return to her furnace. She saith: no. Asked why: a master answers for all her window shows. I cannot answer for mine.

Item, asked what she believed the thing to be. She saith: I can tell you what it is not. It is not in the glass as colour is in glass, for I put the colours in, and I did not put it. It is not in the sky as the sun is in the sky, for the sky from the roofs is empty of it. Do not ask what I let in, for I do not know that I let in anything, and I do not know that I did not.

*[The eighth leaf]*

Item, asked whether she desired anything of the Chapter. She saith: that you leave the window whole. Whatever else it is, it is five years of my life, and true work, and true work is not the world's most common thing.

## The unnumbered leaf

*[Set between the eighth leaf and the ninth in many bundles; of disputed authority. I give it as I found it.]*

Item, asked whence the second light comes. She saith: out of the glass's remembering. Glass keeps the sky it cooled under, as the eye keeps a flame it has looked on. We annealed the great lights under open heaven, and what heaven held above them then, the window holds now. Asked what heaven held then. She saith: what the window holds. Two.

*[Concerning this leaf. No one quarrels over the paper; every leaf in the city is young. The quarrel is whether the words are old. Against it: the true leaves are numbered in Pett's hand, this one in no hand; and* glass keeps the sky it cooled under *is the very catechism of the Colmites, preached by Colm Attergate ninety-seven years after this ink was supposed dry. For it: its lovers say the leaf lent the doctrine its words, read in secret long before Colm; there is no answering that, and no proving it. The Custody pays more for this leaf than for any numbered one — proof it is true, say its lovers; proof, say its doubters, that the Custody pays dearest for whatever it fears is loose. And the Second Hand has never been caught; so every bundle is his until proven otherwise. Including, reader, this one.]*

## The ninth leaf

Item, the deposition being read back to her, asked whether she would set her hand to it. She saith: to every leaf. [She signed each leaf, and cut her mark beside: a small diamond, such as glaziers make.]

Item, it being now near dark, the fathers withdrew to confer upon what should be asked further, or whether aught should. She was bidden wait in the nave, where the light was going.

*[Here the ninth leaf ends, filled to its last rule, and here ends every bundle I trust. Whether the fathers came back with one question more, and a tenth leaf holds her answer, or asked nothing more, and there was never more paper than this — no copy tells, and the Grey Press, which alone could tell, buys back even the asking. She waited in the nave. That is the last thing anyone wrote down.]*
