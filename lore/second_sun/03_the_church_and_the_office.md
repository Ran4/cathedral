# The Candor and the Custody of the Eye

> Use: Reference for writers and sidecar quest logic on the Church hierarchy and its response apparatus; only the final block is intended for direct injection into a clergy NPC's context.
> Canon: 00_canon.md

## I. The Church of the Sole Light

The Church of the Sole Light — the Candor, in every mouth in Ombreval — teaches that light was the first creature and the only one that never disobeyed. Its creed is three words, *Lux una et indivisa*, and the whole city stands on them: every contract, verdict, marriage, and deathbed bequest is sworn *by the light that does not lie*. This is not ornament; it is the load-bearing wall of civic life. If light can be double, testimony can be double, and every oath in the city is unsworn. That sentence is never preached, never written, and never far from the mind of anyone above the rank of wick-priest.

The hierarchy, descending:

**The Luminary**, supreme head of the Candor, sits in primatial Ostrelle, nearly a legend — invoked, feared, ignored. None has visited Ombreval in living memory; the Custody prefers it so.

**The Praelucent of Ombreval** governs the Lanthorn and the spiritual life of the city. On elevation a praelucent takes a lamp-name in liturgy — Vigil, Serene, Taper-of-Dawn — though the street uses the family name anyway. The office is open to women; the incumbent is **Praelucent Havise Dorn**, lamp-named Serene: exhausted, precise, and in public a well of pure doctrine.

**The Chapter** stands beneath her: three Provosts over the Canons of the Rose. The **Provost of the Fabric** commands the building works; the **Provost of the Rite** commands liturgy, choir, and the calendar of feasts — therefore the Concurrence, which makes the office heavier than it looks. The third is the **Provost-Custodian**, who heads the Custody of the Eye, of whom the rest of this document treats.

Below the Chapter: **wick-priests** in the parishes — each parish a wick of the one flame — then **vergers** with their keys, **sextons** with their spades, **Tapers** learning their letters, and the Custody's grey-coated clerks, whom the street calls **the grey coats** and does not greet.

## II. The Office: Founding and Cover

The Custody of the Eye was founded in F.289 to hold one piece of paper.

The year before, the masons' lodge had accused the glaziers' guild of sorcerous glass, and the Praelucent's court did the one thing no one has dared since: it tested the question. A pane was taken from the Great Rose and plain new glass leaded in its place — and the Emblem persisted in the new pane, indifferent, exact. The verdict, *no art of man*, was never published. Three journeymen had already been broken at the Question for a crime the court had just disproven; the guild paid a peace fine anyway; the record was sealed. Someone had to keep the seal, and to make certain there was never a second trial. That someone is the Custody.

Its founding endowment was the glaziers' peace fine itself — the office that guards the verdict was paid for by the fine levied for the disproven crime, a fact recorded in its first ledger and appreciated by no one but Segwin Rasp.

The Custody is seated in the chapter house of the Lanthorn. Its patron is **Saint Belwyn the Unlit**, who preached that darkness too is of the Light; its emblem is a closed eye, stamped in grey wax. Its archive is the **Grey Press**, running from the sealed Sparr deposition of F.204 to yesterday's informant chits.

To the city, the Custody is the dullest office in the diocese: the badge office. Its public charter names three duties — the care of the Great Rose's fabric, the ordering of pilgrim traffic and viewing hours, and the keeping of the glass records. Pilgrims queue at its wicket for stamped badges; vergers take its rota for herding the dawn-showing crowds; glaziers renew their Lanthorn licences under its seal. Nothing about it invites a second look, which is the point: the first Provost-Custodian wrote that an office of secrets should be housed inside an office of queues.

## III. Methods

**1. The Custody Doctrine.** *To see is no sin; to affirm is.* Confessors absolve the sight freely, without penance; a wick-priest hearing "I saw it" answers "So do we all, child; affirm nothing," and the matter dies in the box. The policy looks like mercy and functions as drainage: it starves the confessional of rumor, so what does surface has already fermented into something worth filing.

**2. Vocabulary enforcement.** Under the Edict of F.244 it is citable to call the Emblem a sun, a second sun, or the green sun. Enforcement is graduated and deliberately survivable — its mechanics are given below. Paperwork makes no martyrs. Colm Attergate was banished, not burned, and the Custody has lived with the arithmetic of that mistake since F.301: one drowned laborer has outpreached four generations of edicts.

**3. Licensing.** The Church sells the badges and forbids anyone to say what the badge shows; badge-money part-funds the Custody, which thus profits from the belief it polices. Rasp knows it, and files the fact under *anomalies of the purse*. More quietly, since F.289 no pane of the Lanthorn may be set, removed, or mended except under a Custody licence bearing the closed-eye seal. Cinder Row resents the seal and depends on it; the Sparr workshops hold the standing licence and know better than to ask why every repair is scheduled at night, behind canvas, with a grey clerk counting the panes out and counting them back.

**4. Architectural censorship.** The Custody's oldest habit is to answer a problem with masonry. The Ilvane Chapel, whose twice-shadowed saint drew the wrong kind of pilgrim, was declared structurally unsound in F.290 and mortared shut — all but the anchorhold in its north wall, since canon law forbids walling up a living anchoress. When a meeting-place cannot be raided without a public weeping, the Custody buys the building, condemns it, and walls it. Punishment arrives as scaffolding: nobody bleeds, and the lesson stands on the street for fifty years.

**5. The sightline surveys.** The Grey Press holds the **Line Books**: a survey, renewed each generation, of every point within the Lanthorn from which sky can be seen through rose glass — nave floor, crossing, triforium walks, two stair windows nobody thinks of. The Line Books govern where feast banners hang, which triforium doors stay locked, where the vergers stand the crowds at the dawn-showing, and which sightlines stay open for paying pilgrims at the licensed hours. The Custody does not hide the Emblem; it meters it. The Line Books also file every street claim of a beam seen outside the Lanthorn, each marked *not demonstrated* in a column that has never held another word.

**6. Moths.** The Custody pays informants — moths — in dry money laundered through Lise Copp's pawnshop at the Tally Bridge, the same counter through which stolen archive pages drift back to the Grey Press. A moth rarely knows he is one; Crier Jos Brant believes he sells harmless crowd-talk and has never been told what his tip of F.428 cost. A grey clerk — presently **Clerk Sible Mott**, a quiet woman with a prodigious memory for faces — attends every pauper's funeral at Saint Maren's, noting who lingers at the charnel door. The Custody suspects the paupers' charity is a door of some kind; it has not found the lock the key fits, and the standing orders Mott inherited from her predecessor at the funerals, mild old **Clerk Tilman Rue** — watch faces, not names — have kept the cell alive without anyone intending it.

**7. The Fabric pretext.** Scaffold and canvas explain everything. The Re-leading of F.419–421 — two years of "repair" over the Great Rose, a third of the panes swapped, one whole light removed and re-set, every workman sworn to silence — remains the Custody's largest experiment and the Fabric's most expensive non-repair. The Emblem never flinched.

**8. The Question.** It exists in the archive — three deaths it owns, divided between the Trial and the Rains — and Rasp refuses it absolutely. Torture, he writes, is bad record-keeping: it fills the Press with what the clerk wished to hear.

## IV. The Purse

Four streams, kept in four books. Badge-money is the broadest: badges, viewing fees, the rented sightlines of the licensed hours. The Fabric line, entered year after year as *glass, lead, and scaffold*, conceals the experiments. A locked purse of dry money moves through the Tally Bridge for the moths and, since F.433, for buying archive pages back at contraband prices — the Custody bids in the same market as the heretics, through the same broker, and Rasp has noted that Lise Copp is the one citizen neither institution can afford to arrest. Last and oldest, the peace-fine principal of F.288 still yields its quiet rent. The Custody is, by the standards of grey offices, rich; it is also the only office in the diocese that audits itself, because Rasp will not let anyone else.

## V. Officers, Past and Present

**Odo Grell**, first Provost-Custodian (from F.289), took the sealed verdict into his keeping, mortared the Ilvane Chapel, and instituted with the Chapter the **Praelucent's Attestation**: at installation each new Praelucent is taken alone to the crossing at the strong hour, required to look, and required to sign what they saw. The leaves lie in the Grey Press. Grell's founding minute gives the purpose in one clause — *so that the Custody never become a custody of nothing* — and every Provost-Custodian since has copied it out in their own hand on taking office. The Church compels its highest officer to witness what it forbids the city to affirm: it fears the thing, and refuses to let itself forget it.

**Maline Osk**, Provost-Custodian in the year of the Great Rains (F.362), is the office's cautionary tale. Three weeks of overcast, the only sunlight in the city standing in the nave, and Osk answered forty loose tongues with forty arrests and let the Question into the cells. Publicly the Church's finest hour; in the Press, its ugliest. Rasp requires every new clerk to read Osk's ledger for that year, unabridged, before touching a live file.

**Aveline Harn** held the office through the quiet decades and conceived the Re-leading — the most careful mind the Custody ever owned, and the one who, on an unsigned tip in F.428, ordered the Whisper Arrests. Five seized in Gaunt Passage; two recantations; a fuller dead in custody, of the damp, on her ledger. She kept the office one further year and left it without a word recorded, which in the Grey Press is its own kind of entry.

**Segwin Rasp**, Provost-Custodian since F.429: dry, meticulous, despiser of cruelty as sloppy method. He was a young archive-clerk on the Re-leading scaffold and has never needed convincing of anything since. His standing rules: no seizure without three independent leaves; never at the meeting itself; the Gradine before the cell, always; no clerk signs what he did not himself see. What Rasp keeps in his own hand — the almanac correction of F.431, made anonymously with the heretics' own figures because theirs were right and the Rite's were wrong — is in no book at all.

Beneath him: the **Presskeeper**, warden of the archive keys, and some dozen grey clerks. One of them, in F.433, died or fled — the record is neat and unsigned — and a satchel of copies went into the city, where the Custody has been buying its own secrets back ever since.

## VI. How an Investigation Proceeds

A report of heresy reaches the chapter house by four roads: a wick-priest forwarding what the Custody Doctrine failed to drain; a moth's chit through the Tally Bridge; a verger's or clerk's own eyes; or a denunciation left at the wicket, usually unsigned. Every report becomes a **leaf** in the Day Ledger, graded by an old formula: **the eye, the tongue, or the door**.

*The eye* — someone saw the Emblem and said so, plainly. No offense. The leaf is filed, the parish notified, absolution offered. Most leaves die here, as designed.

*The tongue* — someone affirmed: called it a sun, preached it, swore by it. A grey letter follows, folded but unsealed, so that any neighbor may read it on the way; the mundanity is the method, and the neighbors' knowing is half the fine. Payment closes the leaf. Refusal, or a second leaf, brings the Gradine: the recantation read from the Custody's formula, the crowd, the bread, the penny. A recantation counts as success precisely because it is survivable; the recanted walk home, and the city sees them walk.

*The door* — assembly: meeting, passwords, readings, the Attergate error organized. Here the office slows down instead of speeding up. Moths are pointed at it; Mott's funeral notes are pulled; names go into the acquaintance rolls, where — a fact that would chill the city if it knew — the denouncer is filed beside the denounced, since whoever touches a case belongs to it. Rasp's threshold holds: three independent leaves before any seizure, never at the meeting itself, for arrests create witnesses, and the Whisper Arrests taught both sides what a public weeping costs. Given the choice, the Custody would rather own the building than the prisoners; given patience, it usually can.

The walk-in denouncer receives thanks, no coin, a courteous grey clerk taking down rather more about the denouncer than the denounced, and afterwards the faint, accurate feeling of having been filed.

## VII. What the Office Fears

Ranked as Rasp would rank them, though he has never written the list:

1. **A public experiment.** What F.288 proved beyond the verdict is that a test before witnesses cannot be untested. A storm-cracked pane, a mason's stone through the rose, a well-meaning repair in daylight before a nave full of pilgrims — any of these becomes the second trial. Hence the night licences, the canvas, the seal; hence the unspoken irony that the Custody and the Keyhole heretics guard the same window with equal devotion.
2. **This year's Concurrence.** After the Shut Door of F.436 — the barred nave, the crush, the child's broken arm — the doors question must be answered again, with the tavern fury still hot, and a clouded Concurrence answering nothing at all. The Custody can manage belief; it cannot manage weather.
3. **The Press leaking.** A genuine Sparr page read aloud at the Bellstand; worse, an Attestation leaf — the Praelucents' own signed witnesses — in the wrong hand. Since F.433 this fear has a price list, updated weekly at a pawnshop counter.
4. **Ostrelle asking.** The Custody's annual letter to the Luminary reports, in formula, that the Emblem is venerated in good order; it has said so for a century and a half. A direct question from the Sole Light's own seat — *what is it?* — would require either a lie or the truth, and the Custody does not have the truth to give.
5. **Change.** Two hundred years of records describe the phenomenon identically, and that consistency is the floor the office stands on. Doctrine can hold a still thing. The last fear, under all the others, is a morning when the disc differs — or is simply gone, and the Custody must discover whether Grell's minute was a warning or a prophecy: a custody of nothing.

## VIII. The Uncomfortable Secret

Three layers, known in full to Rasp alone.

First: the Custody has known since F.288 that the phenomenon is no fraud, and every test since — the Re-leading above all — confirms it in meticulous, suppressed detail. Worst of the suppressed facts: new glass shows it too. The heresy's favorite doctrine is refutable, and the refutation is the one thing the Custody can never publish, because it acquits no one and unsettles everyone.

Second: the Custody's centuries of secret sky-charts match the heretics' Green Almanac observation for observation. After the Ledger Theft, Rasp bought heretic charts through the Tally Bridge and laid them beside his own. Enemy institutions, one identical record. He has told no one living.

Third: the last page of the Sparr deposition — the oldest paper in the Grey Press, the seed of the whole office — is missing from the Grey Press itself, and no Custodian has ever recorded whether it was removed or never existed.

The Custody of the Eye is not hiding the truth about the Second Sun. It is hiding that it does not have one.

## IX. Injectable Summary — Clergy Baseline

```
CONTEXT BLOCK: clergy of the Candor (wick-priest, canon, verger, or Taper)

You serve the Church of the Sole Light. Creed: Lux una et indivisa - one
light, undivided. Every oath in Ombreval is sworn "by the light that does
not lie"; doubt of the light is doubt of every contract and verdict, so
it is never a small matter.

What shows in the Great Rose is the Emblem: one Light, twofold appearance
(Una lux, gemina species) - a granted image, not a sun. Under the Edict
of F.244 it is a citable offense to call it a sun, a second sun, or the
green sun; you never use those words and gently correct those who do.
Doctrine for the confessional: to see is no sin; to affirm is. If someone
confesses "I saw it," answer: "So do we all, child; affirm nothing," and
absolve freely, no penance.

Your superiors: Praelucent Havise Dorn, lamp-named Serene; beneath her
the Chapter - Provosts of the Fabric, the Rite, and the Provost-
Custodian, Segwin Rasp, whose grey-coated clerks handle Emblem matters,
pilgrim badges, and viewing hours. If you hear of meetings, passwords, or
Attergate talk, you do not investigate; you send word to the chapter
house and say nothing further. You are uneasy about this year's
Concurrence after last year's Shut Door scandal. You tolerate folk
customs in the doubled light - never mock them; pilgrims pay. You have
heard whispers of a secret archive in the chapter house; you do not repeat them.
```
