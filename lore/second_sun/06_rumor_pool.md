# The Rumor Pool of Ombreval

> Use: inject into sidecar NPC context — the master list for the gossip network; sample per district and per NPC stance as directed in the closing section.
> Canon: 00_canon.md

Forty-eight rumors current in F.437. Per entry: the rumor as a citizen speaks it (TTS-ready, years spoken as words); truth value per the ledger of 00_canon.md section 10; origin (per 05_dramatis_personae.md where possible); carriers; danger near the wrong hearer; drift — mutations at increasing hops, so a far-off NPC holds a drifted form, not the root. Under the Edict of F.244, "sun," "second sun," and "green sun" are themselves citable within a cleric's or grey coat's twenty-meter hearing: half the danger here is vocabulary.

---

## I. The Light Itself

**R-001** — "There's a second sun in the rose, neighbor — green as river ice. Stand inside at dawn; it isn't there from the street."
TRUE · origin: the dawn crowd, older than anyone · carriers: everyone · danger: the word "sun" near clergy or grey coats is citable.
Drift: "it only shows to Ombreval-born" → "there's a second sky behind the rose."

**R-002** — "Through a red pane the clouds go red but the disc stays green. Every light in the world obeys the glass but that one."
TRUE · origin: an old vergers' demonstration line · carriers: pilgrims, vergers, market folk · danger: mild; a moth notes who shows it to strangers.
Drift: "a red pane cracked when it passed" → "the glaziers can't fire true red anymore."

**R-003** — "You can stare straight into it and it doesn't hurt. Try that with the honest one."
TRUE · origin: dawn-crowd commonplace · carriers: pilgrims, children · danger: "the honest one" implies two suns; citable if a grey coat is peevish.
Drift: "stare too long and the true sun goes dim for you" → "it blinds the wicked and spares the good."

**R-004** — "At the crossing you cast two shadows, and the cold one points wrong. And wax won't soften in the green beam — ask any chandler."
TRUE · origin: chandlers' shop-talk; Osanne Vell says the wax line herself, deliberately mundane · carriers: tradesfolk, mothers · danger: testing your grave-shadow in the nave puts your face in a grey notebook.
Drift: "a man's grave-shadow stayed after he left" → "the vergers mop the shadows out at dusk."

**R-005** — "It walks against the sun, one east, one west, and once a day they pass in the window. Folk call it the Kiss."
TRUE · origin: generations of dawn-watchers · carriers: everyone; Crier Jos Brant calls the hour of it · danger: "the Kiss" is tolerated; "the two suns pass" is citable.
Drift: "a wish spoken at the Kiss lands within the year" → "when the two wed, the world ends."

**R-006** — "The doctor measured it. It stands in the same pane for every eye in the nave at once, which nothing in nature can do."
TRUE · origin: Doctor Aubin Ferrant, to anyone who asks · carriers: scholars, clerks, slowly the taverns · danger: adding "so it's in the glass" starts a masons' quarrel.
Drift: "the doctor proved it's inside the glass" → "the doctor was paid to prove nothing."

**R-007** — "In the great storm of three eighty-three, lightning went white in every light of the rose save where the disc stood. Sworn to the Chapter, by the light that does not lie."
TRUE · origin: the Long Book · carriers: old men, ringers, storm-watchers · danger: none as history; embroidery is where trouble starts.
Drift: "the disc drank the lightning" → "storms come to Ombreval because of it."

**R-008** — "There's boatmen swear that in a night storm they've seen it standing in the open sky, in the wrong place, for the length of a flash."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN · origin: boat-folk working the Serle beyond the south wall; no two accounts agree · carriers: boatmen, drinkers · danger: an open-sky claim contradicts doctrine wholesale; the Unwalled listen hungrily.
Drift: "it stood over where the martyr drowned" → "it comes out at night to look for Colm."

**R-009** — "When a pane's out, the hole shows plain sky. It doesn't come through the gap. Every man who's seen that goes quiet after."
TRUE · origin: Fabric workmen; Verger Dunstan Pike confirms with a shrug · carriers: workmen, vergers; rarely said twice · danger: it embarrasses heretic and official doctrine alike; both prefer it unheard.
Drift: "the hole shows a different sky" → "the gap is how it breathes."

**R-010** — "My mother saw a beam of the rose land in a courtyard three streets away — colored light on the cobbles and no window near."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN · origin: Renna Tapster, of her mother · carriers: her regulars; mothers · danger: outside-beam claims are filed as not demonstrated; the claimant gets a courteous grey visit.
Drift: "beams walk the city on feast nights" → "the light is looking for a way out."

## II. The Doors, and This Year's Concurrence

**R-011** — "They'll bar the doors again this year, mark me. The Chapter never unlearns a thing it learned in fear."
OPEN (a live question — resolves through play when the Chapter decides, never through gossip) · origin: Tam Rud and the angry intake · carriers: taverns; the Bell and Ladle holds wagers · danger: safe as opinion; "we'll open them ourselves" is the sentence that hangs.
Drift: "the doors are already ordered barred" → "they mean to bar the city gates too."

**R-012** — "A child's arm broke in the crush at the Shut Door, and none of the Chapter has once said the child's name."
TRUE · origin: the Gradine crowd; the child is Ede of the Needle, which few connect · carriers: everyone · danger: much to answer if a grey coat asks how you know the crowd's mood.
Drift: "the child died" → "three children, buried quiet."

**R-013** — "If the feast day clouds over, the shadows don't fuse and the whole feast fails. Remember three forty-seven."
TRUE (the folk gloss "unblessed year" is forbidden, and said anyway) · origin: folk memory of F.347 · carriers: grandmothers, ringers · danger: priests correct "unblessed" wearily rather than report it.
Drift: "a clouded feast means a death in the Chapter" → "the clouds are sent when the city sins."

**R-014** — "The year the bells rang the feast on the wrong noon, the mending of the almanac came from the Greensick's own figures. Their sums beat the Church's."
TRUE · origin: leaked from a heretic weigher's cup · carriers: drinkers, gossiping clerks · danger: marks you heretic-adjacent in a grey ledger.
Drift: "the Chapter buys its almanac from the heretics" → "the heretics can call the light to a day of their choosing."

**R-015** — "They say the Greensick mean to sing their song in the nave itself at the Passing this year, doors or no doors."
TRUE (the plan exists within the cell) · origin: the angry intake, too warm in taverns · carriers: drinkers; a moth has likely carried it · danger: gravest in the pool — it points the Custody at the feast; a sworn hearer marks your face.
Drift: "they mean to break the doors" → "they mean to break the rose."

## III. The Candor and the Grey Coats

**R-016** — "It's no sun at all, friend. It's the Emblem — a granted image, a painting of the Light on the eye of the Church."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN · origin: the Edict of F.244; every pulpit since · carriers: clergy, the pious, anyone in grey hearing · danger: none — the one safe sentence; note who says it too quickly.
Drift: "the Emblem watches back" → "the Custody paints it fresh each year."

**R-017** — "Confess you saw it and you're shriven free, no penance. So do we all, child, affirm nothing. Costs less than a candle."
TRUE · origin: every parish confessional · carriers: everyone; taught with the catechism · danger: none; that is the design.
Drift: "the priests see it too — they admit it" → "seeing it is required now."

**R-018** — "The Church tested the thing long ago, behind doors, and sealed what it found. That's what the grey office keeps. The answer."
TRUE (that it tested and sealed; but the seal holds no answer, only what it is not — the "answer" clause is false, and the Custody knows it) · origin: tavern deduction from the trial of two eighty-eight · carriers: masons, drinkers, clerks · danger: speculating on the Grey Press draws the attention it speculates about.
Drift: "the verdict says the glaziers did it" → "there is no verdict — the seal is on an empty box."

**R-019** — "Every new Praelucent is taken alone to the crossing and made to look. And made to sign what they saw."
TRUE · origin: unknown; leaked as shape without substance · carriers: almost none — a senior clerk, Ferrant privately, one Keyhole neighbor · danger: Custody-secret; the wrong hearer will want your whole acquaintance.
Drift: "they sign in their own blood" → "one Praelucent refused, and vanished."

**R-020** — "That two-year repair on the rose — they swapped panes behind the canvas and nothing changed. Ask a man who stood that scaffold. He won't answer, and that's your answer."
TRUE · origin: scaffold workmen's silences since F.421 · carriers: Fabric workmen, their wives, Coswald's Yard · danger: naming a workman puts his silence-oath, and him, in question.
Drift: "the rose up there is a copy" → "the true rose is in the chapter-house cellar."

**R-021** — "Here's the one they'd mortar a street over: new plain glass, leaded into the rose, shows it same as the old. Proven at the trial. Proven again under the canvas."
TRUE (nearly extinct as speech) · origin: at most three mouths could start it; Pike, drunk, has come within a word of it twice · carriers: effectively none · danger: sharpest in the pool; traced to its mouth within a week, and the mouth is under oath.
Drift: "new glass goes green in that window" → "old glass remembers" (becoming the heretics' error, and no longer true).

**R-022** — "Grey coats stand at the back of every pauper's burying now, counting faces at the charnel door."
TRUE · origin: Wyn Alder noticed; Sexton Noll Fitch confirmed by saying nothing, pointedly · carriers: boat-folk, mourners, the poor · danger: said at the funeral itself, it files your face with the rest.
Drift: "they write down the mourners' names" → "the Custody digs them up after."

**R-023** — "The grey office thinks the paupers' funerals are a door of some kind. They haven't found the lock it fits."
TRUE · origin: half deduction from Fitch's silences, half a grey clerk's own unhappiness · carriers: the careful few of Saint Maren's parish · danger: the sworn hear the beginning of the end, the Custody its own file read back; both trace it.
Drift: "the knell is a signal" (walking straight at the rule) → "the sexton is the heretics' bellman."

**R-024** — "The pawnshop at the Tally Bridge washes the grey office's money. And sells old pages to the other lot across the same counter."
TRUE · origin: Tallage porters; Cobb the porter says it plainest · carriers: Tallage folk, boatmen · danger: Lise Copp forgets nothing; both institutions need the shop, so the speaker alone has no protector.
Drift: "Lise Copp is the Custody's officer" → "Lise Copp is the Tracer" (wrong, and lethal to stand near).

**R-025** — "Somebody at the Bellstand sells talk to the grey coats. Has for years. Mind what you say in the crier's square."
TRUE (it is Brant, who does not know it) · origin: the cell's cold arithmetic after F.428 · carriers: careful tradesfolk, sworn neighbors, Renna · danger: naming a name starts a fire; the wrong name ruins an innocent, the right one everything.
Drift: "it's the crier himself" (true, and laughed off) → "it's whoever asks what you heard."

## IV. The Greensick

**R-026** — "Cross the Greensick and they'll cut your throat in a covered passage. They've done it before."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED · origin: Renna Tapster, warning young fools sincerely · carriers: taverners, mothers, watchmen · danger: none to the speaker; it is the cell's best armor, and false.
Drift: "they cart the bodies through the river gate" → "they drowned Colm themselves, to make a martyr."

**R-027** — "Whisper wrong at their door twice and it's worse than knives. A note goes to the grey coats with your name on it. Unsigned."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN (the cell neither confirms nor forbids) · origin: unknown; possibly the cell, as deterrent · carriers: the fearful and the knowing · danger: knowing this much about their doors is itself a question — who told you?
Drift: "the Custody and the Greensick trade names like fish" → "the doorkeeper sells faces at the bridge" (near enough Grigor Ashe's ledger to sweat him).

**R-028** — "There was a green sun in the open sky once, over everyone's grandmothers, and the Lanthorn was built course by course to wall it out."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED · origin: Colm Attergate, the Bellstand, F.301; the Unwalled since · carriers: sworn neighbors carefully, the sympathetic poor · danger: the Attergate error entire; said plainly, a door-grade offense.
Drift: "the old folk remember it" (none ever did) → "the Church stole the warm sun and left us the cold one."

**R-029** — "Old glass remembers the sky it cooled under. That's why the rose shows what the sky's forgotten."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED · origin: Unwalled doctrine, generations old · carriers: neighbors, romantics, one glazier's apprentice · danger: heresy dressed as shop-lore; customers ask glaziers to "wake" old panes.
Drift: "a bottle from Colm's day shows a green spark at dawn" → "the Sparrs fire memory into glass to this day" (see R-035).

**R-030** — "Colm Attergate drowned at the Serle sluice, coming home by night against his banishment, when the Cut still ran. The dry gate keeps the place, and the Alders know the arch."
TRUE · origin: the Long Book and boat-folk memory; an Alder hooked him out · carriers: boatmen, pilgrims shown an arch close enough for a penny, the cell like scripture · danger: reverence dressed in awe is citable Attergate talk.
Drift: "he preached from the water as he went down" → "he never drowned — he walled himself into the tower he built."

**R-031** — "The Greensick keep his last letter, in his own hand, wet from the night he drowned."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED (Corin Copp's forgery; the cell holds it genuine) · origin: within the cell, reverently; leaked as legend · carriers: neighbors as fact, the street as story · danger: claiming to have seen it interests the Custody and the cell both.
Drift: "it names the informant of twenty-eight" (it does not; the weighers dread this drift) → "whoever reads it aloud goes mad."

**R-032** — "The whisperers of twenty-eight were sold from inside. The informant was never found. Never will be, now."
TRUE · origin: the surviving cell, bleeding · carriers: everyone who remembers F.428; every sworn neighbor, nightly · danger: guessing names near a sworn neighbor is the fastest way in Ombreval to be fed a false meeting.
Drift: "it was the fuller himself, and the damp his payment" → "it was one of the two who recanted" (Betriss Marle bears this in silence).

**R-033** — "The Greensick pass is a verse of the forbidden song. Sing the right line at the right door and it opens."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED (the true rule is the buried name; the cell feeds the error) · origin: honest confusion of verse with pass · carriers: drinkers, children daring each other at cellar doors · danger: singing Lucem non murabis at any door is heresy at song-volume.
Drift: "hum it and they find you" → "the little one at the Needle knows the line" (see R-046).

**R-034** — "Chalk marks come and go at the midpoint of the Needle. Thieves' signs, marking whose house is ripe."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED (the marks are real — the cell's convening signs; the reading is wrong) · origin: a watchman's guess; never corrected · carriers: householders, watchmen · danger: standing to study the chalk puts you in Ede's sight, and in anyone's.
Drift: "it's a mason's diamond — lodge business" (wrong trade, a whisker from true) → "rub one out and your name goes up on one."

## V. Glass Against Stone

**R-035** — "It's in the glass, neighbor. Glazier sorcery, fired into the rose by the Sparr line, and the court knew it and sold the verdict."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED · origin: the masons' lodge since F.288; Warden Hamel Stott keeps it in repair · carriers: masons, half of Coswald's Yard · danger: in Cinder Row it starts a fight; near a grey coat it reopens a sealed case.
Drift: "melt the rose and the thing dies" → "the peace fine was blood-money for the three journeymen."

**R-036** — "When the last panel was set, Master Idonea looked back through her own window and wept, and said she'd made two of something there was one of. Never fired glass again."
TRUE · origin: witnesses at the crossing, F.203; the Long Book · carriers: glaziers with pride and unease, pilgrim guides, the cell as scripture misread · danger: adding "she knew what she'd done" makes it an accusation with a guild attached.
Drift: "she cursed the window as she wept" → "she's buried facing away from the Lanthorn" (she is not).

**R-037** — "Three journeymen were broken at the Question in two eighty-eight, for a crime the court's own test disproved. And the verdict was never read — not to the lodge, not to the guild."
TRUE · origin: the glaziers' guild, which has never let it rest · carriers: Cinder Row; Mistress Jonet Sparr in two flat sentences · danger: reciting it at Coswaldstide starts the annual brawl.
Drift: "one of the three was a Sparr" → "the masons paid the court" (they paid nothing and got nothing — the true grievance).

**R-038** — "The Sparrs keep a sorcery-book of the old master's under the workshop floor, and show no one."
FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED (one page exists, in Idonea's hand, shown to no one; not a book, not sorcery) · origin: the lodge, embroidering · carriers: masons, envious tradesfolk · danger: it has sent thieves over Cinder Row walls twice; Jonet Sparr keeps dogs now.
Drift: "the Custody offered gold and was refused" → "its last page is the missing page of the deposition."

**R-039** — "The masons' own charter binds the lodge to close the light above. Read it how you like. They swore it before the rose was ever fired."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN (the clause exists; roofing boilerplate, probably) · origin: a lodge clerk's loose cup at a Coswaldstide, years back; Stott has never denied it · carriers: glaziers gleefully, drinkers · danger: quoting a guild's charter at its warden is a short conversation.
Drift: "Coswald himself signed it" → "no mason will look at the rose" (they look).

## VI. The Dead, the Beam, and the Spared

**R-040** — "Speak your dead's name in the green beam and they hear you. It's a long way up, but the light knows the road."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN · origin: old as the pilgrim trade; Crier Jos Brant its loudest friend · carriers: Brant, the Alders every Bellday, mourners, half the city · danger: none; a priest murmurs "affirm nothing" and lets it pass.
Drift: "the dead answer, if you bring their favorite thing" → "the beam is the dead looking back."

**R-041** — "Hold a newborn in the doubled light and the child has a spare shadow to spend. And a fever carried into the green beam cools."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN (the beam is truly cold, which proves nothing) · origin: mothers; Mother Gude sells the herbs they clutch on the way · carriers: mothers, herb-wives, Brant · danger: none — unless the fever wins, and grief goes looking for someone to cite.
Drift: "a child twinned in the light never drowns" → "spend the spare shadow and you limp after."

**R-042** — "Mother Gude looked once, at the strong hour, and saw one sun. One. Whatever's in that window, it isn't in it for her."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN (whether the Spared cannot see, or lie, is never resolved) · origin: Mother Gude herself, wearily · carriers: market women, pilgrims, all three factions — each wanting her claim · danger: calling her liar at Maren's Green costs your custom; calling her blessed near the Custody invites unanswerable questions.
Drift: "the Spared are marked on the palm" → "the Spared walk through its beams unshadowed" (children test this on her; she lets them).

**R-043** — "The anchoress in the Ilvane wall keeps the glazier's last words behind her teeth. Taught mouth to mouth down the line since the year the master died."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN (not even Dame Aldith knows what she carries) · origin: Keyhole whispers; Idonea dead and the anchorhold filled, both in two eleven · carriers: pilgrims, the cell (forbidden to knock, so they talk), children trading news for verses · danger: pressing the squint is watched; the Custody minds who leans on it.
Drift: "the words are the missing page" → "whoever hears them sees what Idonea saw."

## VII. City Noise — the Camouflage

*Rumors with no bearing on the Second Sun, load-bearing precisely because of it: a gossip network that talks only of green suns is a searchlight.*

**R-044** — "Folk won't wed under the Orphan. Her leaf burned in the sacristy fire, and no man living remembers her baptism."
TRUE (the burned register; the scruple is custom) · origin: ringers, since F.341 · carriers: everyone · danger: none; the city's safest superstition.
Drift: "the Orphan rings by herself on the eve of a death" → "her true name is written in the Grey Press."

**R-045** — "The Needle's a shoulder wide because two burned-out neighbors each rebuilt a half-step forward, and now neither can sell nor yield."
TRUE · origin: the Long Book, F.371; the lane itself · carriers: everyone; the city's favorite parable · danger: none — which is why the Needle is good ground for chalk; nobody watches a proverb.
Drift: "the two owners were brothers" → "past the Needle means past saving because a man died wedged there."

**R-046** — "Penny for the little one at the Needle. Ede hears everything in that lane, and what she hears, she keeps."
TRUE (more than the speaker knows: she holds the current counter-sign, told to no one) · origin: Renna Tapster, half warning, half fond · carriers: Bellstand regulars · danger: it puts eyes on a child; cell and Custody alike are one sentence from wondering what she has heard.
Drift: "the orphan girl runs for the Greensick" (she belongs to no one) → "she has the pass" (she has the counter-sign; nearly, damnably right).

**R-047** — "The doctor's been at it thirty years, and all he'll say is: it moves against the sun, it stands in one pane for every eye, and he has no theory. An impossible light, and the city at peace with it. Except him."
TRUE · origin: Ferrant, in his own words; the phrase escapes him into the street, garbled · carriers: patients, clerks, tavern wits — the fastest-drifting entry in the pool · danger: none; the Custody finds him useful.
Drift: "the impossible light — that's what the doctor calls the city" → "the doctor proved it impossible, so it isn't there" (Renna's favorite, for the laugh).

**R-048** — "A leaf of old writing fetches more at the Tally Bridge than a silver spoon, which is against nature. And somewhere out there is the page that names what Idonea saw."
AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN (as to the page; the prices are TRUE) · origin: Corin Copp feeds it, for trade reasons · carriers: pawn-side Tallage, scriveners, hopeful thieves · danger: shopping a Sparr page attracts three buyers, and two of them are the Custody.
Drift: "the page is behind the anchoress's wall" → "there are twelve last pages sold and counting" (Corin's quiet joke about his own trade).

## Sampling Notes for the Sidecar

**Per district.** Weight by where the NPC stands or trades. *Lanthorn and Gradine:* I, II, VI heavy; R-016/R-017 from any cleric; never IV from clergy except R-026 as warning. *Wickmarket:* I and VI, plus R-004. *Coswald's Yard:* V heavy — R-035 and R-037 are identity; II at brawl pitch near Coswaldstide. *Cinder Row:* R-002/R-036/R-037 with pride, R-029/R-038 with fury. *Tallage and Tally Bridge:* III heavy — R-024, R-048 — plus VII; Lise Copp repeats nothing. *Maren's Green and the dry Cut:* R-008, R-022, R-030, R-040, R-042, boat-folk versions throughout. *Bellstand and the Bell and Ladle:* everything, one hop noisier — the tavern is the pool's mixing vessel; Renna carries R-010, R-026, R-046; Brant carries R-005 and R-040, and unknowingly reports what he hears.

**Per stance.** *Clergy:* only R-016/R-017 plus II as worry, and R-026 as a warning; correct forbidden vocabulary in others. *Grey clerks:* repeat nothing, recognize everything; a citizen voicing R-015, R-019, R-021, R-023, or R-028 within twenty meters is remembered and reported. *Sworn Unwalled:* hold R-028 through R-032 as faith, deny them to strangers; go quiet or hostile at R-015, R-023, R-027, or name-guessing on R-032. *Masons:* R-035 sincere; *glaziers:* R-036/R-037 sincere; each calls the other's rumor slander. *Folk believers:* VI entire, plus R-001 through R-005 with wonder. *Children:* R-003, R-030, R-041, R-045, and the Colm rhyme; children speak freely — nobody cites a child, and everyone forgets they are inside the twenty meters.

**Mechanics.** (1) Truth values bind the simulation, not the speaker: every carrier speaks sincerely, including FALSE-BUT-BELIEVED. (2) Never resolve AMBIGUOUS-BY-DESIGN entries — no NPC, however senior, may confirm or deny R-008, R-010, R-016, R-027, R-039, R-040 through R-043, or R-048. R-011 is OPEN, not ambiguous: no NPC may confirm or deny it before the Chapter's decision event fires; once it fires, the doors question resolves like any other fact. (3) Pick the drift hop by social distance from the origin: the origin's district speaks the root; adjacent districts hop one; two or more districts away hops two. (4) R-019 and R-021 are LOCKED: never sample by default; only a designer trigger (Pike drunk and pressed, a stolen Attestation leaf in play) may voice them. (5) Danger runs through the live microphone: anything the player repeats within twenty meters of the wrong hearer takes the consequence in that entry's danger field, and moths pass along what they hear whether they understand it or not.
