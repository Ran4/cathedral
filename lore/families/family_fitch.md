# The Fitches

> *The register and the grave.* — stub. See [overview](overview.md) and
> `../the_dry_boatmen.md` (Noll Fitch and what his lintel is read for).

- **Standing:** family of standing — the parish house
- **Heartland:** Reed Ward (**Saint Maren of the Reeds** and its churchyard),
  branched into Bell-and-Sluice and Fabric
- **Trades:** sexton and funerary work, and the parish register; leather,
  cooperage, masonry, pottery, civic office
- **Anchors:** Noll Fitch (57, Sexton of Saint Maren — opens the ground, rings
  the name-knell, chalks the newest buried name on the charnel-door lintel)

Where a family keeps the register and the grave, it keeps the ward's real
memory. Noll Fitch has slow hands and long silences and has buried too much of
the parish to be hurried by anyone's grief. He long ago worked out *what his
lintel is read for* — the Unwalled's charity pays Saint Maren's for paupers'
funerals, and the freshly-chalked name is the cell's pass — and he rings slow,
chalks plain, and says nothing, on purpose. In the boatmen's phrase, *Noll Fitch
has written down a number of things*: the sexton decides, quietly, who counts as
buried, born, and of the ward. A parish-and-burial house, honest at the center
and lightly crooked at the edges.
