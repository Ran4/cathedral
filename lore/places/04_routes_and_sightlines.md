# Routes, Processions, and Sightlines

## Purpose

Coordinates make a map consistent; routes make it lived in. This file tests
the plan through the journeys residents actually repeat and defines the visual
reveals a first-person build should protect.

## Daily freight routes

### River freight to the Tallage

```text
outer wharf -> River Gate -> River Cartway -> Cut junction
             -> west along Cut -> Tallage queue -> weigh-beam
             -> bonded yard or released warehouse
```

The route is cartable throughout. It never crosses the Gradine or Lanthorn
grounds. A salt cart can reach the Gaunt cellars after weighing by taking the
east/north service lane rather than turning through a pedestrian passage.

The journey should visibly lose the river at the gate. By the time freight
reaches the Cut, only ropes, wet cloaks in bad weather, cargo, speech, and old
architecture remember water.

### Fish to Maren's Green

```text
fish wharf -> River Gate for carts / Reed Postern for handbarrows
           -> east Cut branch / Maren's Slip
           -> Maren's Green -> fish hall, smoke racks, Moorings, sellers
```

Customs can divert suspect handbarrows from the postern toward the River Gate.
The two routes explain both the market's strong relationship to the wharves
and the absence of water inside it.

### Wool to cloth

```text
Brede and Combs -> Wool Gate -> brokers near Wickmarket
                -> Draper's Reach / Tenterhook Lane / cloth halls
                -> Tallage when customs or export requires
                -> River Gate and outer wharves
```

Raw wool need not pass the Lanthorn. Finished cloth can reach either river or
land routes. The Draper's Reach belongs between Coswald's Yard and the
Wickmarket because timber, frames, carriers, capital, and western wool traffic
meet there, not because five squares needed a connecting line.

### Stone and Fabric material

```text
quarries and timber roads -> Stone Gate -> Coswald's Yard
                           -> sorting, dressing, tracing, hoisting
                           -> Fabric Way -> Lanthorn north service lane
```

Loads too long for Crookneck Lane or Malt Passage remain on Fabric Way. The
final turn toward the Lanthorn prevents a runaway cart from having an open
line to a public door and creates the framed scaffold view from the yard.

### Tallow and hides

```text
Wool Gate / Shambles -> hide sorting -> Tanners' Slip or outbound trade
Shambles renderers -> Wickmarket chandlers -> city lamps and Lanthorn candles
```

This route crosses the west Cut and uses service lanes behind the Wickmarket.
It does not parade raw slaughter work through the western pilgrim approach.

## Ordinary walking routes

### Wickmarket to Tallage

Residents have three meaningful choices:

1. the Needle, shortest and slow whenever someone approaches from the other
   direction;
2. Cinder Row and the south working lanes, longer but commercially useful; or
3. the western Cut approach, longest but suitable for a handcart.

That choice turns the Needle into more than a narrow diorama. “Past the
Needle” belongs to a path people actually need.

### Coswald's Yard to Wickmarket

The Draper's Reach is the sheltered trade route. Tenterhook Lane is the open,
messier alternative. A northern back route through Seven Lofts is possible but
too long for daily cloth business. The two primary choices keep the Reach busy
in rain without making it the only connection.

### Lanthorn to Bellstand

Bell Way starts around the apse service ground, turns into a domestic street,
and compresses beneath or alongside Bellfoot's tower stair before opening into
the square. People leaving a proclamation for the cathedral do not walk down a
royal avenue; they lose the watch tower after one turn and find the apse after
the next.

### Bellstand to Tallage

The quickest public route runs south-west through the east ward and joins the
Cut east of the Tallage. A northern alternative passes Crookneck Lane and the
Fabric precinct. This makes edicts, customs news, and pawnshop gossip travel
through actual neighbourhoods.

## Feast and civic routes

### Ambrellestide glass procession

```text
glaziers' guildhall -> Cinder Row -> south side of Gradine
                    -> align before west doors -> Lanthorn
```

Wrapped panes are carried by hand. Street corners must be wide enough for
bearers to turn together but narrow enough that neighbours can lean from
windows and join behind. Furnace work on the Row is cold that morning. The
route enters the Gradine from one side, then becomes formal; that change of
alignment is part of the ritual.

### Coswaldstide scaffold blessing

The principal rite begins in Coswald's Yard among tools, scaffold poles, and
the tracing floor. A smaller guild procession follows Fabric Way to the
Lanthorn works. The whole crowd does not need to reach the Gradine. Brawls over
F.288 can start in either yard or route and attract glaziers arriving from the
west.

### Vhairestide

The year turns with the first boats blessed at the outer wharves. Most of the
rite is outside the south wall and heard or reported within. A smaller return
procession enters through the River Gate and follows the Cut toward Saint
Maren's. Vhairé Farcall connects the off-map event to the whole city through
sound.

### Marenstide

Maren's Green holds the eel fair. The remembrance of the drowned moves between
Saint Maren's churchyard, Green, and Reed Postern. It need not stand at an
actual water edge. Boat-families can carry river reeds, lamps, ropes, and names
from the outer wharves as material signs of the absent water.

### The Concurrence

Every ward feeds the Gradine and west doors by a different approach:

- west through Wickmarket and Pilgrim Bend;
- north through Fabric Way and the statue courts;
- east around the Lanthorn service lanes;
- south from Tallage and Cinder Ward; and
- south-east from Maren's Green along Cut and cross streets.

The Gradine cannot absorb all flows if the doors close. Seller pitches,
statue-court spectators, side exits, and step edges turn a stoppage into the
F.436 crush without requiring an invisible wall. Crowd staging for F.437 must
leave open the question of whether doors bar again.

The impossible light's fusion is visible only inside. People stuck on the
Gradine see dark exterior glass and one sky.

### Wet Alms

Bread distribution occurs at controlled Lanthorn doors and along one Gradine
edge. Grain arrives early from Seven Lofts and ovens by Fabric Way, not through
the waiting poor. The queue can turn toward the statue courts without blocking
all western approach traffic.

### Proclamations and recantations

The Scold gathers people to the Bellstand for ordinary law. Major Church
recantations occur on the Gradine. A crier or clerk carries the notice between
them along Bell Way; the places remain distinct expressions of secular watch
and ecclesiastical theatre.

## The pilgrimage of observation

The canonical route remains physically possible:

1. Arrive on the Gradine before first light and see dark glass under one sky.
2. Enter through the west doors for the dawn-showing.
3. Move to the sightline through the red Burning of Cinder Row pane.
4. Cross aisle to aisle and compare the disc's pane with another observer.
5. Stand mid-nave in the morning lance and test the two shadows and Wickmarket
   tallow.
6. Cross the threshold and see the second light vanish absolutely.
7. Reach the triforium with Verger Pike and compare floor and gallery views.
8. Return to the crossing for the strong hour and Passing.
9. Walk from the Lanthorn to the Tally Bridge at dusk and finish beneath two
   dry arches casting one ordinary set of shadows.

The outdoor part from west doors to Tally Bridge is roughly six to seven
minutes by Cinder and Tallage streets. It should pass ordinary work, not a
continuous trail of mystery props. The final honest shadows matter because the
whole city between church and bridge behaves normally.

## Covert routes and acoustic requirements

### Signal to sites

The Needle's midpoint is the signal site. From there:

- high diamond: continue south/east toward the Tallage, circle through a salt
  lane, approach Gaunt Passage without walking straight from the signal;
- low diamond: double back north through Tenterhook Lane and reach the Draper's
  loft by either stair;
- doorside diamond: travel along the Cut to Saint Maren's after a burial and
  use the lawful charnel stair; and
- struck-through diamond: leave by any ordinary destination and do not test a
  door.

The three sites occupy different trades and wards. No single rooftop or watch
tower should visually connect all of them.

### Gaunt Passage hearing

The grate sits within arm's reach of the public passage. Both approach bends
must put ordinary bystanders, salt workers, or a watcher within the game's
normal twenty-metre speech radius at plausible moments. Walls block sight but
do not create a special stealth volume. The passage's danger is social timing.

The actual inner gathering position should be more than twenty metres in full
3D distance from public occupancy when the closing recitation is spoken,
unless an intentional grate leak is being staged. The cellar and weighing-yard
depth can achieve this without magic soundproofing.

### Draper's Reach escape

Both loft stairs must work. The north overhead bridge must lead somewhere a
person can continue, not to a sealed decorative door. Roof escape is difficult
and visible in daylight. At dusk, any loft light can be seen from Coswald's
Yard, making the place unsuitable for large night meetings.

### Saint Maren's burial cover

The churchyard, Eel Bridge, market lane, and church porch need overlapping
ordinary traffic after a pauper's burial. A grey clerk can observe lingerers
from the bridge or door. The last pair can rake crypt earth and leave by the
lawful charnel stair. There is no secret tunnel from the crypt to the river.

### False site

The brine-rotted cellar off Tanners' Slip must feel plausible enough to test
and unpleasant enough that no normal cell traffic accidentally appears there.
It has one bad approach, no gathering exit, and strong neighbourhood memory of
strangers. Being fed this site is a social consequence, not a dungeon quest.

## Protected first-person reveals

### Wool Gate reveal

From outside or within the gate throat, the player sees dense roofs, perhaps a
Lanthorn crown, and one statue silhouette—not the whole west facade. The
Wickmarket blocks the lower cathedral. Pilgrim Bend later reveals both statues;
the final turn reveals the Great Rose and Gradine.

### Statue scale reveal

The Dawn Bearer and Seraph need two readings. At distance they mark the route
over rooftops. At close range, adjacent three-storey houses and people make the
thirty-metre scale legible. A giant empty plaza would make them feel smaller
and detach them from the city.

### Coswald scaffold reveal

From the masons' lodge porch, poles and stone frame the scaffolded north-tower
crown. Along Fabric Way, the tower disappears behind one close facade and
returns larger at the service-lane turn. This is Coswald's Yard's emotional
axis.

### Cut reveal

Most crooked lanes show twenty to sixty metres. On entering the Cut, the
player suddenly reads hundreds of metres of aligned facades, overhead bridges,
dust, and carts. The line is never perfectly empty, but its engineered origin
is unmistakable.

### Tally Bridge dusk reveal

Approaching from the west or east, twin arches stack freight and silhouettes.
At dusk, one ordinary sun throws coherent shadows through them. No wet shader,
green lamp, or occult graffiti competes with that quiet normality.

### Maren's Green reveal

The first cue is eel smoke and the bell, not water. The Green opens as linked
market pockets. Moving around a cart at the Eel stair reveals Saint Maren's
charnel lintel across the churchyard; climbing the stair makes the name easier
to read and makes the grey clerk easier to notice.

### Bellstand reveal

Bellfoot Passage frames bright square and crier platform while the stair cuts
diagonally overhead. Entering from Crookneck Lane instead reveals the Scold
first above roofs and the platform last. Both approaches concentrate attention
without placing the tower in the geometric centre.

### Ilvane reveal

Crookneck Lane presents the chapel as ordinary old sacred masonry. Only after
turning along its north wall does the open squint and waiting alms basket show
that someone lives inside what was sealed. No lighting sting or apparition is
needed.

### Old Sluice reveal

The straight Cut appears to end in heavy dry arches. Nearer, a pedestrian side
route bends toward Harne Gate while filled ground and abutting houses deny any
waterfront. Candle stubs, if present after Colm's Night, are small enough to be
missed.

## Night and bells

At night, street navigation relies on sparse lamps, windows, known silhouettes,
and bells. The Lanthorn ring carries citywide. The Scold dominates the eastern
half and curfew. Maren Smallvoice is strongest around the Reed Ward. The Hungry
Ox song spills into only nearby lanes.

Belwyn's lamp rotates among the lamps in each square. A dark lamp creates one
local patch, not a citywide stealth mechanic. The Second Sun is never visible
at night and produces no hum, reversed bell, music, or lighting response.

## Flight check

Developer flight should confirm the plan rather than expose its tricks:

- one Serle beyond the south wall and no in-city canal;
- the dry Cut as a straight historical scar;
- dense parcels around, but not through, the Lanthorn;
- five distinct working squares rather than five matching tiles;
- gate roads that converge for material reasons;
- the cathedral dominant over civic and parish towers; and
- one ordinary sky everywhere outside the Lanthorn.

From above, secret sites remain ordinary roofs, cellars, courts, and passages.
The geography can reveal how they connect without revealing what they mean.

## Route acceptance tests for later builds

A place feature is not spatially finished until a tester can complete its
relevant route:

1. carry a notional long timber from Stone Gate through Coswald's Yard to the
   Fabric service lane without clipping a decorative choke;
2. drive a notional cart from River Gate to Tallage and Maren's Green without
   stairs or cathedral ground;
3. walk Wickmarket to Tallage through the Needle and by a handcart alternative;
4. walk both ends of the Draper's Reach and both loft exits;
5. see the charnel lintel from the Eel Bridge rail;
6. traverse both public mouths of Gaunt Passage and the cellar-to-yard escape;
7. walk Cinder Row's Ambrellestide route carrying a wide pane;
8. approach Bellstand through Bellfoot and from Crookneck Lane;
9. walk the full dry Cut from Chain Bridge to Old Sluice; and
10. fly the walls and verify that all visible working water is outside the
    south wall.

These tests are more useful than checking whether a square's centre matches a
coordinate exactly. The coordinates support the relationships; the journeys
prove them.
