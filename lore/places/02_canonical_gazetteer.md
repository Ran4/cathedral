# Gazetteer of Established Places

> This file gives every established or previously approved named place a site
> in the new city plan. Facts marked **secret** are author knowledge and must
> not be injected into an arbitrary citizen's prompt. Claims marked **never
> confirm** remain unresolved even if the player visits the site.

## The Lanthorn precinct

### The Lanthorn

The Great Church of Saint Ambrelle remains exactly where the game has built it,
centred near `(0, -12)`, with quire east, west doors and Great Rose west. Its
nine-bay nave, aisles, crossing, transepts, quire, triforium, clerestory walks,
chapels, towers, chapter house, institutional rooms, and exterior Fabric work
are not redesigned by this plan.

The city presses close without becoming careless. Service lanes run around
the north and south buttresses. The apse has a narrow working apron before
houses resume. The west front alone receives a formal forecourt. Carts serving
the Fabric approach from Coswald's Yard around the north side; funerals and
ordinary worshippers may reach the side doors without crossing the Gradine.
This keeps the church a daily employer and parish-scale machine as well as a
monument.

No exterior point shows the impossible light. From the Gradine the Great Rose
is dark glass and tracery. From rooftops, statues, walls, the Bellstand, and
developer flight, the sky contains one ordinary sun.

The north tower scaffold remains visible from Coswald's Yard down a framed
working route. It should not be visible in full from every northern street;
rooflines and turns repeatedly lose and recover it.

### The Great Rose and the eye

The Great Rose occupies the west front around plan point `(0, 81)`. The eye is
its central round light. They are landmarks within the Lanthorn, not separate
outdoor districts. A future interior area breakdown may name the west-door
bay, nave, crossing, triforium, or quire, but none may overlap the existing
`lanthorn_interior` area until the spatial model supports hierarchy.

The authoritative observation route still works: outside on the Gradine;
through the west doors at dawn; across the nave; under the red Cinder Row pane;
to the crossing at the strong hour; back to the threshold. Architecture around
the cathedral must not block the western sky from the Rose.

### The chapter house and Grey Press

The chapter house belongs to the Lanthorn's north-east institutional fabric,
with its public door reached from the north service lane at approximately
`(58, -65)`. Canons, petitioners, copyists, Fabric clerks, and messengers make
the outer rooms busy enough that a grey-clad clerk does not look exceptional.

The Custody of the Eye is seated inside. Its archive, the Grey Press, is a
secure sequence of dry inner rooms rather than a freestanding sinister tower.
Its existence is public; its contents and exact internal approach are not.
The final position must follow the preserved cathedral geometry when that
interior is surveyed.

**Secret:** The Attestations and suppressed test records remain in the Grey
Press. This plan creates no convenient exterior window, sewer tunnel, or
magic-adjacent shortcut to them.

### The Gradine

The Gradine occupies the compact apron from roughly `z = 105` to `157`, centred
on the west doors. Four shallow bands of steps follow the existing relationship
to the facade. The side edges are framed by low chapter property, pilgrim
sellers, petitioners, and narrow exits rather than identical colonnades.

It is ceremonial ground, counted apart from the five squares. It hosts
processions, petitions, licensed badge sellers, public recantations, court
announcements, and feast crowds. Its capacity is intentionally inadequate for
all Ombreval. The F.436 Shut Door crush makes physical sense because the west
doors, seller pitches, step edges, and side exits create pressure points when
the crowd is stopped from entering.

On ordinary days the space is busy rather than grandly empty: wax drips,
petitioners wait, vergers sort groups, maintenance carts cross early, and
licensed vendors compete for the final step. No central fountain obstructs
processions. No green exterior light commemorates the phenomenon.

### The Dawn Bearer and Seraph approach

The preserved Dawn Bearer at `(-72, 190)` and Seraph at `(72, 190)` stand on
opposite sides of the final west approach. Their immediate twenty-four-metre
vicinities stay open, but the two do not create a second vast square.

Each statue has a side court backed by dense lodging houses and trade fronts.
From the Wool Gate, their upper silhouettes appear over roofs before the road
turns onto the final axis. Nearer, the approach passes between their courts but
keeps enough enclosure that thirty metres feels enormous. Street sellers use
the court edges; children climb only the lower plinth until chased away;
pilgrims use the statues as meeting points.

Their names and sculptural subjects may receive later lore. This guide does
not make either statue supernatural, a source of exterior impossible light,
or a replacement for one of the established saints.

### Skinners' Court

Skinners' Court lies north of the Gradine at approximately `(108, 122)`, behind
a row of narrow houses and entered through an offset covered opening. It is a
working and domestic court: scraping benches, bundled pelts, lye ash, washing
lines, shared cookspace, children, patched plaster, drains, and upper rooms
reached from external galleries.

The court is not directly exposed to the Great Rose. A gap between roofs gives
an oblique view of west-tower masonry and perhaps a sliver of tracery, never a
valid sightline through the glass from inside the Lanthorn. Its relation to the
Gradine is close enough for feast noise to enter but hidden enough that work
continues unseen.

Street lore says a rose-beam once fell here three streets from the church.
**Never confirm:** Nothing in lighting, particles, decals, NPC certainty, or
area logic may prove that tale. The court is compelling because ordinary life
occupies the alleged site without evidence.

## The Wickmarket and western quarter

### The Wickmarket

The Wickmarket is a bent, roughly trapezoidal square centred near `(-25, 355)`.
The Wool Gate road enters its west side, western approach traffic leaves its
east/north-east corner, the Draper's Reach begins north-east, and the Needle
disappears behind the south row. This makes it both a destination and a place
every west-side traveller crosses.

The north row holds Osanne Vell's stall at about `(18, 350)`, close enough to
observe arrivals from both the gate and Draper's Reach. Chandlers dip candles
under lean-tos around several stone hot-work islands. Other pitches sell wax,
tallow, wicking, honey, lamp fittings, sand pots, and rendered fat. The
chandlers' heavy pound is kept here. Cauldrons and open flame occupy controlled
gaps; houses still crowd the cooler edges.

The square should be read by smell and residue as much as props: honey and hot
fat in Highmarket, wax streaks on boards, soot above shutters, pale drippings
in paving joints, ladders and wick bundles, flies in summer. At Lamplight the
market contracts, shutters rise, and public lamps come on one by one. One is
left dark as Belwyn's lamp except on the eve of the Concurrence.

The Wickmarket's relation to the impossible light is material rather than
magical. It supplies the tallow used in the cold-beam test and the Lanthorn's
candles. No exterior candle behaves strangely.

### The Needle

The Needle begins in the Wickmarket's south back lanes around `(-75, 310)` and
works toward the Tallage through several inherited turns. The route as a whole
is narrow, but its famous midpoint near `(-162, 230)` is only about 1.1 metres
wide after F.371 encroachment. Bowed plaster and timber walls almost touch at
upper level. Scraped corners, a worn central tread, dripping eaves, tiny doors,
and a bricked cellar light explain the pinch.

It is a pedestrian shortcut, never a cart route. Porters turn sideways; two
people negotiate who yields; nobody can stand abreast or linger without
blocking a neighbour. Its exits are brighter but not visible from the
midpoint, which gives the route a social pressure absent from an ordinary
alley.

Ede's hiding place is behind the bricked cellar-light at the midpoint. Among
ordinary children's chalk, a small glazier's diamond can be placed high, low,
doorside, or struck through.

**Secret:** The diamond signals Gaunt Passage, Draper's Reach, Saint Maren's
crypt, or scatter. The Needle is never itself a meeting place.

### The Draper's Reach

The Draper's Reach occupies the long cloth-hall frontage between the
Wickmarket and Coswald's Yard, bending from about `(35, 320)` toward
`(210, 185)`. It is not a tunnel for its full length. Arcaded and roofed
sections alternate with two short open courts so rain, work, and the changing
parcels remain legible.

At street level, bolts of wool cloth, tally boards, porters, shutters, and the
fixed iron ell of Ombreval fill the gallery. The standard must be visible and
usable rather than decorative. Merchants can watch cloth measured from raised
counting rooms. At either end, the gallery opens clearly enough that a watcher
can see activity in the Wickmarket or Coswald's Yard without seeing both in a
single implausibly straight vista.

The arcade saved many people in the first moments of the Hammering, while
adjoining roofs, lofts, and upper walls failed over the crowd. A central bonded
wool store stayed dry, the fact preserved in the Long Book's grotesquely small
account of the disaster. Rebuilt bays use mismatched roof material and heavy
braces. Several upper rooms remain closed or serve as stores, and the cloth
trade in F.437 is real but smaller than the architecture around it.

Above is the tenter loft, reached by stairs at both ends. Louvres overlook the
gallery. An overhead bridge at the north end joins the neighbouring block, and
jettied roofs offer a difficult secondary escape. Cloth work ends at dusk;
light in the loft after dark is visible from Coswald's Yard.

**Secret:** The loft is a secondary Unwalled meeting site, used for small day
meetings. Public architecture must support both exits and the roof escape.

### Tenterhook Lane

Tenterhook Lane is now fixed as the open working lane south of the cloth halls,
running from about `(95, 220)` toward `(-35, 185)`. Fullers stretch cloth on
frames where sun and wind reach between lower sheds. The lane needs broad
working pockets without becoming another square.

Water-intensive fulling happens in tubs and workshops, not in an invented
canal. Used water is carried or drained through controlled culverts. Frames,
hooks, mallets, chalk, wet wool smell, and disputes over shade give the lane
its character. Its western end connects to Cinder Ward; its eastern/northern
steps climb toward the Draper's Reach.

## Coswald's Yard and builders' quarter

### Coswald's Yard

Coswald's Yard sits around `(255, 155)`, on rising northern ground between the
Stone Gate and the Lanthorn. It is the largest of the five squares but the
least empty. Rough and dressed stone, long timber racks, lime shelters,
scaffold poles, hoists, templates, cranes, banker benches, and an open tracing
floor divide the working ground.

The Stone Gate road enters from the north. Fabric Way leaves the south side,
framing intermittent views of the Lanthorn's scaffolded north-tower crown. The
Draper's Reach arrives from the west/south-west; Crookneck Lane leaves east;
Malt Passage cuts under the south-east edge. These routes allow stone and
timber to cross the yard without driving through mason benches.

The square's surface is compacted earth, stone chips, reused slabs, lime dust,
and a few durable paved runs beneath hoists. There is no decorative fountain
in the centre. Water is kept in covered work troughs and a well near the lodge.
Pale dust hangs in autumn light; hammer sound and shouted measures carry
farther than conversation.

Hammering repair remains one of the quarter's ordinary trades. Roof frames,
wall braces, stripped jetties, disputed party walls, and the Bench's shoring
marks give masons and builders work beyond the Lanthorn without making every
conversation about the cathedral.

The unfinished north tower is a permanent visual reproach. It should be
possible to stand at the lodge porch and see scaffold against the sky, while a
shift of a few metres lets timber racks or neighbouring roofs cut it off.

At Coswaldstide, work clears enough tracing floor for scaffold blessing and
guild assembly. The annual mason-glazier quarrel has room to become a brawl but
does not convert the entire yard into a festival arena.

### The masons' lodge

The lodge fronts the south side of the yard near `(205, 150)`. A deep porch
faces the working ground. Inside are benches, moulding profiles, templates,
apprentice places, locked charter chests, a meal room, and an upper chamber for
masters. The building expresses durable guild pride through excellent stone
work rather than aristocratic ornament.

The lodge's sightline to the north tower and its easy access to Fabric Way are
essential. Warden Hamel Stott can move between lodge, yard, and cathedral
works without crossing the cloth halls. The F.288 grievance is social memory,
not a haunted architectural effect.

### Coswald's Yard first-build brief

Coswald's Yard is the first place intended for authored construction after
this planning step. Its initial build should stay within the rough envelope
`x = 195..315`, `z = 105..205` and meet these requirements:

- a north entrance accepts stone and long timber from the Stone Gate road;
- a south entrance feeds Fabric Way without aiming a load at a cathedral door;
- the Draper's Reach arrives at the south-west edge;
- Crookneck Lane and Malt Passage leave on the east/south-east side;
- the masons' lodge occupies the south edge with its porch facing the yard;
- a clear heavy-load route survives between stone stacks, hoists, and benches;
- an open tracing floor, lime shelter, timber racks, crane, rough stone,
  dressed work, templates, apprentices, and waste chips all have working room;
- the scaffolded north-tower crown is framed from the lodge porch;
- no central fountain, symmetrical stall ring, generic landmark tower, or
  decorative canal survives from the prototype city; and
- the surrounding facades vary by parcel, height, age, and material rather
  than enclosing the square as one designed set.

For later area ownership, `coswalds_yard` should describe the public working
ground and approaches while excluding any independently accessible lodge
interior and the volume of Malt Passage. The final boxes must follow completed
geometry and remain non-overlapping.

### Malt Passage

Malt Passage is now fixed under the malt-house at the south-east edge of
Coswald's Yard around `(232, 112)`. It links a brewer's service lane to the
Fabric route. The low passage smells of warm grain and kiln smoke; sacks pass
overhead by hoist, and carts cannot take its tight inner turn.

Its name follows the city's rule: it is named for what stands above. It is an
ordinary shortcut and shelter, useful precisely because not every covered
passage in Ombreval hides a conspiracy.

### Crookneck Lane

Crookneck Lane is now fixed as the bent route from the builders' quarter toward
the Ilvane and Bell wards. It turns hard at approximately `(165, -15)` where an
old property neck forces traffic around two opposed gables. From above the two
bends resemble a crooked neck; at street level neither next reach is visible
until the turn.

Stone offcuts, malt carts, household stalls, and later metalworkers share the
lane. It is a useful eastward route for pedestrians but deliberately poor for
long Fabric loads, which must use the wider service ways.

## Cinder Ward

### Cinder Row

Cinder Row bends from the Wickmarket's south-east quarter toward the south
side of the Gradine, passing through about `(-135, 205)`. Before F.171 it was
Glassmakers' Row. Its rebuilt lower storeys are continuous stone with stout
party walls; upper floors return in timber and plaster because people rebuilt
what they could afford.

Workshop doors open onto furnace bays screened from the street by shutters.
Apprentices cut, lead, paint, and pack glass at cooler front benches. Sand,
fuel, ash, cullet, lead came, wrapped panes, handcarts, and quenching equipment
make it a production street rather than a shop display. Furnace glow is
restrained and material; coloured glass never glows by magic.

The glaziers' guildhall and Sparr workshops cluster near `(-140, 235)`. Their
procession route runs north/east along the Row, takes the final working bend,
and enters the Gradine from the side before aligning with the west doors. It is
long enough for citizens to join and narrow enough for wrapped panes to matter.

Practical fire precautions—sand, shutters, masonry, separated fuel, iron
hooks—repeat without looking modern. Smoke darkens eaves. Burn scars are
Ambrelle's kisses; the street remains silent if a stranger says glass
remembers.

### Burnt Court

Burnt Court is now fixed behind Cinder Row at `(-172, 232)`. The F.171 fire
destroyed its former name more thoroughly than its stone party walls. Rebuilt
rooms lean on blackened lower masonry around a small court reached through a
fire-scarred passage.

Today it holds cullet sorting, ash barrels, fuel accounts, cheap rooms, and
domestic washing. It is not an abandoned ruin. The name persists because
calamity defeated whatever people called it before, exactly as the naming
handbook requires.

## The Tallage and salt quarter

### The Tallage

The Tallage is the customs square where the River Cartway meets the dry Cut,
centred near `(-305, 90)`. It is elongated east–west along the former channel.
Freight arriving from the outer wharves is sorted into waiting, weighing,
bonded, released, and disputed space rather than dumped into one open plaza.

The square contains the toll-house, bonded warehouse, weigh-beams, chained
Tallage stone, sealed Gaudry weights, clerk shelters, cart queues, porter
stands, pawnshops, copyists, cask yards, and food sellers serving people who
cannot leave their goods. The Tally Bridge spans the Cut near the centre.
Lise Copp's pawnshop faces it from the east corner; Doctor Ferrant's house and
study stand just off that corner.

The Tallage's mood comes from valuation. People wait while someone else names
the worth of cloth, salt, labour, collateral, testimony, or a page. Sightlines
are good enough for officials to watch the beams and poor enough for business
to pass behind a shutter. Faces are careful because every object can become a
number.

On Lowmarket the Cut stays open to through carts down its middle. Stalls use
marked margins and must clear for a bell. The ground is dry: dust in good
weather, isolated cart-rut puddles after rain, never a canal.

### The Tally Bridge

The Tally Bridge is the F.247 stone overhead connection between toll-house and
bonded warehouse, centred around `(-305, 105)`. Its two arches face along the
Cut and divide passing traffic below. Clerks and sealed documents use the upper
passage; carts, porters, arguments, and sunset shadows use the arches.

The bridge must read as adapted water infrastructure. Springing stones and
blocked bank stairs remember the channel; warehouse doors and worn roadway
show the dry present. The arches create two honest shadows at dusk, an
important final stop on the pilgrimage of observation.

Lise Copp's shop sign and counter are visible from beneath or just east of the
bridge. The bridge is not itself a river crossing in F.437 and must never have
water, boats, mist, or a toll chain beneath it.

### Lise Copp's pawnshop

The pawnshop is a narrow deep-fronted house around `(-262, 43)`, facing the
bridge from the east corner. The public room has a strong counter, hanging
pledges, shuttered shelves, a scale, seals, cord, and a small back light. A
second room receives private valuations without becoming a fantasy thieves'
guild.

**Secret:** Stolen archive pages move outward to the Unwalled and inward to
the Grey Press across the same counter; dry money for Custody moths is
laundered here. Lise prices pages by seal, hand, and fear because she cannot
read. The architecture supports discretion but does not announce it.

### Doctor Ferrant's house and study

Ferrant keeps house off the Tallage's east corner near `(-242, 20)`. Patients
enter at street level. His study occupies a better-lit upper room with a roof
platform screened by parapet and neighbouring gables. Plumb lines, sighting
rods, copied charts, lenses, a quadrant, candle pellets, correspondence, and a
worktable make inquiry physical.

The Lanthorn's west window need not be visible from the study; most important
experiments happen inside the cathedral. The route from his door to the
Tallage, Tally Bridge pawnshop, and Lanthorn is short and public.

### Gaunt Passage and the old Gaunt house

The old Gaunt house stands north/east of the Tallage around `(-218, 18)`. The
salt-factor line is extinct, but salt merchants still use its thick, vented
cellars. Salt bloom, bulging masonry, barrow scars, sack hoists, and a lawful
flow of odd-hour deliveries make the building's survival plausible.

Gaunt Passage runs under and behind it. Both mouths open off different
doglegging lanes, and neither can see the dark midpoint or the other mouth. An
overhead bridge crosses the western lane one house short of its entrance,
giving a watcher both approaches in separate glimpses. The passage is blind to
observation, not literally a dead end.

At the midpoint is a barred grate in Grigor Ashe's cellar wall. The grate is
within arm's reach of the public passage. Beyond, the cellar connects to a
bonded weighing yard around `(-195, -2)` whose two cart gates open onto
different doglegging lanes. These relationships are required for the Whisper
Arrests history, lawful salt traffic, overhearing risk, and escape.

**Secret:** The grate is the Unwalled's primary meeting door and Grigor Ashe is
the Wicket. Do not add occult decoration, green lamps visible from outside, or
a conveniently soundproof vestibule. A whisper risks every person within
ordinary hearing distance.

## The Cut

The Cut runs almost straight from the Chain Bridge quarter near `z = 465` to
the Old Sluice near `z = -610`, centred around `x = -305`. It is simultaneously
a street, a district, and the former bed of the Serle. Its straightness is
strange only because the rest of Ombreval bends.

The central cartway stays clear. Buildings along the old banks stand on
slightly raised thresholds and face the street with warehouse doors, blocked
water stairs, cellar vents, hoists, awnings, counting rooms, and shallow later
shops. Overhead bridges compress long views. Encroachment varies, but no
dogleg erases the line.

The western Cut carries rope, wool, hides, grain, and slaughter traffic. The
Tallage segment carries bonded freight and clerks. The eastern Cut grows more
domestic and fish-oriented through Maren's Green, then poorer and quieter near
the Old Sluice.

In F.437 it is entirely dry. Historical water marks should be architectural,
not a perpetual wet shader.

### The Chain Bridge

The Chain Bridge is now fixed near the western end of the Cut at
`(-305, 425)`. Its working name comes from the former harbour chain-house, not
from a saint. The water chain is long gone. The remaining bridge carries a
narrow upper store and keeper's passage between two old bank buildings.

Unlike the monumental stone Tally Bridge, it combines stone abutments with a
heavily renewed timber middle. Rope-makers and wool carriers use the dry arches
and adjoining yards. A surviving chain wheel or wall socket may show former
work; no chain bars present traffic and no active harbour lies beyond.

### The Old Sluice

The Old Sluice closes the east end of the Cut around `(-305, -610)`, just
inside the eastern wall system. It is a dry stone gatehouse of multiple heavy
arches, blocked side apertures, worn lifting grooves, removed gate machinery,
rusted iron, and buildings pressed against later repairs.

One arch carries pedestrians and small handcarts toward Harne Gate roads. The
former water bed ends in filled, compacted ground rather than an opening onto a
view of river. A keeper occupies part of the upper structure and rakes candle
stubs from the dry grate after Colm's Night.

Colm Attergate drowned at the sluice while the Cut still ran. Wyn Alder guides
pilgrims to one arch, which is not the true arch. **Secret:** the Alder family
keeps the true location as trade knowledge. **Never confirm:** No spirit,
voice, green reflection, or environmental effect marks the true arch.

## Maren's Green and the Reed Ward

### Maren's Green

Maren's Green occupies a linked set of market spaces around `(-305, -365)`,
where the east Cut, River Gate branch, churchyard, fish hall, Moorings gate,
and Maren's Slip converge. It is called a green by inheritance, not because it
is a lawn. Packed earth, dry paving, fish slabs, smoke racks, stall boards, and
cart wear cover most of it.

Fish and eels arrive from the outer wharves by handcart and cart. Boat-families
sort baskets, rope, poles, canvas, freight, and news here even though no boat
can enter the city. Eel smoke catches late-summer sunlight and makes the
church tower appear and disappear. Market geometry must preserve a view from
the Eel Bridge rail to the charnel-door lintel.

On Lowmarket, a laden cart approaching the bridge stair can halt pedestrian
movement for a moment. This is a designed piece of congestion, not a collision
bug. People wait, trade remarks, read the chalk across the yard, or take a
longer route.

The Great Rains and the arrests that followed remain inherited parish memory
here; only Ombreval's oldest inhabitants retain childhood fragments of F.362.
The Hammering remains living memory. The square's ordinary work must be strong
enough that neither history turns the place into a memorial set.

### The Church of Saint Maren of the Reeds

Saint Maren's is a low parish church north of the old Cut around
`(-235, -382)`. Its modest tower holds Maren Smallvoice. The church is visibly
older and poorer than the Lanthorn: patched fieldstone, limewash, reused roof
timber, a small porch, alms rooms, and practical additions built by
boat-families.

The churchyard lies between church and Green. Graves are close, paths narrow,
and the surface dry. The charnel door around `(-276, -365)` faces the market;
its lintel can be read obliquely from the Eel Bridge without a person lingering
at the threshold. The single current given name is chalked plainly and changes
with the next burial.

The Hammering overwhelmed individual name-knells and left many dead with only
the Chapter's common toll. Families still bring names omitted from the
Hammering rolls and pay for belated knells; “one bell is not a name” is heard
most often here. This practice uses the existing churchyard and bell rather
than requiring a monumental disaster shrine.

The crypt lies low beneath the eastern church and yard. Beaten earth and lime
show footprints, so anyone concealing a gathering must rake them smooth. The
charnel stair is lawfully open after burials and closed at other times. The
name-knell is audible throughout the Green and much of the Reed Ward.

The church remains a parish, burial ground, poor-relief institution, and
boat-family landmark. Scenes should include bread, accounts, sweeping, grave
work, children, quarrels over fees, and ordinary services, not only the cell.

**Secret:** After a pauper's burial funded by the Unwalled, the newest chalked
name becomes the pass. Burial-day meetings may occur in the crypt. Sexton Noll
Fitch understands what the lintel is read for and keeps silent on purpose.

### The Alder Moorings

The Alder Moorings is a dry warehouse court south/east of the Green around
`(-367, -407)`. A broad arched gate admits handcarts. Timber galleries, loft
doors, hoists, rope racks, canvas repair, fish baskets, freight bales, poles,
and a rough ledger table fill the yard.

The family kept the name after the water left because the business and memory
remained. The court has no mooring posts, quay edge, boat basin, wet paving, or
river reflection. It is an inland transfer yard whose workers spend part of
their day at the actual outer wharves.

An upper gallery joins the Eel Bridge system. The yard has a second service
door into Eelback Alley, important for morning loading and family privacy.

### The Eel Bridge

The Eel Bridge is a low enclosed timber gallery over the fish-market lane near
`(-343, -374)`. It joins upper rooms of the fish hall and Alder warehouse
complex. Its external stair occupies too much of one side of the lane, leaving
just enough clear width for a laden cart.

The small stair landing and bridge rail give a clean oblique view of Saint
Maren's charnel lintel. Pedestrians can read the current name without standing
at the churchyard door. A grey clerk can therefore watch either the lintel or
the watchers from the same place.

It is a bridge over a dry street, never over water. Its age shows in replaced
oak, sag, tar, fish scales, soot, and hand-worn rails.

### The Hungry Ox

The Hungry Ox stands on the south/east edge of the Green near
`(-330, -447)`. It is a broad-fronted, low-ceilinged boatmen's tavern with a
public room, small kitchen, yard, loft lodging, and doors placed to serve both
the Green and Eelback Alley.

Prices are working prices. Boatmen, porters, fish sellers, wharf hands, and
their households dominate; prosperous merchants come only when business
requires it. Stew, ale, smoke, steaming cloaks, rushes, rope, poles, and scarred
tables matter more than picturesque bottles.

In the evening, “The Water Knows One” spills roughly thirty metres into the
lane. Grey coats can quiet or sharpen the chorus without producing a scripted
magic effect. The sign may show an underfed ox pictorially but need not carry
readable words.

### Tanners' Slip

Tanners' Slip runs behind and south of Maren's Green, centred around
`(-390, -345)`. It is a cramped working dogleg of hide scraping, trimming,
stretching, bark tannin, closed barrels, covered empty vats, sheds, and patched
firebreak masonry left after the F.157 tannery fire.

The route is completely dry in ordinary depiction. It does not descend toward
water, open onto a waterfront vista, or contain picturesque flowing drains.
Sharp smells and stained labour are present without graphic gore. A blind turn
and service courts make it a place market visitors avoid unless they have
business.

An empty brine-rotted cellar near `(-415, -405)` is reached from its back end.
**Secret:** A twice-wrong whisperer may be fed a false meeting here precisely
because nobody the cell loves uses the place.

### Maren's Slip

Maren's Slip is now fixed as the steep pedestrian and handcart way from the
Green toward the Reed Postern, from about `(-375, -435)` to `(-455, -535)`.
Its name attaches Saint Maren to a walkway as the naming handbook requires.

The slip uses shallow ramps, a few side steps, retaining walls, handholds, and
resting ledges. Fish carriers can climb it from the postern, but ox carts must
use the River Gate. Its final turn hides the wall opening from the Green and
hides the river from inside the city.

### Eelback Alley

Eelback Alley is now fixed behind the Hungry Ox and smokehouses around
`(-370, -445)`. Uneven fill over old bank foundations gives its paving a long
hump like an eel's spine. Kitchen doors, fuel stacks, smokehouse vents, stable
gates, tavern refuse, and the Moorings service door face it.

The physical shape earns the name. It is an ordinary service route and a
useful alternative when the Eel Bridge stair blocks the market lane.

## The Bellstand and eastern quarter

### The Bellstand

The Bellstand lies east of and behind the Lanthorn's east end, centred around
`(45, -255)`. Bell Way enters from the cathedral quarter through a compressed
approach; the Harne Gate road leaves east; Crookneck Lane arrives from the
north. The square slopes slightly east and is smaller than its public role
suggests.

The old watch-bell tower stands off-centre on the north/east edge around
`(62, -270)`. The Scold is visible in its open upper stage. A steep external
stair descends over Bellfoot Passage. The crier's worn platform, posting wall,
watch shelter, and a stone edge for listeners create a civic focus without a
central ornamental fountain.

Edicts are cried and posted here. Windows, passage mouths, stairs, and shop
fronts provide many listening positions within ordinary speech range. The
Scold's summons reaches farther; the crier's actual words require people to
gather close.

Colm Attergate preached here that the towers were coffin lids. A worn stone at
the tower door is shown as Colm's stone. **Never confirm:** The identification
is unattested. It receives no special light or physical response.

### Bellfoot Passage

Bellfoot Passage runs directly beneath the watch-tower's steep external stair
near `(55, -248)`. Thick civic masonry, timber bracing, worn notices, guard
traffic, and the bright square framed at its exit give the passage its
identity.

It looks ideal for secrecy and is not. Watch and Custody eyes are permanent,
approaches are exposed from the stair, and proclamations keep people nearby.

**Secret:** The Unwalled have never used it. This false friend should tempt an
observer without containing a hidden grate, code mark, or alternate vault.

## The Ilvane Chapel and anchorhold

The Ilvane Chapel stands north-east of the Lanthorn around `(175, -92)`, where
Crookneck Lane and a smaller east–west lane meet. It is close enough for the
Custody's architectural punishment to feel deliberate, but not inside the
cathedral precinct.

Doors and public windows are visibly mortared. Cracks, buttressing, patched
roof, weathered sacred carving, and reused boundary walls support the official
claim of structural danger. The building is neither a romantic ruin nor an
open dungeon. Neighbours use the lane and walls as they do any stubborn old
property.

The occupied anchorhold is built into the north wall near `(193, -92)`. Its
small squint opens north onto the lane. An alms shelf, basket, rain hood, worn
standing stone, and space for two quiet visitors let Dame Aldith speak and
receive necessities without revealing most of her body or cell.

The nameless day can bring unobtrusive offerings to the lane. No apparition,
second shadow, or confirmed miracle occurs here. **Secret:** The oral
Glazier's Vespers passes from anchoress to anchoress, but the architecture does
not resolve whether it preserves Idonea Sparr's words.

## Cross-city relationship checklist

A later build of these places is spatially wrong if any of the following fail:

- Coswald's Yard cannot frame the north-tower scaffold.
- The Draper's Reach does not genuinely connect the Wickmarket side to
  Coswald's Yard.
- The Needle does not offer a plausible Wickmarket-to-Tallage shortcut.
- Cinder Row's procession cannot reach the Gradine carrying fragile panes.
- River freight must pass the Lanthorn to reach the Tallage or Maren's Green.
- The Tally Bridge is depicted over water.
- The Eel Bridge rail cannot see the charnel lintel.
- Saint Maren's crypt lacks a lawful burial-day approach.
- Gaunt Passage has only one usable mouth, a straight sightline, or no cellar
  escape.
- Tanners' Slip reveals an in-city waterfront.
- Bellfoot Passage contains a true secret meeting site.
- Skinners' Court confirms the rose-beam tale.
- The Ilvane anchorhold's squint opens in a wall other than the north wall.
- The Old Sluice is not at the east end of the dry Cut.
