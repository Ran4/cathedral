# New Places and City Infrastructure

> These additions complete the city around the established lore. Their names
> are fixed by this plan unless an entry explicitly says it is a reservation.
> They should remain practical background to the older named places.

## Why add anything

The established places describe trade and belief unusually well, but a 1.2 by
1.0 km fortified city also needs controlled entrances, food stores, waste
work, water, fire-prone industry, ordinary congregations, and a clear
relationship to roads outside. Without them the named squares become isolated
sets rather than parts of one material economy.

The additions below answer those needs with as little new nomenclature as
possible.

## The gates

### The Wool Gate

The Wool Gate is the western gate at approximately `(-35, 510)`. Wool from
Brede and the Combs, honey, hides, upland food, western pilgrims, and ordinary
road traffic enter here. Its two squat towers are broad enough for guard rooms
and upper storage, not cathedral-scale keeps.

Inside is a holding wedge where pack trains can be counted without blocking
the road. Cheap lodging, fodder, harness repair, brokers, and food stalls crowd
the first bend. The route then enters the Wickmarket, so gate traffic supports
the chandlers without turning their square into a customs yard.

The gate is not aligned with the Lanthorn. A traveller sees tower crowns and
statue silhouettes, loses them behind the Wickmarket row, and regains the west
front only after Pilgrim Bend. This staged arrival is a central first-person
experience.

### The Stone Gate

The Stone Gate is the northern gate at approximately `(495, 135)`. Quarry
stone, lime, scaffold timber, charcoal, grain from northern farms, and the land
road toward Ostrelle use it. Its inner road descends directly to Coswald's Yard
with wide corners, reinforced paving at braking points, and no steps.

The largest timber cannot pass under a decorative barbican full of sharp
turns. The defense therefore uses paired outer and inner doors with a straight
but steeply controlled throat. A side wicket and inspection shelter keep foot
traffic away from moving loads.

Beyond the wall, the Lantern Road branches from local quarry and timber roads.
Ombreval residents still describe Ostrelle as six weeks away through two
toll-gates; this plan fixes only the first direction, not the distant route.

### The Harne Gate

The Harne Gate is the eastern gate at approximately `(15, -665)`. It serves
the downstream land road toward Harne and Salorge, messengers, carriers,
pilgrims arriving by road rather than river, and eastern farms.

Inside, the road doglegs past wall housing and Bellfounders' Yard before
reaching the Bellstand. The Old Sluice lies south of that route, clearly inside
the wall and at the east end of the Cut. Neither gate nor road opens a view of
water through the Old Sluice.

The gate inherits Harne's name because that is the first consequential place
on its road, just as practical gates often do. It does not imply that Harne
controls Ombreval.

### The River Gate

The River Gate at approximately `(-505, -135)` is already implicit in core
lore and receives its fixed site here. It is the city's principal freight
gate. The outer wharves, north-bank apron, toll shelter, animal holding,
stable yards, porters, and customs messengers all focus on it.

The paired leaves admit a laden cart; a smaller wicket stays useful when the
main gate is shut. The gate throat must allow inspection and temporary closure
without creating a huge square. Inside, the River Cartway climbs gently to the
dry Cut and splits. Westbound freight reaches the Tallage; eastbound fish and
boat-family goods reach Maren's Green.

At flood warning the gate can be closed against people and goods without
pretending the wall keeps all water out. Vhairé Farcall reports conditions at
the wharves; it does not activate an in-city water simulation.

### The Reed Postern

The Reed Postern at approximately `(-455, -535)` is a narrow south-east wall
opening for eastern wharf hands, fish baskets, handbarrows, funerals, and Reed
Ward residents. One animal may be led through; an ox cart may not.

Maren's Slip turns twice between postern and Green. From inside, the last wall
turn hides the Serle. From outside, the postern is a work entrance in flood-
scarred masonry rather than a romantic riverside portal. Customs officers can
close it on Lowmarket when too much untallied cargo attempts the shortcut.

## The wall and its inner lane

The fortification is a continuous civic work, not an ornamental border. Wall
height varies from about twelve to fifteen metres; gate and corner towers rise
to roughly eighteen to twenty-four. Stone changes at repair campaigns. Old
crenellations may be filled, wall walks roofed in exposed stretches, and
timber hoardings stored rather than permanently mounted.

An inner lane remains mostly passable for watch, repair carts, fire response,
and dung collection. It narrows where poor leases and workshops encroached but
never disappears for hundreds of metres. Small gardens, timber stacks, and
animal pens use awkward triangles of defensive margin. Houses may lean onto
the wall only with visible agreements and later reinforcement; private postern
doors are exceptional and politically charged.

The four corner towers are landmarks from outside but should not all share one
model. Foundation, repair date, roof, stair, and attached use differ. None
receives a grand proper name in this guide.

## The outer Serle wharves

The working Serle lies outside the south wall. Its near bank and wharf strip
sit around `x = -570..-590`; navigable water lies farther south. The developed
wharves extend roughly from `z = 120` in the west to `z = -450` in the east.

Separate working reaches handle wool and general bales, salt casks, fish and
eels, timber rafts or poles, and passenger landings. Cranes are timber
treadwheel or lever machines; most transfer is human, animal, ramp, rope, and
handcart labour. Warehouses inside the wall remain useful because the bank is
exposed to flood, theft, and closure.

The Long Departure left fewer boat households to divide the work, not an empty
river. Some wharf leases and inner warehouses have passed to larger families;
others are held by absent heirs or used intermittently. The approaches should
show active freight alongside more capacity than present trade requires.

The strip also carries **the outlodge**: sheds, half-lofts over wharf shacks,
unlicensed drinking huts, and beds outside the wall, used by crews caught out
at the Snuffing. Because being gate-caught is normal, boat-families keep beds
out here as a matter of course; old boatmen spend more nights on the strip
than in the houses their grandmothers keep, which gives a boat-family its
split-household shape — men half-resident outside the wall, women, children,
and old wholly resident inside. The Bench cannot easily regulate the outlodge
because it is outside; the Church dislikes it; everyone uses it.

This is lore geography and a horizon/through-gate asset, not part of the
current playable walled city. A future expansion may build it, but no present
route should let the player accidentally wander from the Reed Postern into a
half-made river zone.

The Serle reflects one ordinary sun. Every route back inside reinforces that
the impossible light belongs to one interior window only.

## Seven Lofts

Seven Lofts is a defended granary compound near `(360, 335)`, between the Wool
and Stone gate routes. Its fixed name comes from seven tall roofed storage bays
built in different campaigns around a shared weighing yard. The bays need not
be identical, and residents argue whether a rebuilt eighth bay counts.

Grain enters from farms and western carriers, is dried, sampled, turned,
guarded, and released to bakers and institutions. Raised floors, rat ledges,
vented walls, hoists, sealed doors, sweepings, grain dust, cats, and fire rules
make storage visible. The Chapter rents space here but does not own all city
food.

The F.183 memory that the Chapter opened its granary late can attach to rented
or reserved bays without placing every grain store inside the cathedral. Seven
Lofts gives scarcity, rationing, release-order, and bargaining stories a physical destination.

Hammering repairs should be legible in roof trusses, replaced shutters, and
one rebuilt bay. F.415 destroyed both city stores and the standing late-summer
crop, making this compound a relief centre, a guarded asset, and a later source
of argument over who received grain first.

## The Shambles

The Shambles is fixed near `(-395, 315)`, south of the western Cut and down-
slope from the Wickmarket. It grows from the already established historical
reference to wolves crossing frozen Serle ice into the shambles in F.38.

Butchers' stalls face a broad working lane; slaughter courts, renderers,
bone-workers, hide sorting, covered drains, pens, and dung collection occupy
the rear. The place is busy, regulated by nuisance and custom, and unpleasant
without graphic spectacle. Tallow moves north to the Wickmarket; hides move
toward Tanners' Slip or westward carriers; meat moves to households,
bakehouses, taverns, and institutions.

The Shambles sits near the west Cut and wall routes so animals and waste do not
cross the Gradine. It has water from a dedicated well and carried barrels, not
from an in-city stream. Its relation to the Wickmarket explains part of the
chandlers' supply without turning the square itself into a slaughter yard.

## Bellfounders' Yard

Bellfounders' Yard is fixed near `(155, -485)` in the east ward, close to the
Harne road and wall. Casting needs clay, wax, charcoal, metal, pits, lifting,
and a way to haul heavy bells; testing needs neighbours willing or unable to
complain. The site is therefore accessible but away from the Lanthorn and
principal markets.

The yard contains a casting house, covered pit, mould shed, charcoal store,
metal lockup, lifting frame, and open hard ground. Small domestic metal work
continues between rare large castings. Saint Perrin Halfbell's patronage is
visible in a small workshop image, not a fourth great church.

The Lanthorn bells were cast in earlier forms of this trade district. A bell's
journey from casting pit to tower requires rollers, sledges, capstans, prepared
streets, and many hands. Keeping the yard east of the city makes that journey a
real civic undertaking.

## Ford Well

Ford Well is fixed near `(88, 35)`, north-west of the Lanthorn crossing ground
and outside its preserved footprint. It marks the practical survival of the old ford-
market without claiming that the former Serle channel remains beneath an open
shaft.

The well has a roof, windlass, stone curb, trough, drain apron, queue space,
and a keeper's niche. Its water comes from a deep groundwater source. Fabric
workers, nearby households, petitioners, and street sellers use it. On feast
days vergers control the queue so it does not block cathedral service routes.

Saint Vhairé may appear on a worn relief because the ground is associated with
her crossing. No lamp remains miraculously dry and no water reflects a second
sun.

## Parish reservations

Core lore establishes three named sacred places inside Ombreval: the
Lanthorn, Saint Maren's, and the shut Ilvane Chapel. It also says other
parishes exist. A city of this extent needs them, but naming all of them during
a spatial pass would create shallow canon.

Four sites are therefore protected from dense redevelopment:

1. west parish reserve around `(105, 430)` for gate households, chandlers,
   wool carriers, and lodging keepers;
2. north-east reserve around `(330, -300)` for wall households, carters, and
   small metal trades;
3. west Cut reserve around `(-220, 395)` for rope-makers, Shambles workers,
   porters, and poor tenants; and
4. River Ward reserve around `(-420, -35)` for gate workers, wharf porters,
   stable hands, and freight families.

Each future parish needs a congregation, income, burial arrangement, feast,
and material relation to its ward before it receives a saint and proper name.
They should not all concern the Great Rose. Until then, the reserves may hold
an unnamed small church, yard, priest's rooms, and poor-relief space in world
planning, but must not appear in NPC prompts as newly named lore.

## Wells, cisterns, ovens, and fire

Besides Ford Well, each major ward needs at least one public well or cistern
within roughly one hundred and fifty metres of homes. Their names are left
local and mutable. A later prop pass should place them before decorative
fountains.

Communal bakehouses cluster near grain routes and ward edges. Private ovens
exist but fire risk and fuel economy make shared ovens common. Breweries need
malt, water, fuel, cooling, carts, and waste; the malt-house above Malt Passage
belongs to that network.

Fire response uses hooks, ladders, buckets, sand, demolition tools, wells, and
the bell code. The Ruin means a burning quarter or flood emergency; Coswald
Ironthroat alone calls help to a local breach or fire. Streets wide enough for
a cart are not automatically wide enough for a panicked crowd and ladder crew.

## Waste, animals, and service routes

Dung, ash, tannery residue, fish waste, broken pots, building rubble, and
night-soil do not vanish. Collection carts use the Cut, River Cartway, and
inner wall lane at regulated hours. Useful matter is sold: dung to gardens,
ash to trades, bone and horn to workers, rags to paper and stuffing. Illegal
dumping still fills alleys.

Stables concentrate at gates, the Tallage, and freight yards. Small animals
live everywhere. Pigs should not wander through every square as generic
medieval dressing; their ownership and nuisance matter. The Gradine and
Lanthorn precinct are swept more often because the Church can pay for it.

## Markets are days as well as places

Highmarket is the third day, chiefly at the Wickmarket and Coswald's Yard.
Lowmarket is the sixth day, chiefly at the Tallage and Maren's Green. Neither
word names an additional square. The line “the salt's weighed at Lowmarket”
means on the market day and at its customary weighing places, not at a missing
location called Lowmarket.

Stalls outside those four squares continue on ordinary days at lower density.
The Bellstand gathers proclamations rather than a weekly commodity market.
The Gradine licenses pilgrim sellers but is not one of the five.

## Roads beyond the walls

This plan fixes which way common travellers leave Ombreval without attempting
to map the wider world in playable coordinates.

- Brede and the Combs are reached first by the western road through the Wool
  Gate. Brede remains three days' pole upstream by river; the land road need
  not match that time exactly.
- Harne and downstream road country lie east through the Harne Gate. Harne is
  four leagues below the city beside the Serle.
- Salorge is normally reached by river from the outer wharves, six days down
  and twelve back, although an eastern land route exists.
- Ostrelle is reached by the Lantern Road, leaving through the Stone Gate and
  parting from local roads beyond the wall. It remains six weeks and two
  toll-gates away.
- The outer wharves connect to the whole Serle from the Combs to the salt pans.

Nothing in this directional plan puts a second impossible phenomenon outside
Ombreval. Every road and all water beyond the walls know one sun.

## Civic buildings without a settled constitution

The Bellstand needs posting rooms, watch shelter, lockup space, crier storage,
and chambers where an edict is copied before it is cried. The Tallage needs
customs administration and records. The walls need ward captains and stores.
Those functions can occupy buildings around their existing places.

This guide deliberately does not create a palace, royal keep, mayor's castle,
or dominant council hall. Core canon leaves Ombreval's secular constitution
open. A later politics feature may establish who governs and where they meet,
but it must grow from the Bellstand, Tallage, guilds, wards, and Church rather
than assuming the Praelucent is temporal monarch.

## New-place restraint

Future writers may add the name of a court, tavern, well, bridge, or workshop
when a story needs it. Each addition should answer four questions:

1. What material work or event gave it that name?
2. Which existing route feeds it?
3. Who maintains, owns, rents, or resents it?
4. What waste, noise, traffic, or obligation does it impose on neighbours?

A place that answers none of those is likely a label attached to scenery, not
part of Ombreval.
