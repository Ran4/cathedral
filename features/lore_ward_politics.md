# Ward Politics

The eight tax wards and their annual reckonings could become the city's main layer of everyday political identity.  Each ward would have a reputation it claims as a virtue and its neighbors repeat as an insult, plus one or two current material grievances.  Reed might fight to keep its postern open on Lowmarket, Cinder might demand stricter cistern and furnace rules, Cloth might oppose new cart fees, and Wallwright might resent paying for drains in lower ground.  Candidates for the two bencher seats could seek support through meals, favors, kin, credit, guild pressure, and visible service rather than modern speeches or permanent parties.  A local custom might require candidates to sit on a plain public bench while householders state complaints to their faces, giving “standing for the Bench” an appropriately literal inconvenience.

For gameplay, the player could carry evidence, expose a concealed interest, repeat or correct campaign gossip, broker support, or simply discover that helping one ward disadvantages another.  Election results need only change small, legible things—gate hours, stall licences, watch routes, shoring orders, well repairs, or enforcement of the Cut game—to make civic life feel responsive.  An NPC's ward, creditors, household, and trade would provide understandable reasons for political opinions without assigning everyone a modern ideological label.  Recurring reckonings would also create public gatherings and evolving disputes that belong to the Common Bench rather than the Church.
