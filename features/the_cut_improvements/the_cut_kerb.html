The measure

The Cut is not a big street. It is a different category of object — and it is currently drawn like a lane that got away from itself.

Panel 2  ·  plan

What the line makes possible

Sixty metres of the Cut from directly above. North is up the page, so east is on the left — the world’s compass is left-handed.

  1. The freight cart holds the cartway. The lane is for it, and it must never be blocked. Everything else goes round — and going round is now possible.
  2. Porters and handbarrows move in the margin, past the cart, both legal, neither in the other’s way.
  3. A stall board pitched on the margin, inside the line. legal The trader keeps the pitch and knows exactly where it ends.
  4. The same trader’s barrels spill over the kerb. obstruction The sergeant’s beat arrives — raise_notice. The law can finally see a rule being broken, because there is finally a rule with an edge.
  5. A hoop-poling lane. The only street straight enough to roll it, down the camber, inside the kerb — and it blocks the cartway, which is exactly why enforcing it is an election issue.
  6. A blocked water stair and a mooring ring set in the kerb. The Serle is gone; the ironwork is not.

Why this only works here

Every other street in the city fails the test. In an ordinary 4 m Ombreval lane the whole lane is the cartway — a kerb would leave a metre of margin nobody can stand in, and a cartway too narrow for the cart it was drawn to protect.

A kerb needs surplus width before it means anything. The Cut is the only street in the city that has 8–12 m of surplus, and it has that surplus for one reason: it used to be a river.

Panel 4  ·  the ledger

Cheap geometry, large consequences

One extrusion and four props. No new verbs, no new behaviour.

What it costs

All static geometry

  • One extruded kerb strip along the existing road centreline, offset ±5 m.
  • One raised margin surface, 0.25 m, from kerb to housefront.
  • A step at the housefronts — the bank threshold, 0.40 m.
  • About four repeating margin props: blocked stair, mooring ring, cellar hatch, bollard.
  • No new sim verbs. No new NPC behaviour. The cast already walks this street.

What it unlocks

All of it downstream of one line

  • The freight cart has a lane worth defending.
  • Obstruction becomes a visible, playable offence for the sergeant beat that already walks here and currently does nothing.
  • Hoop-poling gets a course — and a reason to be illegal.
  • The soundings become readable off the ground itself: no UI, no journal, no marker.
  • The street stops reading as a 20 m car park.

One line of stone, and every other idea for the Cut gets somewhere to happen.
