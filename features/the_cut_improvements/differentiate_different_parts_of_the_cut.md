Give each reach its own play

The Cut should not be equally packed end to end.

- The western reach gets bulk loads, hide and wool carts, wheel repairs, and an actually working ropewalk. The player can crank, carry hemp, or untangle a fouled line.
- The Tallage is bureaucratic congestion: queues, weighing, manifests, pawning, arguments, and inspections.
- The eastern middle gets frequent warehouse loading, coopers, laundresses, handbarrows, and the roaming Cut game.
- Maren’s Green remains the fish-and-smoke burst, especially on Lowmarket.
- The Old Sluice end stays poorer and quieter. Its gameplay comes from shored buildings, illicit meetings, the soundings, and occasional night gatherings—not constant
crowds.

The soundings could later become an excellent investigative job: walk a cracked property with Cobb Hawser or an Alder, determine whether it is built on the bed, then
sell, conceal, or falsify that knowledge. That turns settling walls and crooked doors into evidence rather than decoration.

Large calendar events—the Dry Race, Colm’s Night candles, a funeral procession, hard-rain freight trouble—would then transform an already functional street instead of
temporarily concealing an empty one.
