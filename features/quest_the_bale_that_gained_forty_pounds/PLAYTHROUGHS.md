# Forty Pounds Over — ten ways it goes

Ten worked routes from the gate stop to the aftermath, written to test the design rather than to
advertise it. Each names the bells, the people and the systems it uses, what goes wrong, and whether
the player can come back from it.

Storyboard panels for each are in `storyboards/NN_<slug>.png`, generated by
`generate_storyboards.py`.

Read them as a spread, not a ranking. The point of the set is that **four of them clear Hugh Crake
and no two of them leave the same city behind** — and that the two routes which fail (04 and 06) fail
for reasons the player caused and can still work with.

---

## 01 — The Proved Weights
**Lane:** lawful · **Outcome:** Hugh cleared by amendment; Clemence Hobbe named; Skell retaliates

![The Proved Weights — storyboard](storyboards/01_the_proved_weights.png)

**High Wick.** The player reaches the Wool Gate as the cart is being turned. Douce Fenn is holding
the ox's head; Colin Barley is counting bolts he cannot see. Hugh says the one thing that starts the
quest: the bale was on Tarn's beam two hours ago and it weighed what the paper says.

**Waning.** The player walks it backwards. At the Tallage, Ewart Tarn will not have his weighing
doubted by any factor in a good coat — but he *did* say the figure aloud, and Averil read it back,
and half the porter stand heard it. The player takes the names off `heard_by` rather than arguing
with Tarn.

**Waning, later.** The Cut. Bertran Hobbe, white to the eyebrows, hauling flour and telling the
miller joke. He proves his own sealed weights against the standards every Lowmarket with the square
watching, and he will bring them a day early for the pleasure of being asked. Getting Odo Trask to
attend costs the fee in the open book and nothing else; he cannot be bought and does not need to be.

**Lamplight.** The proving. The bale comes out of impound onto the beam, on Bertran's weights, before
Trask, and the beam says forty pounds over — which is the point. The bale really is heavy. What the
proving establishes is that the *standards* are sound and the *sealing figure* was honest, which
narrows the crime to the two hours between them.

**Snuffing.** The player pays the duty on forty pounds of unentered broadcloth and lodges an amended
manifest with Trask. Hugh's notice settles by amendment. But an entered bolt needs a weaver's name,
and the only truthful name is Clemence Hobbe's.

**Dayspring.** The opening is a formality read out to a small crowd.

**What goes wrong:** nothing, mechanically — and that is the cost. Ewart Skell is at the opening. He
now knows a bolt he did not put out came off a loom he pays by the ell. By the Waning he has cut what
the Reach pays, and every weaver on the Cut feels it.

**Aftermath:** the Brede cart runs. Six households are paid. Clemence Hobbe has her first customer in
her own name and has lost her cover for it. Renn Crake is never mentioned and walks away entirely.

## 02 — Three in One Room
**Lane:** social · **Outcome:** Renn exposed and runs; Hugh cleared; Clemence Crake takes her son's trade

![Three in One Room — storyboard](storyboards/02_three_in_one_room.png)

Averil Tarn's sheet already contains this quest's confrontation mechanic: *what you have not yet
worked out is how to get those two men into one room without the whole square hearing it.* The player
does it with three.

**Waning.** The player collects incompatible accounts rather than evidence. Hugh: the bale was sealed
and never opened. Renn, cornered at the Tally Bridge, laughing a half-beat early: he never touched it.
Clemence Hobbe, at the Needle, level and short: she has no cloth on that cart, which is true in the
only sense she permits herself.

**Lamplight.** The player puts them in the bonded weighing yard — two cart gates, poor sightlines,
and inside the 20 m hearing radius of each other and of nobody else. It is not a cutscene. It is
three scheduled people and a place.

**What happens:** the accounts cannot all stand. Clemence Hobbe learns her bolt travelled and that
Renn's promise was a theft. Hugh learns the cart was diverted. Renn learns that being watched is now
unavoidable, which is the one thing his sheet says he cannot bear — *you would sooner be ruined than
watched* — and he runs.

**What goes wrong:** Renn's flight raises a notice of its own and the warrant ladder starts climbing
on the man the player wanted to question. He also holds the only account of the diverted hour.

**Recovery:** the flight *is* the evidence. Warin Underbridge, watching the man who owes him leave the
city, stops protecting him and finally says what the beam's note told him at the warehouse door.

**Aftermath:** Hugh cleared at the opening. Renn loses his trade; Segwin Sedge picks up the loads.
Dunstan Skell forecloses on keys that were never Renn's to pledge, and that becomes Clemence Crake's
war with him — a woman who carries a struggling man three seasons and takes his shop in the fourth,
now pointed at the money-dealer across her own square.

## 03 — The Bolt That Was Never There
**Lane:** covert · **Outcome:** everyone cleared, nothing learned, the player holds the difference

![The Bolt That Was Never There — storyboard](storyboards/03_the_bolt_that_was_never_there.png)

**Lamplight.** The player never goes near the truth. The bale is in the bonded warehouse; the
warehouse has three sets of keys, and one of them belongs to a man who walks in and out of it all day
carrying other people's freight and cannot read the bill he does it on.

**Snuffing.** Inside. The cord is cut — already cut once, and re-laid by a hand that was in a hurry —
and the player takes out one bolt of broadcloth and re-lays it. Douce Fenn's cord cannot be
reproduced, but nobody at a public opening reads a cord unless someone asks them to.

**Dayspring.** The opening. Cord cut, contents counted, weight called. The bale matches its manifest,
because it does. `true_weight_lb` is a sum, and the player changed one of the terms.

**What goes wrong:** the player is standing in the Wickmarket at Dayspring holding forty pounds of
bulky broadcloth worth forty sparks that belongs to a weaver who cannot afford to claim it, and every
route to converting it runs through Ewart Skell, who is looking for exactly this cloth.

**Recovery:** give it back. Clemence Hobbe cannot be told how it was retrieved without learning her
bolt was stolen by the man she trusted — which is playable, and is how this route sometimes turns
into 02 a day late.

**Aftermath:** Hugh cleared publicly and the officers wear it. The gate's word was wrong. Renn Crake
gets away with it entirely and still has eleven weeks. Nobody in the city knows what happened, which
means nothing in the city changes — except that there is now an hour in the bonded warehouse's day
that nobody records, and the player knows about it.

## 04 — A Better Forgery
**Lane:** covert · **Outcome:** public failure at the opening; the notice turns on the player

![A Better Forgery — storyboard](storyboards/04_a_better_forgery.png)

**Waning.** The player works out that the seal is imitated before anyone else does, and draws the
wrong conclusion: that a *better* imitation solves it. Wax is six sparks at any chandler's pitch on
the Wickmarket. Warin Underbridge is on the porter stand when the player buys it and is delighted to
help carry it, because he is first to put his shoulder under anything anyone is struggling with.

**Snuffing.** A new seal on the bale, pressed clean, indistinguishable to any officer — because
nothing that reads a seal asks who pressed it. The same rule the chalk marks taught: a forged cross
refuses a stall exactly as hard as the ward's own.

**Dayspring.** The opening. Odo Trask is there, because Clemence Crake keeps him on retainer and she
has begun looking twice at her son's figures. Trask is the man who two winters back witnessed a seal
he could not place and chose not to look twice, and it has come to him at the Kindling bell every
morning since. He looks twice. He says so aloud, in conditions and exact words.

**What goes wrong:** everything, in public, at the worst moment. And Warin Underbridge, cheerful and
easily flattered and asked to remember, remembers the wax.

**Recovery:** the player is now accused, which is survivable — `notices.rs` climbs Word → Summoned →
Warranted at a documented pace, `custody.rs` has a tether and the Stone House has a posted fee and a
bell you are told you go at. Hugh's own notice is *helped* by this: the forgery is proof the bale was
tampered with by somebody, and Hugh was in the officers' sight the whole night.

**Aftermath:** Hugh cleared, by the player's mistake rather than the player's work. The player has a
notice, an enemy on the porter stand, and a notary who now looks twice at everything they touch for
the rest of the game.

## 05 — Skell Buys It
**Lane:** social · **Outcome:** the crime ceases to have happened; the weaver's independence dies

![Skell Buys It — storyboard](storyboards/05_skell_buys_it.png)

**Waning.** The player works out whose bolt it is and takes the fastest lawful route: if Ewart Skell
buys the bolt, it becomes his stock, entered against his own account, and there is no unentered cloth
in the bale at all. The crime stops existing retroactively.

**Lamplight.** The Draper's Reach. Skell lays every bolt against the standard ell with his own hands
because his father was cheated of nine ells once and died still counting them. He will buy it. He
buys it at his own reckoning, which is what he does to everything, and he asks one question the
player must answer or refuse: whose loom.

**Dayspring.** The opening finds a bale of Skell cloth in a Skell bale. Formality.

**What goes wrong:** nothing goes wrong. That is the trap. Clemence Hobbe's fleece, her month, her
one chance to sell work in her own name, has been converted into an ordinary week's putting-out at a
price Skell set — and she was not asked.

**Recovery:** none available. This is a deliberate design position: the smoothest route through the
quest is the one that quietly costs the person the quest was about. Nothing tells the player this. It
shows up as Clemence Hobbe speaking to them exactly as short and level as she speaks to Skell.

**Aftermath:** Hugh cleared, Renn untouched, six households paid on time, and one weaver back where
she started, working wool put out to her by a man who pays by his own reckoning — and now watching
her.

## 06 — Six Households at the Door
**Lane:** social, public pressure · **Outcome:** the bale opens early in a scrum; the evidence dies

![Six Households at the Door — storyboard](storyboards/06_six_households_at_the_door.png)

**Lamplight.** The player finds the pressure point that needs no investigation at all: six loom
households have lawful bolts inside a bale that is now evidence and will not be paid while it stays
one. That is not a mystery, it is a wage. The player says so at the Needle, at Tenterhook Lane, in
the Cinder Row.

**Snuffing.** They come. Not a riot — weavers, in the dark, at a warehouse door, saying a true thing
loudly. Officers with a crowd in front of a bonded door make the decision officers make.

**What goes wrong:** the bale is opened early and badly, by lamplight, with forty people pressing in.
The cord is cut by whoever is nearest and trampled. Petronel Roper never gets to read it. The seal
comes off in three pieces and Odo Trask is at home. The extra bolt is found in the worst possible
conditions: publicly, chaotically, and attached to nothing.

**Recovery:** real but harder. The physical evidence is gone; the human evidence is not. Warin
Underbridge still heard the beam. Ede of the Needle still carried the message. `heard_by` from the
morning weighing is still a list of people who were standing there. The quest becomes a pure
testimony problem with a day less to solve it.

**Aftermath:** the households are paid. Hugh is seized at Dayspring because there is nothing left to
clear him with, and the player has to get him out — surety, the Stone House, or the bench.

## 07 — The Penny and the Fright
**Lane:** investigative · **Outcome:** varies; documents the cheapest lead and how to lose it

![The Penny and the Fright — storyboard](storyboards/07_the_penny_and_the_fright.png)

**Waning.** Ede of the Needle is eleven, belongs to nobody, runs messages from the Needle to the far
side of the Bellstand for a penny, two if it climbs stairs. She carried every word between Clemence
Hobbe and Renn Crake, because Clemence Hobbe writes nothing down, ever. She is the fastest route in
the quest and she costs one spark.

**The good version.** The player pays her. She never gives away a word she was not paid for, but she
was paid, and she talks fast and cheeky and short, and she names both ends of the message. The player
has in ninety seconds what the physical evidence takes a day to establish.

**The bad version.** The player presses her, or reaches for her, or raises their voice, or arrives
with an officer. Her left arm healed crooked after the crush at the Shut Door. She goes quiet and
still — that is on her sheet, in those words — and she stays closed for the rest of the quest.

**What goes wrong:** the fright is permanent and there is no apology mechanic. There should not be.

**Recovery:** the other six evidence traces are all still there. The cord, the wax, the beam's note,
the count at the Reach, the spoken figure, the warehouse hour. Losing Ede costs a day, not the quest.

**Aftermath:** if she was paid and treated well, she is a message-runner the player can use for the
rest of the game — which is worth more than the lead was. If she was frightened, the player has
taught an eleven-year-old that adults with questions are dangerous, and the city contains one more
closed door that nobody else can see.

## 08 — Sold to the Man Across the Square
**Lane:** predatory · **Outcome:** the player is paid; Hugh carries it; the Brede cart stops

![Sold to the Man Across the Square — storyboard](storyboards/08_sold_to_the_man_across_the_square.png)

**Lamplight.** The player learns the truth and correctly identifies that it is an asset. Dunstan
Skell has broken money at the toll-house end of the Tally Bridge for twenty years, holds Renn's
pledge of his mother's warehouse keys, wants the freight brokers lending through him and not around
him, and fears one unpaid season more than he fears the grave. He will pay for this.

**Snuffing.** The player sells it. Or takes Renn's warehouse key as the price of saying nothing,
which is better, because a key is a route and a route lasts.

**Dayspring.** The opening happens without the player. The extra bolt is found. Hugh Crake, whose
obligation is exact and who would rather forgive a late gate than a vague tally, is seized in front
of the crowd for a thing he did not do.

**What goes wrong:** from the player's position, nothing. From the city's, a great deal.

**Aftermath:** `brede_wool_gate` does not run. Raw wool into Ombreval constricts, and it is felt at
the Draper's Reach within days and by every weaver Skell puts out to — including Clemence Hobbe,
whose independence the truth would have exposed and whose livelihood the silence destroys anyway.
Dunstan Skell forecloses on Clemence Crake's warehouse. The player has a key to it. Colin Barley and
Douce Fenn are in the city with no cart and no road.

## 09 — Late to the Opening
**Lane:** any · **Outcome:** the failure that is a second act

![Late to the Opening — storyboard](storyboards/09_late_to_the_opening.png)

The player spends day one on something else entirely, or on the wrong thread, or in the Stone House.

**Dayspring.** The opening happens. The player may or may not be in the crowd. Cord cut, contents
counted, weight called: one bolt of broadcloth over, on a bale sealed by the official weigher, with a
seal that Odo Trask — present, because Clemence Crake pays him to be — calls an imitation.

Hugh Crake is taken in charge for the Stone House.

**Why this is not a fail state.** Nothing about the quest's evidence expired. The cord is now cut and
in a heap, but Douce Fenn saw it; Warin Underbridge still heard the beam; the morning weighing's
`heard_by` list is unchanged; Ede still carried the message; Ewart Skell's count is still short by
nothing. What changed is that the question is no longer *what is in the bale* — it is *who broke
custody between the beam and the gate*, which is a better question and has the same answer.

**The second act:** surety (someone with standing pledges for Hugh — Clemence Crake can, and might,
because he is family and because her son's figures have been bothering her); a debt bargain with the
principal in Brede; proving the diverted hour from the custody copy at the Tally Bridge; or the fifth
door out of the Stone House, which `law_and_order` M5 already built and which costs what it costs.

**Aftermath:** whatever the second act reaches — but Hugh has been publicly taken, and a road trader
who has been taken at a gate is a road trader whose loads are searched at every gate afterwards.

## 10 — Nobody Came
**Lane:** none · **Outcome:** the null route, which must be playable and must be dull

![Nobody Came — storyboard](storyboards/10_nobody_came.png)

The player never engages. They hear a commotion at the Wool Gate at High Wick and walk the other way.

**Dayspring.** The bale is opened. The bolt is found. Hugh is seized. Renn Crake sleeps badly and
gets away with it. Clemence Hobbe waits for a bolt that never comes and does not dare ask where it
went. Ewart Skell hears there was unentered cloth in a bonded bale and starts, slowly, working out
whose loom.

**Why it is in the list:** because the quest must not wait for the player, and because the aftermath
of ignoring it has to be as legible as the aftermath of solving it. `brede_wool_gate` stops running.
The player will notice, three Highmarkets later, that a cart they never cared about is not at the
Wool Gate any more, and the reason will be a thing they walked away from.

There is no notification. There is no missed-quest entry. There is just one fewer cart.

Four clear Hugh. Two of those (03, 04) do it without anyone learning anything, which the design
permits on purpose. Two (06, 08) leave him taken. One (10) is the player not playing, and it still
changes the city.

**No two rows leave the same Ombreval.** That is the test the idea bank asks for, and it is the only
reason this suggestion was worth promoting.
