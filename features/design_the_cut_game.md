# Design the Cut game (together — parked for now)

**Status:** discussion doc, not ready to implement. The developer wants to figure this
out together when there's time; nothing here is canon yet.

## Why this file exists

`features/lore_ward_politics.md` mentions "enforcement of the Cut game" as one
of the small, legible things an election could change — and the phrase is
defined nowhere in the lore corpus. While reconciling `lore/the_dry_boatmen.md`
(July 2026) the obvious candidate was the **Dry Race** (the Marenstide
boat-on-a-cradle race down the dry Cut), and we explicitly decided **the Cut
game is NOT the Dry Race**. The Dry Race is a once-a-year feast event with its
own meaning; the Cut game should be its own, separate thing.

## What canon already constrains

- The Cut is dry, dead straight (the only straight street in a city that
  bends), 8–12 m of working cartway; the Bench issues obstruction notices.
- Gaming law (`core_lore/secular_government.md`): small-stakes public gaming is
  legal in licensed taverns/rooms; unlicensed large-stake play and lending
  against a player's person are offences; informal children's games need no
  licence.
- For "enforcement of the Cut game" to swing with ward politics, the game
  needs to be (a) played in the open street, (b) marginal enough that a bencher
  can plausibly crack down on it, and (c) loved enough that cracking down
  costs votes.
- No water, no river magic, no secret channel (`wells_and_water.md` is law).

## Seed sketches to react to (none of these is decided)

1. **Hoop-poling.** Barrel hoops rolled down the Cut's camber and steered with
   river poles; wagers on distance and lane. The Tarns supply hoops, and Gude
   Tarn already canonically "runs an illegal game" — this could be the one.
2. **Kerb-pitch.** Porters and carters pitching sparks against the Cut's
   kerbstones between loads. Enforcement = obstruction plus unlicensed stakes;
   utterly ordinary, which is its charm.
3. **Wet-or-dry.** A children's chase game where the "wet" line is the old
   channel and the "dry" is the bank — the soundings taught as a game, without
   anyone calling it that. Enforcement pressure would come from Cut landlords,
   who hate a game that publicly maps who is built on the bed.
4. Something eel- or fish-market-adjacent out of Maren's Green.

Open questions: who plays it, how often (ongoing, unlike the annual Dry Race),
what "enforcement" concretely looks like, and whether it belongs to one ward or
travels the whole length of the Cut through three of them.
