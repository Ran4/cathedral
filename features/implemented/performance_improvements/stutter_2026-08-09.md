# Stutter: what it actually was (2026-08-09)

Follow-up to `findings.md` (2026-07-23). That session halved the median and cut the
tail; this one was asked to go after **stutter** specifically, and spent its first half
finding out that most of what looked like stutter was not the game.

Read this before optimizing anything here again. Four of the six things this session
first "found" were wrong, and each was disproved by a measurement that cost minutes.

## The tooling this added (use it, it is why the answers below are answers)

- **`perf::span(Probe::*)`** (`src/perf.rs`) — 24 probes across the game's own Update
  systems, charging a static atomic array. Free unless `CATHEDRAL_PERF` is set.
- **`[spike]` lines** — every frame over 20 ms writes one line to `logs.jsonl` naming
  where its time went, split into the **ECS main schedule** (`First`..`Last`) and the
  **render + present** remainder. Stutter is an event; a run average cannot name events.
- **`scripts/perf_spikes.py`** — summarizes those lines: what a bad frame is made of.
- **`scripts/perf_suite_headless.sh`** — the standard suite, unmapped and silent.
- **`scripts/perf_ablate.sh`** — the `CATHEDRAL_NO_*` quadrant.
- **`scripts/perf_gpu_sample.sh`**, **`scripts/perf_clocks.sh`** — samples GPU
  utilization, SM clock and P-state alongside a run. This is the one that cracked it.
- **`scripts/perf_under_load.sh`** — the same route against 0/4/8/12 extra busy cores.
- **`scripts/perf_audio_ab.sh`** — audio on/off, played into a null sink so a
  measurement run stays silent on somebody else's desktop.
- New levers: `CATHEDRAL_NO_SHADOWS`, `CATHEDRAL_NO_VOLUMETRIC_FOG`,
  `CATHEDRAL_THREADS=<n>` (ECS compute pool size), `CATHEDRAL_PERF=plain`
  (records frames *without* `RenderDiagnosticsPlugin`, so the instrument can be told
  apart from what it measures).

## The answer

**The game does not have a baseline stutter problem. The machine's load does.**

Same binary, same route, 1920x1080, headless, fake backend, RTX 4070 / 20 cores:

| machine load (1 min) | GPU P-state | SM clock | p50 | p99 | frames > 16.7 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14.7 | P0 | 2775 MHz | 5.92 | **14.0** | **0.3%** |
| 18–21 | P0 | 2775 MHz | ~7.9 | ~28 | ~6% |
| 27–29 | **P3** | **1100–1260 MHz** | ~30 | ~97 | **68–90%** |

At a quiet machine the game holds **p99 ≈ 14 ms** — better than the 2026-07 baseline
(p99 17.7). Under contention it degrades gently, then falls off a cliff: once the CPU
can no longer feed the GPU, the card drops to a low P-state and every frame doubles
again. The cliff is the thing a player would call "it started stuttering".

This machine idles around load 13 of 20 (browser, editors, agents), which is why the
first measurements of the session looked like a large regression against `findings.md`.
They were not comparable: the July numbers were taken on a quiet machine.

## What it was NOT (each disproved, not argued)

1. **Not the game's own systems.** Probed individually, every Update system in the game
   accounts for **5% of spike-frame time**. The ECS main schedule stays flat at 6–9 ms
   even on 45 ms frames; **66% of every spike is render + present**.
2. **Not the new content.** Ablating vermin/rats, dogs and chalk marks — all shipped
   since the last perf pass, all prime suspects — changed nothing beyond the ±10–20%
   run-to-run noise. Nor did weather.
3. **Not shadows or volumetric fog.** `CATHEDRAL_NO_SHADOWS` and
   `CATHEDRAL_NO_VOLUMETRIC_FOG`, together, moved p95 from 20.5 to 20.1 ms.
4. **Not audio.** A first reading showed audio tripling the over-budget frames (44% vs
   12%). Re-run back-to-back through a null sink it is within noise, and slightly
   *faster*. The first reading was contamination: 30 analysis agents had just started on
   the same machine.
5. **Not single-threaded Bevy.** `Cargo.toml` uses `default-features = false` and never
   re-adds `multi_threaded`, which *is* one of Bevy's defaults — but it arrives
   transitively through `bevy_internal` anyway. Confirmed with
   `cargo tree -p bevy_ecs -f "{p} FEATURES={f}" --depth 0` and by counting the running
   process's threads (12 Compute Task Pool + 4 Async Compute + 4 IO). **`cargo tree -e
   features | grep` gave a false negative here** — see the warning below.
6. **Not resolution.** 640x360 measured *slower* than 1920x1080 on one run, which sent
   this session looking at the presentation path. It was a single sample of a bimodal
   process; repeated, the two resolutions are indistinguishable and the variance is the
   machine's.

## Measurement discipline this session had to learn

- **Run-to-run noise in the tail is ±10–20%** on this machine, and much larger when
  something else is compiling. Any single-run difference under ~20% is nothing. Repeat
  three times before believing a number, and take before/after **back to back**.
- **Sample the GPU's clock and P-state alongside the frame times.** Two runs that differ
  by 7× in over-budget frames looked identical in every game-side metric and differed
  only in `pstate`. Without `perf_clocks.sh` this session would have "optimized" the
  game until the numbers happened to look good.
- **Do not run agents, builds, or anything else while measuring.** This is not a small
  effect; it is the largest single effect in this document.
- **`grep` in the agent environment is wrapped and silently drops matches.** Use
  `rtk proxy grep`. A bare `grep -c` returning 0 is not evidence of absence — it cost
  this session a confidently-stated wrong root cause.
- The absolute numbers here are all **headless** (`CATHEDRAL_HEADLESS=1`, window created
  but never mapped). That was checked, back to back, with `scripts/perf_windowed_ab.sh`:

  | | p50 | p95 | p99 | frames > 16.7 ms |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | headless | 7.69 | 18.24 | 26.45 | 6.9% |
  | windowed | 7.42 | 15.56 | 23.07 | 3.6% |

  **A mapped window is if anything faster.** Headless is a slightly *pessimistic* proxy,
  never a flattering one, so a headless number is safe to trust as an upper bound on the
  played game's frame times. Worth re-checking if the harness ever changes; it needs one
  visible run, which is not something to start on somebody else's desktop without asking.

## What was changed anyway, and why

The diagnosis says the frame is fine when it is given a machine. It also says the frame
collapses when it is not — and that *is* the bug the player hits, because a real desktop
has a browser on it. So the work went to load-robustness: fewer barriers, less
main-thread blocking, smaller per-frame fixed costs. Those are the changes that make a
frame survive being descheduled, as opposed to making it cheaper on an idle machine
where it is already cheap.

Measured with `scripts/perf_ab_repeat.sh`, which alternates the two binaries and reports
the **median** of each percentile over five paired runs — a single pair decides nothing
here. 1920x1080, headless, fake backend.

Idle machine:

| | mean | p50 | p95 | p99 | frames > 16.7 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 8.70 | 6.97 | 17.60 | 22.36 | 7.1% |
| after | 8.15 | 6.71 | **15.93** | 23.10 | **4.5%** |

With 8 extra busy cores — the case the work was aimed at:

| | mean | p50 | p95 | p99 | frames > 16.7 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 12.58 | 11.71 | 19.53 | 26.14 | 12.2% |
| after | 11.53 | 10.83 | **17.88** | 26.82 | **7.1%** |

**Over-budget frames down 37% idle and 42% under load**, with p95 down ~1.65 ms in both.
p99 is unchanged within noise: the extreme tail is still the render-side stall nobody has
explained yet, and none of this work touched it.

## The one that went backwards

The first `after` binary was **worse at every load level** — +4 ms on the median, and
frames over budget up from 13% to 24%. The probes said the ECS had got *faster*
(8.17 → 7.36 ms mean, engine pump 0.94 → 0.61), so the whole regression was render-side,
and one run of each binary with `CATHEDRAL_NO_SHADOWS=1` came out identical — which
named the culprit exactly.

It was a change that grouped the five interior shadow lights onto one shared gate, to
stop the shadow atlas being reallocated as each was crossed separately. The reasoning was
sound and its own reviewer flagged it as needing measurement. It was still wrong: the
union leaves all five casting — twenty auxiliary shadow views — across the whole
175–250 m shell around the cathedral, which is where the player actually is. The rare
reallocation was much the cheaper of the two. Reverted to per-light gates, with the
finding written into the doc comment so nobody re-derives it.

The lesson worth keeping: **a render-side change cannot be reasoned about from the ECS
side.** The probes are blind past `Last`, so an "obviously cheaper" change to lights,
materials or meshes has to be measured against an ablation run before it is believed.
