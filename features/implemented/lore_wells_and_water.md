# Wells and Water

Ford Well could become the best known member of a small network of ward wells and cisterns, each with a practical name, distinctive water, keeper, queue, and group of habitual users.  A mineral-tasting Bitter Well, a chained lifting mechanism at Chain Well, or a Three-Curb Well shared by competing courts would acquire reputations without possessing magical properties.  Bakers, brewers, dyers, animals, fire crews, and households need different quantities and qualities of water, so access rights and carrying labor would shape streets and daily schedules.  Well-keepers and professional carriers would hear news from many households while also being blamed first for sickness, shortages, damaged buckets, and queue jumping.  Seasonal lowering, a cracked curb, a fouled cistern, or a disputed private pipe could become a ward-wide problem before it becomes a civic one.

GAMEPLAY IDEAS, these are NOT implemented:

For gameplay, wells would be natural encounter hubs where NPC routines overlap and gossip travels between otherwise separate social groups.  A player might trace contamination without a glowing quest marker, choose which users receive scarce water, recognize a false poisoning rumor, or help organize buckets when the Ruin sounds for fire.  Audible windlasses, splashing troughs, waiting animals, arguments, and changing queues would make water supply perceptible even when the underlying simulation remains simple.  The direction should keep water stubbornly physical: its drama comes from labor, access, weather, maintenance, and belief rather than from enchanted springs.
