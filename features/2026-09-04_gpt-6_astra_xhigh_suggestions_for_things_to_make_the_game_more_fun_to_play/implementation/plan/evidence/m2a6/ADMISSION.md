# M2a6 law admission and closed layout — 2026-09-08

Status: Implemented. This is a component bound, not complete-save or synchronous-host acceptance. The shared 1 GiB cap, 128 MiB encoded/expanded caps and depth 64 are unchanged.

The existing nonallocating lexical preflight runs before borrowed owner extraction or typed decoding. Every object/array contributes 512 B, every scalar/string/key 64 B and every encoded string byte two more bytes. Let E be that expanded charge and J the actual input bytes. The attached reservation is at least `4,096 + 4 × E + 3 × J + 4,194,304` B. Decode uses the original lexical charge even for escaped or whitespace-padded input; candidate conversion never shrinks it to a canonical encoding. The private test appends 1 MiB of whitespace and checks the full extra 3 MiB remains attached through candidate validation. Error and cancellation paths release the lease; save bytes and candidate owners retain their own leases until destruction.

The four expanded copies cover these concrete v1 DTO/remote shapes, deserializer and collection capacity, retained candidate indexes, and validation/error scratch. They are not a claim about arbitrary Deserialize types. The three encoded copies cover input, escaped-string parser scratch and exact-sized encoding work, with expanded string/key charges also covering owned strings and error formatting. Arrays/objects are admitted before serde can extract records; many empty values and deep unknown input are tested for the specific preflight refusal. Typed records reject unknown fields, duplicate map/set keys and missing nullable fields. All owner prose is at most 65,536 UTF-8 bytes; the composed cached notice line permits four such source fields plus 1,024 B of fixed separators/rung text. General actor/item/place IDs use `crate::MAX_ID_CHARS` (128 Unicode scalars on this build), with a sufficient 512-byte early check and exact scalar validation.

## Closed records and sparse indexes

The ignored `checkpoint_law_layout` diagnostic measures the actual target layouts: Notices 32 B, WardNotice 296 B, Summons 48 B, Custody 24 B, CustodyRecord 216 B, Station 80 B, ActorId/Option<ActorId> 24 B, Option<f64> 16 B and Option<Office> 1 B. PlayerNotice is 64 B, PlayerCustody 208 B, EngineMessage 232 B, WorldLaw DTO/candidate 160 B and EngineLaw DTO 432 B. Owned String and Vec headers are included in those inline records; string payload and nested collections have their own lexical charges. Remote Option decoding is direct and mandatory, and there are no internally tagged provenance buffers in these shapes.

Custody uses a strict streaming BTreeMap adapter, with no intermediate row Vec. On the pinned target a conservative node uses eleven `(ActorId, CustodyRecord)` slots plus 168 B = 2,808 B. A minimal record alone receives 4,816 B of lexical expansion, before its map key/container charges. This covers a sparsely populated singleton root as well as ordinary occupied nodes; it does not assume eleven useful records inhabit every node. WardNotice Vec growth uses the initial allocation floor of up to four 296-byte slots (1,184 B), then geometric growth below twice the populated row count. Record field/container charges cover both cases even when prose is empty. Served BTreeSet<ActorId> singleton nodes need at most `11 × 24 + 168 = 432` B before payload; container plus first scalar/string charges cover these sparse roots. Holder Vec capacity uses an initial floor of four 24-byte slots (96 B), then geometric growth below twice its admitted rows; the container charge covers the sparse initial floor. Every record's aggregate counts constrain the Cartesian product of custody records and holders before allocation; independent per-field count caps are not the allocation argument.

## Borrowed context and validation scratch

A separate fixed 4,194,304 B allowance is admitted before hashing/validating any borrowed context. At most 25,000 characters and registry entries are accepted; the exact ordered registry/home representation is streamed through a 4 MiB byte limit. Its finite points, identities, indexes and home-owner references are then checked by the existing pure PlaceRegistry validator. No trusted definition parser, constructor, navigation bake or singleton runs here. Frozen Station is separately validated saved history, not resolved against this current registry.

Registry name validation retains a BTreeMap<(&String, usize)> with 16-byte key/value slots. With at most 25,000 entries, a root with at least one key and other nodes with at least five keys permits at most `1 + floor((25,000 - 1) / 5) = 5,000` nodes. Eleven slots plus 168 B per node totals 1,720,000 B. This bound covers sparse roots too. It is below the 4 MiB allowance, with ample remaining space for bounded serializer/SHA state and transient error work. Registry scratch is dropped before custody/cache holder uniqueness sets; a maximum 25,000-entry BTreeSet<&ActorId> needs at most `5,000 × (11 × 8 + 168) = 1,280,000` B. Cache notice IDs have only eight rows. Candidate validation reconstructs no World, Engine or station registry; serde already constructed the private law indexes, and into_candidate repeats context/reference/projection validation while moving the admitted DTO.

Validation traversals are bounded by the lexical totals and context count/stream ceilings. The current grip calculation is checked against the saved actor references without a temporary World, and rejects nonfinite multiplication. This proves representability of that current operation, not safe future occupation/status changes or an all-consumer CPU horizon.

## Supported numeric and continuation policy

Notices retain at most eight live records in increasing allocation order; IDs may be sparse after expiry, settlement or eviction. Warrant-only saturation refuses another allocation. Served names and private attempted/settlement names are historical; current actor/item existence is not required. Every live custody prisoner and holder, and each InCharge officer, must resolve to a saved actor. A Committed officer may have departed. At most four non-authored arrests and 25,000 total custody records are supported by v1; low-level custom states exceeding the production arrest cap are outside this format.

Seizure, commitment and officer-turn anchors are finite nonnegative logical seconds no later than the supplied boundary, with commitment/turn not before seizure. Sentences and notice dates are signed calendar values; absent dates remain absent and overdue state waits for the ordinary pass. Dated notice expiry arithmetic remains representable. InCharge records have an officer and no commitment; sentence office/deadline pairs belong to Stone House commitments. Authored inmates retain their exemption from logical hold ceilings.

Notice allocation and struggle hashes require 257 advances of headroom (`MAX_STEPS + 1`). A private test restores the last supported value, performs exactly 257 ordinary advances, and refuses a subsequent capture at exhausted state. This is an explicit bounded component horizon, not proof that an arbitrary Engine command vector, catch-up burst or lifetime is safe. Full host/batch admission and calendar/accounting horizons remain mandatory before M2b adoption. Hash behavior still needs the M2a1 exact build/target/behavior manifest; the law context marker and selected constants do not replace it.

## Diagnostic phases

`alibi_law_cost --mode authored|populated --samples N --output PATH` reports the established six raw arrays. Preflight includes bounded context fingerprint/index checks and borrowed full-component cost counting. Export repeats admission/context checks, validates and clones both law owners plus the exact Engine cache. Encode counts/writes canonical bytes. Decode validates raw admission, parses/indexes and validates references/cache. Candidate validation repeats pure checks and moves the DTO; it is not a second BTree reconstruction. Drop includes candidate and save-byte destruction and lease release. Fixed context working bytes remain retained throughout, even after their temporary work ends.

The deliberate `law-obligations-v1` setup uses actual authored/+2,000 production placement, four active notices, normal served publication, an unheld player's closing escort, a separate held NPC and a committed historical notice/officer link. Ordinary follow_escorts moves NPCs before tick_custody, so the closing witness uses the player. It preserves eight normal authored inmates and publishes the law cache through an ordinary poll. This bounded active workload is not maximum legal occupancy, full-city authority or a universal p99 proof. Release repetitions and independent auditing are coordinator work.
