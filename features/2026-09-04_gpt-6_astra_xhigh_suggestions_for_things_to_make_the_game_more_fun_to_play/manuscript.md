# A crime you solve with your feet

**An Alibi in Stone** is a first-person investigation in Ombreval. A notary has been assaulted and a packet of disputed documents stolen. A carter is being held. The man with the strongest apparent alibi was seen at the same counter before and after the attack. The player discovers what those sightings actually establish, walks the intervening routes, and reconstructs how the crime was possible.

The central pleasure is a change in understanding: you return to an ordinary wall, look up, and realise that two places you thought were far apart almost touch. Then you have to establish who used that connection. A route proves opportunity; identifying the assailant requires a further, independent chain of evidence.

## The game this quest proposes

Observe a place; ask a precise question; form a hypothesis; test it by walking, watching or arranging a demonstration; decide what to disclose. Conversations produce claims and conditions. The world supplies ways to check them. The player's notebook supports memory without announcing the culprit.

This document specifies one complete case: fixed truth, timeline, cast, clues, access routes, suspect responses, alternate solutions, failures and aftermath. Its rewards are useful access, recovered property and particular relationships. It does not depend on combat, a detective statistic, a supernatural revelation or a model grading a final speech.

# The opening: the wrong man holding a wound

The player reaches the Tallage during Dayspring on case day 1. A familiar working square is disrupted in one place. Warin Underbridge sits beside a watch post with his cart idle nearby. A clean rag still bears a stain from helping Odo. Averil Tarn is arguing with Sergeant Segwin Mott about releasing him. Public stairs to a nearby comparison room are cordoned for inspection.

Odo Trask was attacked there at the previous day's High Wick. Warin says he found him, steadied him and shouted for help. Odo's packet has not been found. Warin had publicly disputed a copying fee earlier, and he was the first person discovered in the room. These are grounds for suspicion and a temporary hold in this proposed procedure, not proof of guilt.

## A conversation with something to act on

Mott supplies three facts: Odo is alive and can speak; the packet held originals needed to settle a disputed pledge; and Corin Copp says he was at his aunt's counter throughout the relevant interval. Mott will hear contrary evidence. He does not appoint the stranger magistrate or grant unrestricted access to the square.

Averil offers the invitation: “Ask him where I was standing when Odo cried out.” Following that lead starts useful work immediately. Warin instead describes the room and names the hired writer who can read his delivery instruction. Looking up reveals its back light above an apparently unrelated yard.

| Opening action | Immediate next step |
|---|---|
| Speak to Warin | Learn his delivery, fee dispute and account of finding Odo |
| Ask Averil about the cry | Visit the lower beam and reconstruct her observation |
| Ask Mott to inspect the room | Receive supervised crime-scene access |
| Visit Lise's counter | Hear the reassuring alibi at first hand |

No acceptance button creates the crime retroactively. It belongs to a fixed history seeded before the opening campaign clock. The local inquiry has an announced review at High Wick on day 2. The player can ignore it, arrive later, or reopen its finding with new evidence.

The hook is personal and spatial: an amiable man may lose his work because helping an injured person put him in the wrong room, while a window above the square suggests that the witnesses have not all been asked the same question.

# Author truth: what actually happened

**Corin Copp committed the theft and assault, alone. Warin is innocent. Lise provided an honest but incomplete alibi.** There is no twin, teleportation, manipulated bell, supernatural cause or second culprit. The player's route through the evidence varies; the truth does not.

Odo was comparing an original pledge against a purported release copied by Corin. The release falsely cancelled a debt secured on workshop tools and named Corin's client as the person entitled to collect them. A craft household could lose its tools if the substitution went unexplained. Corin expected the original never to be produced. This transaction is new quest content; the household is represented by documents and later return of property, not another essential witness.

## Motive, method, mistake

The packet contains the original pledge, the disputed release and Odo's signed inventory wrapper. Removing it delays the comparison and lets Corin recover his forgery. Odo has already deposited a counterfoil describing the discrepancy; Corin does not know that. Theft obstructs the inquiry without erasing every trace of it.

During a short hoist operation at Lise's shop, she leaves the counter to supervise the load in the rear yard. Corin knows an upper service connection between her pledge loft and the adjoining comparison room. He uses it, finds Odo at the desk, snatches at the packet and shoves him when he resists. Odo falls against the desk, cuts his brow and cries out. Corin takes the packet. The attack is brief and non-graphic.

In the struggle, Odo catches Corin's document wallet and its brass catch breaks. One half falls under the desk; Corin retains the damaged wallet. Lise saw the catch intact before the hoist operation and notices its awkward closure after it. This independently dates the physical connection later described in E10–E12.

Returning through the loft, Corin hides the whole packet in a disused document rack. He is back at the counter before Lise returns. Later he strengthens two genuine sightings into a false statement of continuous presence. Lise initially repeats that conclusion because she trusts him, not because she conspires.

## Two separate resolutions

**Clearing Warin** requires establishing that the attack and his presence below coincided, or reliable evidence identifying someone else. **Establishing Corin's responsibility** requires connecting him to the packet or an independently corroborated admission. Finding the passage alone accomplishes neither.

Recovering the original matters independently: Odo can complete the comparison and prevent the forged release from deciding the tools' ownership. The player can help Warin, recover property and expose Corin in different combinations. The outcome follows evidence actually delivered, not a hidden “correct suspect” selection.

Corin chose the offence. Lise made an understandable inference. Mott acted too quickly on incomplete evidence. The investigation becomes interesting when the player distinguishes those responsibilities instead of treating every mistaken statement as a malicious lie.

No additional named person is required to solve the case. The absent client who paid for the forgery is identified in the packet as a later lead; exposing a wider conspiracy is outside this quest. Existing lore about other forged papers and the impossible light remains unresolved.

# The geography is the central discovery

{{figure:case_topology.png|Proposed topology, not surveyed coordinates. The comparison room and pledge loft occupy adjoining rear property. Separate public entrances require a long lane circuit; the private connection crosses their upper boundary.|3.05}}

The Tallage, Tally Bridge and Lise's pawnshop are established places. The case adds a comparison room rented by Odo behind the shop's block, a usable pledge loft and a service connection. It does not move the canonical Tally Bridge or introduce water into the dry Cut. Placement must be reconciled with current geometry. [S4]

## Every space has a purpose

**A — Pawn counter:** the two sightings, public alibi and Corin's conversational performance. **Y — Hoist yard:** the blind interval and its physical rhythm. **C — Comparison room:** assault, missing packet and upper entrance. **W — Lower beam:** Warin's position at the cry. **G — Writing station:** Gile's observation and counterfoil. **H — Loft rack:** the packet's initial hiding place.

The public walk teaches how the street-facing entrances relate. The upper route reveals how different those relationships become above ground. A small grilled back light links sound from the room to people below without being a walkable opening. Each connection can be inspected independently.

## A visible connection to interpret

From the lane, mismatched shutters and a repair seam suggest two buildings nearly touch upstairs. Inside Odo's room, a service latch faces the wrong property. At Lise's shop, the stair has mundane labels for stored pledges. None glows, appears only after a dialogue flag, or bears a conveniently incriminating symbol.

The intended discovery is spatial recognition: “That back wall is the back of her shop.” It remains rewarding if found before hearing the alibi. The player can then ask better questions earlier.

The public circuit is an explicit production risk: fitting a long return route around two close properties must feel like inherited city fabric. If the actual Tallage cannot support it naturally, change the proposed room site and revalidate the case. Preserve established anchors; do not contort the city merely to protect a number.

# Make the alibi mathematically fair

The premise must survive the fast player controller. Current walking/running speeds are 8/12 m/s; the ordinary NPC walk constant is 2.1 m/s. A trip that only seems impossible when walked slowly cannot justify a firm conclusion. Developer flight and teleportation are outside the case's physical rules. [S3]

{{figure:route_timing.png|Design arithmetic, not measured navigation. Public travel alone exceeds the generous gap at the sprint ceiling. The private trip fits at NPC pace, including the offence and concealment.|2.3}}

| Quantity | Greybox contract |
|---|---|
| Unobserved interval | Nominal 36 simulation seconds; defensible range 32–40 seconds |
| Shortest admissible public return trip | At least 576 m: travel alone takes 48 seconds at 12 m/s |
| Private circuit | 48 m: counter → room 22 m; room → rack 6 m; rack → counter 20 m |
| Private execution | 48 / 2.1 + 8 seconds for encounter and concealment ≈ 30.9 seconds |
| Result | Private trip fits the lower bound; public trip fails the upper bound |

These are intended distances for adjoining properties whose public entrances face different lane networks. They are **not measured existing routes**. A 576 m return detour is a demanding site requirement; survey before committing this puzzle to production.

## The interval belongs to work, not bells

The visible hoist follows a fixed rack travel between marked stops under a loaded, geared mechanism. Its uninterrupted operation is established by the people tending it. Supervised repeated cycles establish the conservative range. Stop it today and the demonstration records a stop; it cannot certify an uninterrupted historical interval.

No character derives precise minutes from bell strokes. The office establishes the period; a shared work event brackets the gap. At other day lengths, compare spatially meaningful elapsed durations with their recorded clock mapping. Do not multiply feasibility by a changed clock rate.

If another legal route beats the public bound, honour it. Re-author the strong “public route impossible” claim or choose another site. Do not add invisible walls, slow the player secretly, or reject a correct demonstration because it bypassed the planned passage.

## Questions that move the case

Ask Lise “Could you see the counter from there?” Ask Averil “What were you doing when the voice came from that window?” Ask Gile “Did you look up before the carter went upstairs, or afterwards?” Ask Odo “Which side did you hear the latch?” Each turns an account into something inspectable.

The interface offers those topics after the relevant person, object or claim is known. Voice and free text remain available; no exact sentence is required. Unsupported details receive uncertainty rather than model invention.

Rumour compresses Lise's words into “his aunt watched him all the time.” The original attribution remains retrievable. Correcting her account does not turn her into a liar. A careful player can defend her honesty while exposing what Corin made it appear to prove.

Every character has a fixed knowledge boundary. Later learning updates an account with a source. No prompt grants a witness the author's hidden route or culprit flag. The case uses the shared knowledge-and-rumour system when it ships, rather than creating a parallel store. [S5]

# Location one: Odo's comparison room

Mott permits supervised inspection as soon as asked. The player can also return through an appointment with Odo. Covert access is optional expansion content; the essential investigation never requires burglary.

{{figure:scene_plan.png|Conceptual room plan. The public stair, service latch and back light support different inferences. The attacker is not identified by a coloured footprint trail.|2.6}}

## What the player can do

Inspect the empty packet position and ask Odo what belonged there. Compare the counterfoil when obtained. Examine the fallen stool and desk edge; their arrangement matches his account. Look through the back light toward the beam. Try the service latch from inside and notice it opens toward another property's loft.

Odo remembers without convenient total amnesia. He faced his papers, grasped for the packet, and turned into the shove. The partial view did not establish a face. He identifies the documents and entry side, not hair colour, height to the inch or an exact clock second.

## Three facts, restrained deductions

The counterfoil establishes **what is missing**. The latch establishes **an alternative entrance exists**. The back light establishes **the cry could reach the witnesses below**. None states who committed the crime.

Ask Odo to stand at the desk and have a consenting helper speak from the relevant position. This tests audibility, not a replay of the assault. Captions carry equivalent source and timing information. Renderer geometry and sim witness calculation must agree.

Odo authenticates a recovered packet by its described contents, wrapper and matching counterfoil. He reads it aloud privately or publicly. He cannot be bought, accept a player-made substitute as an original, or identify Corin merely because the accusation sounds convincing.

The room remains available after review. Its state is recorded before cleaning, and later visits explain what moved. A late player can inspect a preserved scene sketch and repeat the sightline test, rather than losing the case to housekeeping.

Give the room enough tactile specificity to reward looking: an ink rest caught under the fallen stool, the free edge of a cord where the packet sat, and daylight through the upper seam. Those details orient the player. Only the named, validated observations enter the proof system.

# Location two: the pawnshop alibi

Lise answers ordinary questions about the hoist and her nephew at the counter. Access to stored pledges requires a stated purpose because the loft holds other people's property. Her valuation fee is not a mandatory payment for essential testimony.

{{figure:alibi_sightline.png|A person can honestly be seen twice without being watched continuously. Taking Lise's position makes the blind interval understandable by eye.|2.45}}

## The first satisfying reversal

Ask her to show where she stood. Enter the yard with permission. A wall and stored goods block the public room. The last place you saw Corin is simply out of sight. Lise concedes this readily: she had work to do; she did not claim to be his keeper.

The notebook preserves two entries: Lise saw Corin at the marked start and finish; Corin claims he remained throughout. That distinction is a discovery. No red “LIAR” stamp appears over Lise's portrait.

## Three ways into the loft

**Ordinary access:** ask to inspect the connection while Lise accompanies you. Naming the latch seen from Odo's side is sufficient reason. **Official inspection:** show Mott the two-door topology and request a limited route inspection. **Independent exploration:** enter during a supervised pledge retrieval, then ask to examine the connection while already upstairs.

All three use the same door and permissions. None requires exposing Lise's illiteracy, threatening her customers or completing an unrelated favour. A refused broad search can be answered with a narrower request to inspect the passage alone.

Lise will not knowingly certify a forgery. When text matters, she asks Odo or Gile to read it under witnessed practice. Her inability to read must never become a mandatory public revelation to unlock the solution.

Shown strong evidence against Corin, she asks to see the packet and hear his actual admission. Loyalty delays emotional acceptance, not submission of evidence elsewhere. She may remain an honest witness while withdrawing her warmth from the investigator.

If Corin is present, private questioning can warn him. The player sees that he is listening and can choose another time or room. A hidden universal suspicion meter cannot punish a conversation no one relevant heard.

# Location three: the beam and Warin's alibi

Warin's position below is the fastest way to do something useful. The player can clear the wrong man's name before solving the entire crime. This creates an intermediate payoff and a genuine choice about pursuing Corin further.

## Reconstruct the interrupted task

Averil shows the beam, the binding Warin was securing and Odo's window. She recalls a concrete instant: his hands were on the load when the cry interrupted Gile reading a line aloud. Warin finished securing it before going upstairs. That delay explains why he arrived after the attacker left.

Ask Warin to demonstrate and Averil to show her position. The player can see him and the window from there. Ask Gile separately what interrupted the reading. He identifies the same event and man below without first receiving Averil's account from the player.

| Element | Evidential use |
|---|---|
| Averil's direct observation | Warin below at the cry; her relationship is disclosed as context |
| Gile's independent observation | Corroborates his position during that cry |
| Odo's account and local audibility | Connect the cry to the upstairs attack |
| Delivery instruction and case | Explain Warin's legitimate later approach, not innocence by themselves |

When these align, Mott lifts the temporary hold and corrects the record. The player does not need the route puzzle first. Strong evidence against Corin also permits release if the witness chain was missed.

## The human payoff

Warin goes back to his cart and checks it before saying anything elaborate. Averil stops arguing because its purpose is achieved. Warin offers one future cart journey on terms he can fulfil. He cannot grant someone else's cart or repeat that unique reward on every visit.

The fee dispute remains real. Warin can owe an honest copying charge while being innocent of assault. Odo corrects the accusation without inventing a spotless past for him. The distinction should be audible in their first conversation after release.

If only Averil is available, Mott records her account and offers provisional release under a named sponsor while seeking corroboration. Missing a second witness changes confidence and procedure; it does not erase the first observation. If the player lacks a sponsor, the free Gile route remains available.

At no point does the player have to know the hidden culprit to ask for Warin's release. Relieving a wrong and proving another person's responsibility are distinct achievements with separate state.

# Location four: the man who taught Corin

Gile sits with a writing board near the Tally Bridge. His trade and usual place make him easy to find. If absent, a posted return office and people who saw him leave give a bounded lead. He is never scattered to an arbitrary corner to extend the investigation.

## Start with what he saw

He remembers Warin below when the cry interrupted the reading. He did not see the attacker. He can read Warin's instruction and identify its delivery. These answers are free; affection for Corin does not make him suppress evidence clearing Warin.

His board holds Odo's duplicate counterfoil. Odo left it before comparing the originals, because moving papers without a list is bad practice. Gile permits inspection, supplies a witnessed copy or accompanies the player with it. Theft has custody consequences but is unnecessary.

## What the paper establishes

The counterfoil names the original pledge, disputed release and distinctive wrapper. It records a mismatch between the release's supposed authority and the earlier instrument. It does not name the assailant. A notation says the fair copy came through Corin's desk, providing a reason to ask him about it.

Gile admits he taught Corin and recognises habits in his legitimate copying. He refuses to certify a crime from handwriting alone. He can point out a specific alteration for Odo to test against the original. Useful information stays narrow enough to be honest.

## A conversation with weight

Shown the packet or a substantial contradiction, Gile stops praising the fine hand and asks whether anyone was hurt badly. He can attend the reconstruction. The player may show him evidence privately first, bring him unprepared into public proceedings, or leave him out if other proof suffices.

His reaction reflects that choice. He should not demand an unrelated errand, a persuasion roll or an unaffordable payment before stating what he witnessed. The tension is asking him to confront what the evidence may mean for someone he taught.

This location provides two independent forms of support: a direct observation about Warin and a pre-existing inventory record. Neither is manufactured after the questions begin. Creation time and custodians belong to the authored history.

Gile's counterfoil remains accessible if he is unavailable for the final scene: a witnessed copy can be lodged with Mott. That preserves the document's chain while acknowledging that a copied statement is a different thing from examining a living witness.

# Walk the account before trying to disprove it

The player can ask Mott to mark the two public entrances on the learned map. The route overlay stops at known streets. It does not reveal a hidden loft, compute an unknown shortcut or draw a line through a solid building.

## First walk: understand the mistake

Start at the pawn counter, leave through its public door and find the comparison room's public stair. The way winds around two separate yards. At the room, look back through the window and recognise how close the pawnshop's rear actually is. The detour has a reason in the architecture: entrances serve different lanes and property divisions persist above the street.

The walk should offer things to notice without unrelated interruptions. The sound of the same hoist carries across the two yards. A repeated painted shutter gives orientation. Looking upward reveals a service-level seam that the street route does not explain. The player learns the district while forming a new hypothesis.

## Test what was claimed

The notebook can time a complete walk between nominated endpoints and record movement mode, stops and current door states. That is a measurement of today's attempt, not proof nobody could ever move faster. The case uses the conservative public minimum only after the admissible graph and speed ceiling are established in production.

| Player result | Honest conclusion |
|---|---|
| Slow public walk takes longer than the gap | This attempt failed; a faster route or pace remains possible |
| Verified public lower bound exceeds the gap | The claim cannot be explained by the known public network |
| Player finds a faster legal route | The strong public-impossibility claim is false; preserve the valid discovery |
| A private route fits | At least one physically possible trip exists; its user is not yet identified |

Do not require repeated sprint attempts. A successful measurement stays in the notebook. A guided reconstruction can later reproduce the route with a helper while the player watches the critical doorway.

If the player stops investigating after exposing the gap, the public record may remain “alibi incomplete.” That is useful progress, but it does not justify arresting the person the player dislikes most. The next question is who had access and what connects them to the missing property.

# Find the upper way

The service route should feel like something work created and habit concealed. A narrow stair climbs above Lise's pledges; a short covered connection crosses a property seam; the comparison room's inner latch lies on the far side. It is mundane, awkward and extremely useful.

## Discovery has three entrances

**From the crime scene:** inspect the service latch and ask where it goes. Odo knows it reaches the neighbouring upper property but normally leaves it alone. **From the street:** connect the back light and loft shutters, then ask Lise about her stair. **From work:** accompany a pledge retrieval and notice the short upper connection firsthand.

These clues coexist from the start. There is no hidden-route visibility flag unlocked by hearing the correct NPC sentence. Knowing what a doorway connects can add it to the map; discovery does not grant permission to use it.

## What proves access at the relevant time

Lise confirms Corin routinely retrieves papers from the loft and used its key before the incident. Odo confirms the service latch was left unbarred for document transfer that morning. Those statements establish opportunity only. Neither remembers watching Corin cross during the attack.

The player can repeat the trip under supervision, entering the room and returning to the same counter. The 48 m reference circuit includes stairs and valid door interactions. It is not a line through roofs available only to the developer camera. A consenting stand-in at normal NPC pace can perform the route; no precision jumping is needed.

## An additional discovery, not an automatic solution

The old rack H sits just off the return leg. It is reachable during route inspection, but a search of stored papers needs Lise's consent or Mott's narrower evidence authority. If the player inspects it before Corin's later retrieval, the packet is physically there. Its wrapper matches the missing inventory. Shared access means this does not prove who placed it.

Once the case ends, access to the passage may be granted, restricted to daylight, or barred by Lise. The player still knows the spatial connection. A later job can make that knowledge useful even if they must negotiate access anew.

The reward for discovery is immediate understanding and future capability. The route must remain interesting after its role in the mystery is known; otherwise it was only a disguised keyhole for one quest.

# Stage a reconstruction people can watch

A reconstruction is a supported operation: choose a known claim, name its endpoints, obtain permission and ask a consenting helper to enact it. The player can carry the evidence, watch a blind corner, or walk alongside. They are arranging an experiment inside the city rather than solving a diagram in a menu.

## The pawnshop experiment

Invite Lise and Mott; set the hoist to its marked starting rest; appoint a stand-in. Lise tends a comparable uninterrupted cycle in the yard. The stand-in leaves the counter, takes the authorised service route, pauses at the desk for the specified encounter interval, visits the rack and returns. Mott watches the relevant transitions and records the result.

When Lise comes back and finds the stand-in at the counter, the player sees the point of the case. Her original sightings can both be true. The demonstration does not imply she was lying or that the stand-in committed yesterday's crime.

## Rules that keep the experiment honest

The action preview names route, movement speed, access states, expected duration and the claim being tested. No one is attacked. The encounter uses a short neutral “collect the marked packet” stand-in action. A stopped hoist, barred door, obstructed actor or player interruption invalidates that run's timing certificate and explains why.

The player can retry without paying a fee. A prior successful certificate survives later obstruction. If a witness leaves, Mott can record his own direct observation and arrange a later presentation. Multiple people need not be herded manually across the whole city.

## Two useful outcomes

**Fits:** the alibi's claim of impossibility is refuted. Mott can authorise a specific further inspection when additional evidence makes it relevant. **Does not fit:** the current route or setup cannot establish the proposed opportunity. The notebook keeps the failed conditions; it does not quietly award success because the author knows Corin is guilty.

At normal clock settings this operation should take roughly two minutes including setup and acknowledgement. A skip option may advance a validated, already-seen reconstruction while presenting its result, but the first demonstration must be watchable. Text, captions and a simple interval bar communicate the same result for players who cannot depend on sound or precise visual timing.

The best line afterward belongs to Lise: “I saw him twice. I did not see him stay.” It summarises a discovery the player has already made with their eyes.

# The notebook keeps claims apart

{{figure:notebook.png|Proposed casebook view. Learned observations, source, uncertainty and a player hypothesis coexist. The interface does not announce the culprit or display hidden timestamps.|2.65}}

The notebook has four views: **people's accounts**, **places and routes**, **objects and custody**, and **my reconstruction**. All draw from the completed knowledge system's learned facts and receipts. A case-specific view may arrange them; it must not create a second authoritative truth store. [S5]

## The player builds a reconstruction

Select an account, attach the work event that brackets it, and connect it to a location. A route can be linked as “possible journey” after it has been learned. Direct observations appear with their witness and date. Hearsay carries its source chain. Personal notes are visibly separate from certified facts.

Useful system statements are narrow: “Lise did not see the counter during this interval”; “This observed trip fits”; “The packet's wrapper matches the counterfoil.” Avoid “Corin is lying” or a percentage guilt gauge. The player should perform the act of interpretation.

## An accusation is reviewable before submission

The evidence bundle preview lists the exact claims being advanced, the supporting objects/statements, missing corroboration and whether any evidence has a disputed custody chain. It never supplies an unknown clue. The player can submit a limited request—release Warin, search the loft, authenticate the packet—without selecting a culprit.

The interface supports free speech with a compact evidence picker. “Show what Lise said about the lift” selects the same learned claim as clicking it. A misrecognised name prompts confirmation; it does not accuse the wrong person in public.

## Returning after a break

On load, show where the investigation stood, the next announced appointment and the last confirmed custodian of each important object. Do not give omniscient live tracking. If Corin moved a packet out of sight, the notebook still says it was last confirmed in the rack until the player learns otherwise.

A layered reminder can offer a known unanswered question, then suggest a person who might answer it. The final hint may restate a learned contradiction. It cannot reveal the upstairs connection before any source or observation has made it available.

# Opportunity is not identity; identity is not proof of every charge

The resolver evaluates submitted, admissible facts without reading the hidden culprit. These thresholds support correction, investigation and referral in this fictional inquiry. “Substantiated” means sufficient grounds to refer an allegation, not a final conviction or deductive certainty against every imaginable alternative.

|---|---|---|

| Clear Warin on the witness route | E01 + E06 + E07, with verified shared cry and local sight/sound | Averil's affection or an unrelated delivery receipt |

This quest does not stage a final criminal trial, choose a sentence or make the injured Odo judge his own case. What the player sees is the correction of an accusation and the supported next action.

# The public reconstruction

The announced review happens at High Wick on day 2 in the Tallage. The player can request an earlier limited finding once a sufficient bundle exists. The event should gather the necessary people and objects within a small scene; it must not require individually escorting seven NPCs through a large city.

## A playable sequence, not a final speech puzzle

**1. State the request.** Release Warin, record a defective alibi, authorise a search, substantiate a particular offence, or defer judgment. The preview shows the claims and evidence the player is choosing to disclose.

**2. Hear the relevant accounts.** Mott invites only witnesses needed for those claims. The player can ask one of the learned clarification questions or show a contradiction. Each witness's essential recorded proposition is deterministic; the model supplies phrasing and reaction around it.

**3. Put a claim into the world.** Demonstrate Lise's blind position, repeat the private trip, align the catch pieces or have Odo compare the packet. The player chooses an appropriate demonstration, and the observers see the same geometry and object state.

**4. Receive an explicit finding.** Mott says what the evidence supports and what it does not. A deficient bundle names the missing link without revealing an unknown solution. The player may continue collecting evidence and return.

## Where drama enters

Corin can amend his claim, refuse a voluntary comparison or request a private submission. Lise can correct the crowd's “she watched him” account and still defend her nephew. Warin can interrupt a description of his supposed anger. Odo asks that the actual disputed paper be read, not a colourful account of it. These reactions respond to the player's chosen disclosures.

The physical payoff is immediate. A cleared Warin stands and returns to work. An authenticated packet goes into named custody. A supported case against Corin prompts Mott to approach and escort him, if present, or issue a targeted warrant if absent. No victory animation substitutes for those actions.

The player is not forced to broadcast unrelated pledge holders' private information or Lise's illiteracy. The same material can be authenticated in a closed comparison and its narrow conclusion read publicly. Preserving privacy should not require deliberately weakening an otherwise complete case.

If the player is absent, the scheduled review resolves from what was actually lodged. It does not invent their unsubmitted deductions. New evidence later permits a correction and visible amended notice.

# A quieter resolution—and a compromised one

The case should support discretion without implying that every official can be bought. Odo's established refusal of bribes is binding. Corin can bargain over how he gives evidence and makes restitution, not over whether a witnessed assault happened.

## Private submission

With enough pressure from the recovered packet, observed retrieval or catch chain, the player can propose a closed meeting with Corin, Odo and Mott. Corin confirms a typed account of the acts he admits, produces the packet and offers eight sparks to Warin for lost work. Payment transfers real coins and cannot exceed what the scenario has seeded in his purse.

Odo authenticates the originals. Mott records the admission and applies the same corroboration standard as a public submission. Warin's correction can be public while unnecessary customer details stay sealed. Corin's case still goes to the Bench. The reward is a defensible resolution with less public humiliation, not an erased offence.

Lise may appreciate discretion while refusing to thank the player for uncovering her nephew's conduct. Gile can hear the result privately before the square. These are specific later reactions, not a universal reputation bonus for choosing the “nice” option.

## Hush agreement

Corin can separately offer eight sparks for the player to withhold the packet or a witness account. The offer card states what silence covers and who may remain suspected. Accepting does not stop Averil, Gile or other informed people from giving their own accounts, and it does not make already-submitted records disappear.

If the player takes payment then discloses the evidence, Corin has a personal grievance about the broken arrangement; the evidence's truth is unchanged. If they genuinely conceal it, the official result follows the surviving evidence. Warin may still be cleared through the independent witness chain, while Corin's responsibility remains unresolved.

## Refuse the bargain

The player can submit a sound case without negotiating. No essential object is locked behind an agreement to spare Corin, protect Lise or accept money. That matters because the most satisfying version for some players is simply doing the investigation carefully and refusing to trade its conclusion.

Discretion, restitution and concealment are presented as separate choices. The document should never blur them into one morally convenient “settle quietly” button. The player sees exactly which facts remain on record and what action follows.

The game should show that correction at the cart stand and through relevant conversations, not just print a completion message.

Lise decides future access based on the actual incident and what the player disclosed. A lawful, discreet inspection may preserve a supervised shortcut; an unauthorised search may close it. She cannot erase the player's geographical knowledge. No universal reputation number makes unrelated citizens instantly react.

The final line in the notebook lists the settled facts and remaining questions. It must be possible to end with “Warin cleared; stolen packet recovered; assailant not established” and feel that real work was accomplished.

## Accept genuine lateral solutions

A faster valid public route refutes a claim of impossibility. A stolen key may grant physical access, with separate permission consequences. An early voluntary, confirmed admission with adequate corroboration may end the case. A player who establishes Warin's position need not find Corin first.

The engine must not use private author knowledge to make every official infallible. Unobserved planting can fool a casual witness. The game has no required combat route. If future systems allow a key witness to be incapacitated, use preserved observations and physical alternatives; do not silently resurrect them or give another character their unshared knowledge.

The first slice can operate with seven focal actors and ordinary background crowds. The crime, packet transfer and hearing skeleton must run under the fake backend. Live cognition adds individual voice and flexible phrasing, not correctness of the solution.

All new geometry and biographies remain proposals until reconciled. The code's actual speed and collision rules take precedence over attractive route numbers in this document. A convincing mathematical table cannot substitute for walking the site.

# Milestones and tests that can reject the design

| Milestone | Deliverable | Advance only when… |
|---|---|---|
| **M0 — prove the site** | Survey existing geometry; build proposed paths and sightlines; reconcile cast and knowledge API | Public/private timing inequality and local cry/visibility work without artificial blockers |
| **M1 — one honest case** | Fixed incident, room, packet/counterfoil, witness accounts, catch chain and simple findings | An offline run can clear Warin and substantiate Corin through independent evidence |
| **M2 — walk and demonstrate** | Doors, consent, notebook, stand-in reconstruction and public scene | Players discover the gap and can explain possibility versus guilt |
| **M3 — alternate approaches** | Early packet recovery, private admission, late reopening and bounded retrieval | Solutions survive changed order, absence and partial observation |
| **M4 — persist and polish** | Full checkpoint, input parity, final presentation and return-state behaviour | Quit/reload at every important transition preserves a coherent case |

## Deterministic acceptance

Check all legal paths against timing, including sprinting and changed doors; the private incident with actual NPC motion; intact-before/broken-after catch timing; custody conservation; facts limited to actual recipients; no false independence from repeated hearsay; interrupted experiments; early packet recovery preventing later pickup; admission limited to corroborated acts; and findings computed without reading culprit identity.

Enumerate evidence subsets around each threshold. Removing one essential link must downgrade the corresponding finding. Adding a rumour or personal accusation must not restore it. The companion design validator checks the numeric proposal and these illustrative proof recipes; it does not certify navigation, NPC performance or the final game implementation.

## Human acceptance

With five to eight formative testers, look for three unaided discoveries: two sightings are not continuous observation; the upper connection changes the possible journey; opportunity is insufficient to identify an assailant. Ask them to explain their own conclusion before showing any author solution.

At least two approaches should lead to satisfying conclusions. Players who stop after clearing Warin should recognise that as a legitimate achievement. Voice, typing and action-list testers must reach equivalent evidence. A slow reader must not lose the whole mystery while reading a document.

If testers merely collect highlighted clues until the notebook names Corin, the design has failed. If the geometry needs a contrived detour to preserve its number, revise the site. If the case is solvable but dull, improve the demonstrations and character reactions before multiplying crimes. Validate visuals using CATHEDRAL_HEADLESS=1; actual enjoyment still requires human play.
