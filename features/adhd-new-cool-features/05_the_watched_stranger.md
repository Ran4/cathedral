# The watched stranger

Invert the lens: the player is the one being observed, priced, named,
misdescribed and planned around. Less control and less information as the
feature, not the bug.

### Named by the Town `[N8 V8 F9]`

You have no name until an NPC coins one, and gossip decides which epithet
sticks city-wide — the first memorable thing you do in front of a talker
becomes your handle: Bread-thief, the Wet Stranger, the One Who Paid. You
can't set your name; you can only behave your way into a new one, staging a
deed in front of the right witness before a worse name propagates — and the
old one lingers in some mouths. Identity done *to* you, riding existing
memory and speech for no extra turns.

### Your Silence Is Heard `[N9 V8 F8]` ★

The engine already knows when the microphone opens and when a reply is owed —
track the pattern, and let each ward settle its own theory about a stranger
who never talks: holy fool, spy, deaf pilgrim, penitent under a vow. NPCs
then change how they address you — writing in the dirt, speaking slowly and
loudly, testing you with an insult to see if you flinch. The absence of input
becomes a first-class expressive channel almost no voice-enabled game has
dared.

### The Confessional Erases `[N9 V8 F9]`

The cathedral priest already knows what the town knows about you: his prompt
is seeded from ward notices and the gossip layer, so he confronts you with
things you never told anyone — and completing a real in-world penance
genuinely deletes that entry everywhere. The cathedral becomes the single
place in the surveillance economy where information flows backward. You
finally get an interface to the ledger, but only on your knees, item by item.

### Eavesdropping Your Own Dossier `[N8 V8 F9]`

The only way to learn what the town thinks of you is to lurk under windows
and hear yourself described — often wrongly. Each ward trades one observation
about the stranger per day through the existing batched curfew prompt, and
the dossier accumulates errors ("he's the bishop's man," "she paid Odo in
foreign coin") that NPCs then act on. You can never read it directly — only
overhear fragments and decide whether correcting them is worth revealing you
were listening.

### The Stranger's Surcharge `[N7 V8 F8]`

Every vendor privately appraises you — mud on your clothes, drunkenness,
urgency, how long you've loitered, whether a sergeant is watching — and
quotes a price that is about *you*, not the bread. You never see the markup;
you only hear a local pay 2 for what cost you 3. Discovering how to dress,
walk and behave your way down to local prices becomes an unspoken minigame
the game never explains.

### Your Body Talks Without You `[N8 V8 F8]`

A tiny observer system classifies player kinematics into a handful of social
verbs — loitering, fleeing, shadowing, barging, kneeling — and feeds the
label into nearby actors' prompts the same way drunkenness already goes in.
Doors shut as you approach, mothers call children in, a sergeant changes his
beat, all without a scripted reaction anywhere. You learn that in this city
you are never not saying something.

### The Coat Trick `[N7 V7 F8]`

Ward notices attach to a description, not a soul — the "unknown people" rule
made literal — so swapping hood and coat launders your identity against gate
keepers who only know "a man in a green coat." A Major who spent an hour
talking with you sees straight through it, because their memory holds more
than a coat. Sequence-breaking your own criminal record becomes the stealth
endgame, with intimacy as the counter.

### The Curfew Applies to You `[N6 V8 F8]`

At the Snuffing the city closes for the player too: doors bar, honest NPCs go
in, and a player abroad without a lantern-token or a ward captain's night-pass
gets the existing tether-escort walked home or to the Stone House. Night
flips from "same city, darker" into a separate contraband space populated
only by people who also shouldn't be there. Mostly a gate on the player —
the seize/commit and bell systems are already built.

### The Map Is Your Memory `[N8 V5 F6]` ⚠ trap

The minimap becomes your character's hand-sketch: streets you haven't walked
are drawn confidently *wrong*, old coverage decays into smudge, and NPCs sell
corrections — some themselves wrong, because the seller has never crossed the
canal either. **Trap because:** it fights the already-shipped minimap and
click-to-teleport investment head-on, and fallible-map art is expensive; the
idea's soul could survive as a fog/confidence layer instead.
