A Typical Day in a Citizen's Life

Everyone in Ombreval carries a Round — the thing they do when nothing is wrong. It is pegged to the seven bells of the day, bends on market days and the holy day, and decides where a body walks, where it stands, where it works and where it sleeps.

This is the movement system's default — the bottom rung ("rung 9") of a deterministic priority ladder that runs in plain Rust, with no model involved. Getting thirsty, tired or frightened doesn't summon an LLM; it just bumps a citizen to a higher rung of that same ladder (walk to a well, go home, flee). The LLM is a separate, expensive layer that only spends a turn on the handful of NPCs near enough for you to see or hear — or the one you actually speak to. The Round is authored by hand for the twenty people you meet, and derived from a per-trade template for everyone else. Pick a bell, a day of the week, or a trade below and watch the city rearrange itself.

The clock

The seven bells

The day is cut into seven canonical offices, each announced by a great bell that rings its ordinal number of strokes at three-second intervals — so a player anywhere in the maze can count the hour. A citizen's schedule is not written in clock-time; it is written against these bells.

How to read a day

What "doing" means on the ground

Every leg of a Round says where and what. The "what" is one of seven verbs. None of them nail a citizen to a single tile: each drifts and mills within a leash around the leg's anchor (0 m for the bricked-in anchoress, 24 m for a patrolling watchman), so a crowd looks alive rather than frozen.

The eight day-shapes

The archetypes — one template per kind of life

Ninety percent of the city is one of eight templates. Each block below is an office; its colour is the verb, its width is how many real hours that office lasts. The selected bell is ringed, and the whole chart re-draws when you change the day of the week — watch the trades empty into the Wickmarket on Highmarket, or into the nave on Bellday.

Which day does your trade live?

All sixty-five occupations, each bound to one archetype and a set of candidate workplaces (the sim binds each person to the nearest one to their home). Click a row to jump to its day-shape. Type in the search box up in the bar to filter.

The twenty authored rounds

The people you actually meet

These rounds were transcribed by hand from the dramatis personae — the twenty characters the player will really talk to. Each strip is a full day (hover a block for the place); change the day of the week and the market-folk swing to their squares, the day-workers file into the Lanthorn on Bellday, and the night trades keep the streets after curfew.
